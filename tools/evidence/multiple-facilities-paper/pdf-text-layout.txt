 FACTOR-TWO SUBGAME-PERFECT EQUILIBRIA WITH
  ATOMIC CLIENTS AND A COMMON LOCATION SET

        Abstract. Krogmann, Lenzner, Skopalik, Uetz, and Vos introduced
        a two-stage facility-location game in which facilities choose locations
        and weighted atomic clients then choose accessible facilities to minimize
        congestion. Their existence guarantee for weighted clients depends on
        the number of facilities, while their lower bound is the golden ratio.
        We prove that a factor-two approximate subgame-perfect equilibrium
        exists whenever the facilities share a finite location set, independently of
        their number. Client equilibria are exact at every placement. On path,
        every covered client chooses an occupied location and independently
        randomizes uniformly among its facilities; all other continuations may
        be pure. We obtain the placement by lexicographically maximizing
        the facility-load vector. The resulting weight bounds, combined with
        an atomic packing argument, yield exact client equilibria after every
        deviation and limit every facility’s improvement factor to two.




                                  1. Introduction
   We study approximate subgame-perfect equilibria in a facility-location
game with weighted atomic clients. Facilities first choose locations, and the
clients then choose accessible facilities to minimize congestion. A facility move
changes both the clients it can serve and the client game. The equilibrium
existence question therefore requires a choice of client equilibrium at every
placement, with a bound on each facility’s gain from moving.
   Krogmann, Lenzner, Skopalik, Uetz, and Vos [1] study this problem for
clients with unsplittable weights who may randomize over accessible facilities.
They prove existence of exact subgame-perfect equilibria for unit-weight
clients. For unequal weights, their Theorem 5 gives examples excluding every
approximation factor below the golden ratio, while Observation 4 gives a
guarantee of k for k facilities. Their Section 6 asks whether the golden ratio
suffices in the weighted game. We consider the case in which all facilities
have the same available locations, and prove an existence guarantee of two
for every k.
   To prove the factor-two bound, we need a placement with controlled client
weight after each facility move and an exact client equilibrium respecting that
control. We first maximize the sorted facility-load vector over placements and
a finite family of mixed client profiles. We show that the selected profile is a
client equilibrium and derive bounds on the weight affected by every facility
move. We then construct a pure client equilibrium after each deviation. In
this construction, a client heavier than the required load bound stays alone

                      2. A factor-two equilibrium
   To obtain a complete continuation, we must bound the moving facility’s
load at an exact client equilibrium after every deviation. We first derive
sufficient weight bounds from a lexicographically maximal placement and
assignment. An atomic packing lemma then gives a pure client profile after
each move. We show that strict improving client moves preserve the required
load bound and terminate at an exact equilibrium.

location, independent uniform randomization gives equal expected facility
loads while keeping each client’s weight indivisible in every realization. We
maximize the resulting load vector over a finite family of profiles.

2.2. Packing clients and equilibrating after a move. We now distribute
the weight bounded in Lemma 2.3 over the stationary facilities. The next
lemma allows a client heavier than the desired cap to occupy a facility alone.
All remaining facilities satisfy the cap.

positive weights with total at most (h + 1)a can be partitioned into at most h
parts, each having weight at most 2a or consisting of a single client of weight
greater than 2a.

Lemma 2.5. Fix a placement, a facility f , and a number a > 0. Suppose
there is a feasible pure client assignment in which f has load at most 2a,
every client heavier than 2a is alone at a facility other than f , and every

Then this placement has a pure exact client equilibrium in which f has load

2.3. Completion of the continuation. It remains to specify one contin-
uation at all placements. Since distinct unilateral deviations from a fixed
labeled placement reach distinct placements, the client equilibria supplied by
Lemma 2.6 can be selected simultaneously.

Theorem 2.7. For every finite nonempty common location set, every finite
set of clients with positive real weights, and every integer k ≥ 2, there exists
a 2-approximate subgame-perfect equilibrium. On path every covered client
may choose one occupied location and independently randomize uniformly
among its facilities. Every off-path client equilibrium may be chosen pure.

Proof. If R = 0, all facility loads are zero at every placement and the assertion
is immediate. Otherwise choose (s, p) as in Construction 2.1. Lemma 2.2
gives an exact on-path client equilibrium, and Lemma 2.6 gives a pure
client equilibrium satisfying (2) with α = 2 at each actual deviation. These
placements are pairwise distinct: deviations by different facility labels change
different coordinates of s, and different targets for one facility change its
coordinate to different values. At every remaining placement, start with
any feasible pure assignment and perform strict improving client moves.
Equation (9) ensures termination at a pure client equilibrium. These choices
define the required complete continuation.                                      □

  The on-path construction gives equal expected loads to facilities at the
same location. After a deviation we use a different selection: an isolated
heavy client remains at a stationary facility, and the moving facility satisfies
the factor-two load bound. The continuation need not preserve symmetry
among facilities at a location.

  For rational input, we can record the selected equilibria with polynomially
many bits and use a fixed rule to complete all other placements. The next
proposition states this encoding consequence.
Proposition 2.8. Suppose the weights are positive binary rationals and
the client–location incidences and all k facilities are explicit. There is a
finite exact procedure that outputs the placement in Theorem 2.7, its on-path
client equilibrium, a pure client equilibrium for each of its k(|S| − 1) actual
deviations, and a deterministic default-rule program for all other placements.
The displayed strategies have encoding length polynomial in the input length.
The stored on-path and actual-deviation profiles, and the facility inequalities
for those deviations, can be checked in time polynomial in their explicit
encoding length; correctness of the default-rule program follows from the
potential argument.
Proof. Enumerate all placements and all feasible client site assignments in
the fixed order from Construction 2.1, and retain the first lexicographic
maximum. The on-path probabilities belong to {0} ∪ {1/q : 1 ≤ q ≤ k}.
At each actual deviation, perform the mergers of Lemma 2.4 and strict
improving client moves as in Lemma 2.5. These processes terminate and
their output probabilities are zero or one.
   For the default rule, fix an enumeration of the clients. Assign each covered
client to its least-index accessible facility and repeatedly choose the first
client in this enumeration with a strict improvement, moving it to its least-
index cost-minimizing accessible facility. Equation (9) proves that this rule
terminates at every placement. Store its fixed description together with the
displayed on-path and actual-deviation profiles. Each displayed pure profile
needs at most one facility index per client; the number of displayed profiles is
polynomial in the explicit input length. The rational loads are sums of input
weights, possibly divided by an integer at most k. A denominator for any
one load is obtained by multiplying the product of the input denominators
by an integer at most k. Its bit length is therefore at most the sum of the
input denominator bit lengths plus O(log k), so exact comparisons also have
polynomial bit length. Formula (1) checks each displayed client equilibrium,
and (2) checks the facility deviations.                                       □
  Proposition 2.8 distinguishes a short description of the continuation from
an efficient search procedure. Exhaustive selection and finite improvement
suffice for existence; neither step has a polynomial running-time bound in
this proof. Determining the best uniform approximation factor between
the golden ratio and two, and finding an efficient construction for general
common location sets, remain separate questions.
