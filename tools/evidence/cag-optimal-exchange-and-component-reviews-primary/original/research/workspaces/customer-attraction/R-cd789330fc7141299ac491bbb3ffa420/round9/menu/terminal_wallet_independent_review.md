## Statement and definitions

Fix an arbitrary finite common catalog, m>=2, and any complete history-dependent pure SPE. Let B_1,...,B_m be its actual leaf. Put A=B_(m-1), B=B_m, k_x=|{i<=m-2:x in B_i}|, a=u_(m-1), v=u_m, F_actual=sum_(i<=m-2)u_i, and

    L_A=sum_(x in A)1/(k_x+2),
    C_p=a-L_A=sum_(x in A outside B)1/[(k_x+1)(k_x+2)],
    C'=min(a,v)-L_A.

The actual last player can copy A, so v>=L_A. Directly a>=L_A. Thus 0<=C'<=C_p. The candidate is

    (2H_m+1)C'<=W,   H_m=sum_(r=1)^m1/r.          (TW)

## Independent proof

For every earlier actual role i<=m-2, let b_ix be the x-load in its actual prefix and r_i=m-i+1. Consider that role's genuine actual-prefix deviation to A followed by the original continuation. Whatever the successors do, x in A has final load at most b_ix+r_i. This one complete deviation and SPE give

    u_i>=q_i:=sum_(x in A)1/(b_ix+r_i).

There is no customer-reweighted IC here. Since b_ix<=k_x and k_x+r_i<=(k_x+1)r_i, summing these complete role inequalities yields

    F_actual>=Q:=sum_(i<=m-2)q_i
       >=(H_m-3/2)sum_(x in A)1/(k_x+1).         (ANCHOR)

For m=2, the sum is empty and H_2-3/2=0. For any k>=0,

    (H_m-3/2)/(k+1)+2/(k+2)
      >=(2H_m-1)/[(k+1)(k+2)],

because the excess after multiplication by (k+1)(k+2) is k(H_m+1/2)>=0. Therefore

    Q+2L_A>=(2H_m-1)C_p.

Finally W=F_actual+a+v>=Q+2min(a,v)=Q+2L_A+2C', giving

    W>=(2H_m-1)C_p+2C'>=(2H_m+1)C'.

This proves TW for arbitrary history-dependent ties, repeated topics, and empty topics. If A or all customer values are empty, all relevant terms are zero. For m=1 the terminal pair is undefined and TW is inapplicable.
