5    We study how local cost comparisons determine approximate stability in weighted congestion games with polynomial
6
     latencies. We develop tools for comparing endpoint costs with area estimates, combining resource potentials with
7
     a common scale for each player, and realizing stability on a structured set of states by legal congestion resources.
8
     These tools explain both the amplification caused by large atoms and the existence guarantees available under small
9
10
     atomic shares, compatible weight types, or bounded weight depth. As an application, we construct rational games of
11   degree 𝑑 with no (𝑑 − 1200⌈log2 𝑑⌉2 )-approximate pure Nash equilibrium, for every integer 𝑑 ≥ 220 . Together with
12   the known 𝑑-approximate existence theorem, this answers the asymptotic existence question raised by Christodoulou,
13   Gairing, Giannakopoulos, Poças, and Waldmann. We also give conditions under which the same realization method
14   preserves social optimization and approximation quality.

26   1    Introduction
27
     Weighted congestion games describe systems in which indivisible users choose resources and the cost of
28
29
     each resource depends on its total load. Approximate pure Nash equilibria provide a finite-state notion of
30   stability when exact equilibria may fail to exist. Their analysis often begins with a local comparison between
31   a player’s cost and a change in a resource potential. To obtain a statement about the whole game, however,
32
     these local comparisons must remain consistent across every resource that the player can enter or leave.
33
34
         We study three aspects of this passage from local costs to global stability. First, we compare the cost at a
35   polynomial’s endpoint with a discrete approximation to the area beneath it. The comparison distinguishes
36   the total mass in a window from the size of the individual atoms used to sample it. Second, we identify
37
     when local resource potentials admit the same multiplier for a player on all resources. For resources with
38
39
     two optional weight types, this compatibility is described by product inequalities around type cycles. Third,
40   we give a construction that confines every stable state to a prescribed set of structured binary states, while
41   retaining an explicit formula for the approximation tolerance introduced by the added resources.
42
         Each tool has a positive use. Small actual atomic shares make the integral potential effective. On forests
43                                                                                                             √
44
     of interacting weight types, the common-scale construction gives an approximation factor of order           𝑑,
45   independently of the number of types. For arbitrary resource populations, a common ordering by decreasing
46   weight gives an existence bound determined by the height of the player graph. The realization construction
47
     allows stable states to be selected on the smaller structured domain; with an additional externality bound,
48
49
     it also allows social optimization on that domain.
