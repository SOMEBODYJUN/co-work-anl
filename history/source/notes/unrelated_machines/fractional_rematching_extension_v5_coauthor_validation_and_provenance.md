## New checks and verdict

Verdict: no fatal obstruction found in the two new replacements or their connection to the previously checked framework. The v5 input is unweighted explicit finite XOS with arbitrary nonnegative binary-rational coefficients, without an original normalization or global-grid promise. C-D-E retain their additional normalized-menu domain.

1. Residual normalization. On active agents W_i=v_i(B)>0 is an exactly evaluable binary rational. Every residual coefficient divided by W_i has polynomial representation length and every resulting clause total is at most one; a maximizing whole-B clause has total exactly one. Hence b_i=1/a is feasible for the fixed lift and the maintained set gives x_i>=1/(4a). The normalizer never depends on the numerical size of the smallest positive coefficient.

2. Rational iteration. With ell=1/(4a) and h=ell^2/2, the same LP stopping rule gives the same c=2. At a non-stopping step the log-potential increase is at least a*ell^2/8=1/(128a); the initial deficit is a ln(a)<=a^2. Thus 128a^3 updates suffice. This log quantity is only an analysis device. Basic optimal vertices of the same fixed lambda/z polytope have uniform bit bounds independent of the current objective, so the maintained lift's bit length grows additively over this polynomial number of updates.

3. Actual targets. Nested-threshold extraction uses actual normalized XOS values, giving Vhat_i>=x_i. Multiplying by W_i yields V_i in original units. Every feasible fractional original residual utility family maps to the same normalized lift, so the stopping inequality gives sum z_iT*v_i(T)/V_i<=2a for ALL feasible fractional families. This does not merely bound the recovered family or integral allocations.

4. Fixed candidates. The welfare rounding and candidate-round construction still use original exact rational utilities. Their distributions, clauses, fixed bundles S_i, round indices, and subsequent local weights are all determined before labels. The original-unit inequalities delta*V_i/6 <= v_i(S_i) <= V_i and w_ij<=nu_i remain available. SetSplitting's k<=|T|/2 bound is unchanged and prevents a scale-dependent expansion.

6. Inverse moment and deterministic mask.
