## 1. Common integer allocation lemma

Author section 1 correctly turns equal fractional group mass H into a common integral quota floor(H). For any group set A, summing its fractional mass and resource capacities gives H|A|<=|N(A)|. Cloning q=floor(H) identical-neighborhood slots per group then satisfies Hall for every slot subset: if it meets the group set A, its neighbor set is precisely N(A), and its size is at most q|A|. The q=0 case is handled explicitly. An integral-capacity flow is an equivalent construction.

The main application has q<n=2^t, so slot count at most n^2 and explicit slot edges at most n^3 are polynomial in the complete stated input. This is not an unjustified numerical-value expansion in a succinct t-only input.

## 2. Arbitrary nonlinear maps, product union, and exact floor

Author section 2 uses the same complement pairing reconstructed independently. Any selected supports of x and x+1 intersect at exactly one resource, for any nonlinear F. The author's multiplicity proof charges each such pair intersection to the actual duplicate-incidence loss. Since the pairing is a matching, its resource charge is at most floor(m(u)/2)<=m(u)-1 for a used resource. Thus the block union loses at least 2^{b-1} from its total 4^b incidences.

This agrees with the simpler pair-union summation in the independent record; both bound all nonlinear functions, including constant and repeated-output maps. The author correctly factors the full union by blocks only because each F_j is globally shared and depends on its own input block. It imposes no product or functional shape on the allocated partial sets B_g. The integer minimum count, not only the fractional union average, produces q=floor(H).

## 3. Attainment and one simultaneous allocation

Author section 3 takes a cyclic coordinate map inside every block. Its kernel equations show that a non-all-one a in a block has one owner per reachable resource; the all-one a has a two-element kernel and two owners per reachable resource. These statements include a singleton block and coincide with the independently reconstructed rank argument.

For a global resource, the number of owners is 2^{z(a)}. Equal sharing among owners gives capacity exactly one per used resource and the same fractional mass H to every group, by the displayed block product. Its total mass also proves that the selected union reaches nH. Applying the single common Hall/flow lemma gives q resources to every group simultaneously, without a Cartesian requirement on the final B_g.

The q/n identity, exact one-block value, singleton-block floor, strict rounding error below 2^{-t}, and the bounded-block exponential estimate in section 4 are all correct. In particular k>=ceil(t/L) is used with a base strictly between zero and one in the proper inequality direction.

## 4. Fixed chain and its precise counterexample meaning

Author section 5 correctly identifies the lower-bidiagonal matrix. Its nonzero (t-1)-minor and diagonal determinant give rank t for a=1 and rank t-1 otherwise. The separate t=1 check closes the degenerate minor. The union is therefore n(n+1)/2 and its average common count is (n+1)/2, whose integer floor is n/2 because t>=1 makes n even.

The author's lower bound shares each resource equally among its one or two owners, giving each group fractional mass (n+1)/2 and hence integer n/2. The independent record instead used one-half on every selected incidence, already giving a feasible fractional n/2 row. These are two valid witnesses for the same joint integer quota, not a discrepancy in the value.

The chain has exactly the stated acyclic one-other-coordinate dependencies. The author does not claim the optimum over all acyclic maps is one-half, nor that this chain is block-fiber admissible for every partition. This distinction is necessary: a chain dependency crossing blocks violates the stronger block-fiber rule.

## 5. Complete ordinary task accounting and explicit input size

Author section 6 actually schedules every original job. Only selected-state incidences indexed by B_g are exported; all others, including all unselected states, remain on their original private machines. Resource loads are at most one, unselected private loads are one, and selected private loads are 2-|B_g|/n. No logical constraint was inserted into the machine eligibility matrix.

The ordinary-OPT argument is valid independently of the restricted logical optimization. A global cycle allows the n-1 nonexceptional a-values to give each group unique resources, yielding actual load 1+1/n. Conversely complementary groups' actually selected supports intersect at one resource. At a schedule below two, their two distinct unit-resource incidence jobs cannot both use that resource, so one stays with its selector and gives load at least 1+1/n. Schedules at least two satisfy that lower bound trivially. Thus ordinary P_t remains near one; 2-Q under imposed block-fiber choices is not its ordinary optimum.

Section 7's object counts and binary bounds are correct: n groups, n^2 state-private machines, n^2 resource machines, n^3 incidence jobs and n selectors, with O(t)-bit private time 1/n. The selected support graph has n^2 edges. H and q are computed exactly with O(t+k)-bit integers, and q<=n bounds the optional slot graph. The proof does not enumerate nonlinear functions, all assignments, or a number of objects proportional to a processing-time denominator beyond what the explicit P_t already contains. No software or finite-enumeration evidence is needed for these general statements.
