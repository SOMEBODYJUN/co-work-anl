# Finite kernel modulo vertical divisors and lifting an effective movable domain

## Statement

Let \(f:(X,\Delta)\to S\) be a klt Calabi–Yau fibration over \(\mathbb C\) with normal projective \(\mathbb Q\)-factorial total space and normal quasi-projective base. Assume existence of good minimal models for effective klt pairs in dimension \(\dim X-\dim S\). Write \(V=N^1(X/S)_\mathbb R\), \(T\) for the real span of vertical prime divisor classes, \(p:V\to V/T\), and \(C=\overline{\operatorname{Mov}}(X/S)\cap\operatorname{Eff}(X/S)\). Let \(\Gamma\) be the numerical image of the relative pseudoautomorphisms preserving the boundary or its support. The following three assertions hold.

1. \(T\) is rational, both signs of every element of \(T\) are relatively effective, \(\Gamma|T\) is finite, and \(\ker(\Gamma\to\operatorname{GL}(V/T))\) is finite.
2. For any rational relatively ample class \(a\), the stabilizer of \(p(a)\) in \(\Gamma\) is finite.
3. Suppose the induced image in \(\operatorname{GL}(V/T)\) has a full-dimensional rational polyhedral weak domain \(\bar P\) on the actual cone \(p(C)\), with finite setwise stabilizer in that image. Then \(C\) has a strong rational polyhedral domain for the whole of \(\Gamma\) in Li–Zhao's Definition 3.4, including separation from a whole closed translate.

This result uses the relative numerical equivalence testing all contracted curves. It does not require pointedness of the closed movable cone or \(\mathbb Q\)-factoriality of the base.

## Vertical classes, the full group and a stable lattice

For a vertical prime divisor \(Z\), \(f(Z)\) is proper closed in \(S\). Choose an effective Cartier divisor \(A\) on \(S\) containing it, by a sufficiently positive hypersurface in a projective embedding. Indeed \(S\) is projective because it is a quasi-projective proper image of \(X\). Then \(kf^*A-Z\) is effective for \(k\) large, proving effectivity of \(-[Z]\). The positive sign is effective by construction and \(\mathbb Q\)-factoriality. Each translate of \(Z\) over \(S\) is a prime divisor in \(f^{-1}(f(Z))\), hence an irreducible component of this proper closed subset of \(X\). There are finitely many. Taking finitely many such \(Z\) whose numerical classes span \(T\) proves rationality and the finiteness of the linear action there.

There is a full \(\Gamma\)-stable lattice. Take the numerical images of all integral Weil divisors. On a smooth projective resolution \(u:Y\to X\), if \(D\) is an absolutely numerically trivial integral divisor on \(Y\), then \(u^*u_*D-D\) is exceptional and numerically trivial over \(u\). The negativity lemma makes it zero. Every curve in \(X\) is dominated by a curve in \(Y\), so \(u_*D\) is numerically trivial over \(S\). Pushforward from the finitely generated Néron–Severi group of \(Y\) consequently has image all these Weil numerical classes. It is a finitely generated subgroup of the rational space with full rational span and hence a lattice. Strict transformation of Weil divisors preserves it.

If the group is defined by preserving the support of \(\Delta\), the subgroup which fixes its finitely many prime components individually has finite index. Denote its numerical image by \(\Gamma_f\). Elements of \(\Gamma_f\) have representatives preserving all coefficients. Finite-index arguments below are inside the full specified numerical group.

## Same-support rational working pair

If the given klt boundary \(\Delta\) is real, fix its finitely many prime components. The condition \(K_X+\sum d_iD_i\equiv_S0\) defines a rational affine subspace of their coefficients, since \(X\) is \(\mathbb Q\)-factorial and the numerical space is rational. The klt condition and positivity on the fixed support are open near the given coefficients. Choose a rational point there to obtain a klt boundary \(\Delta_0\) with the same support. The relative numerical-triviality theorem of Hacon–Xu, Corollary 1.4 in the published paper (Corollary 1.6 in arXiv:1107.4149v2), gives \(K_X+\Delta_0\sim_{\mathbb Q,S}0\). The objects \(C,V,T,p\) depend only on \(X/S\). If the initial group preserves support, it is exactly the same for \(\Delta_0\). If it preserves coefficients, retain that specified group throughout. In either convention the finite-index subgroup induced by component-fixing maps preserves all the working coefficients.

Use \(\Delta_0\) in every subsequent good-model and geography argument. For a positive integer \(r\) write \(r(K_X+\Delta_0)=\operatorname{div}\varphi+f^*B_S\), with \(B_S\) Cartier. For any effective real \(D\), any real \(\epsilon\ge0\) giving a klt perturbation, and \(m=r\ell>0\), one has
\[
 \lfloor m(K_X+\Delta_0+\epsilon D)\rfloor
 =\ell\operatorname{div}\varphi+\ell f^*B_S+\lfloor m\epsilon D\rfloor .
\]
The last divisor is effective. Trivializing \(B_S\) over a base open set gives a nonzero local section of the rounded sheaf, so its direct image is nonzero generically and the ordinary relative Kodaira dimension is nonnegative. This supplies the hypothesis of Z. Li, *On finiteness of log canonical models*, Corollary 1.2, in the real extension of the good-model criterion of Li–Zhao, Theorem 2.4. The same restriction to a very general closed fibre has ordinary Kodaira dimension at least zero, so the standing good-model assumption in its dimension applies. In dimension zero the conclusion is immediate.

## A single ample class detects vertical shears

Assume positive relative dimension. Choose a positive integral multiple of the given rational relatively ample class and an effective relatively ample Cartier representative \(A\) of this multiple, of class \(a\); twisting by a sufficiently positive base line bundle preserves the relative class. Let \(g\) stabilize \(p(a)\). Replace it by a positive power that fixes \(T\) pointwise and lies in \(\Gamma_f\). Represent that power, still called \(g\), by a boundary-preserving pseudoautomorphism \(\phi\). Set \(l=ga-a\in T\). Then \(g^na=a+nl\) for every integer \(n\). If \(l\ne0\), the rational cone \(P=\mathbb R_{\ge0}a+\mathbb R_{\ge0}l\) is pointed because \(a\) has nonzero generic numerical class. It lies in the actual effective cone by the preceding paragraph. Li–Zhao, Theorem 2.7, covers \(P\) by finitely many relatively open rational polyhedral cells on which effective small klt perturbations share their marked weak log canonical models over \(S\).

Let \(B_n=(\phi^n)_*A\) for \(n\ge0\). Infinitely many classes \([B_n]\) are in one such cell. Fix in it \(n_0\) and an unbounded subsequence \(n_j>n_0\). The marking \(\phi^{-n}:X\dashrightarrow X\) is a weak log canonical model for the effective perturbation \(\Delta_0+\epsilon B_n\), with sufficiently small \(\epsilon>0\). Indeed on a common resolution \(u,v\), from source and target respectively, \(u^*(K_X+\Delta_0)-v^*(K_X+\Delta_0)\) is exceptional and numerically trivial over \(u\). It vanishes by negativity, using compatible canonical representatives for the birational map. The difference \(u^*B_n-v^*A\) is exceptional over both projections, and anti-nef over \(u\) because \(A\) is relatively ample and the map is over \(S\). Hence this difference is effective by negativity. The target log divisor is relatively nef. These give the discrepancy and nefness conditions of a marked weak log canonical model. Smallness of \(\epsilon\) can be arranged separately for each comparison pair.

Apply Theorem 2.7 with the markings for \(n_0\) and \(n_j\) in turn. Their target log divisors for the other member of the pair must be nef. Since \(K_X+\Delta_0\equiv_S0\), this gives
\[
 a+(n_j-n_0)l,\qquad a-(n_j-n_0)l\ \in\operatorname{Nef}(X/S).
\]
Division by \(n_j-n_0\) and passage to the limit place both \(l\) and \(-l\) in the closed relative nef cone. This cone is pointed by the definition of relative numerical equivalence. Hence \(l=0\), contradicting the assumed shear. We have proved that a positive power of the initial element fixes \(a\).

A pseudoautomorphism taking a relative ample numerical class to another ample class is an automorphism. One proof reconstructs each marked model as the relative Proj of the section algebra of a sufficiently divisible relatively ample divisor: smallness identifies the section algebras of strict transforms on every open of \(S\). The numerical stabilizer of \(a\) in the group of genuine relative automorphisms is finite. Indeed the set \(\{x:a\pm x\in\operatorname{Nef}(X/S)\}\) is a compact neighborhood of zero. Interior of \(a\) gives the neighborhood assertion, and an unbounded sequence, upon normalization, would put a nonzero vector and its negative in the nef cone. An integral linear map preserving this bounded neighborhood and \(a\) has only finitely many possible images of a fixed lattice basis. Thus every element stabilizing \(p(a)\) has finite order in the original numerical group. The level-three congruence kernel in \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb Z)\) is torsion-free. To see this, a nonidentity element of finite order in the kernel would yield one of prime order \(\ell\), of the form \(I+3^rB\) with \(r\ge1\) and \(B\not\equiv0\bmod3\). For \(\ell\ne3\) its \(\ell\)th power differs from the identity modulo \(3^{r+1}\). For \(\ell=3\), its cube is \(I+3^{r+1}B\bmod3^{r+2}\), again a contradiction. Thus the entire stabilizer of \(p(a)\) injects into the finite group \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb Z/3)\). This also proves part 1, as the kernel on \(V/T\) is a subgroup of this stabilizer. In relative dimension zero the generic fibre is a point, so the relative birational automorphism group is trivial and both claims follow separately.

## Polyhedral intersection and the exact domain

For any rational polyhedral \(R\subset\operatorname{Eff}(X/S)\), the intersection \(R\cap C\) is rational polyhedral. Here is an argument on the actual cone. If \(R\) contains lines, subdivide it into finitely many pointed rational cones first, for example by the coordinate orthants in a rational basis. On each piece the finite cover of Li–Zhao, Theorem 2.7 applies. For a relatively open cell meeting \(C\), choose an effective representative \(B\) of a point of that intersection. Good minimal models in the relative dimension and Li–Zhao, Lemma 2.3, give a marked small weak log canonical model for \(\Delta_0+\epsilon B\); equivalently see the displayed union in their proof of Lemma 5.1(2). Its original log divisor \(K+\Delta_0\) stays crepant, since strict transform identifies the relatively Q-principal relation and the discrepancy difference is exceptional and both relatively numerically trivial. The same small model thus works for arbitrarily small choices of the perturbation coefficient. Theorem 2.7 puts every other effective class in the cell on this marked model as a nef class, choosing a common small coefficient for each pair. A nef class there is a limit of relatively ample classes. By strict transform the original class is in the closed movable cone. The entire closure of the cell lies in the closed movable cone and in \(R\subset\operatorname{Eff}\). Hence \(R\cap C\) is the union of finitely many closed rational polyhedral cones; it is convex, so it equals the rational polyhedral cone generated by their rays.

Now let \(\bar P\) satisfy part 3. Its full inverse image \(R=p^{-1}\bar P\) is rational polyhedral. Since \(\bar P\subset p(C)\), every point of this inverse image is an effective lift plus an element of \(T\), hence \(R\) is contained in the actual effective cone. Let \(P=C\cap R\); the intersection result proves its rational polyhedrality. We have \(p(P)=\bar P\) and \(\Gamma P=C\), so \(P\) has ambient interior by countability and the relatively ample open cone. The quotient \(p\) is open, so \(p(\operatorname{Int}P)\subset\operatorname{Int}\bar P\). Thus any translate meeting \(\operatorname{Int}P\) must project to a stabilizer of \(\bar P\). Every element in that inverse-image subgroup preserves \(P=C\cap p^{-1}\bar P\), and this is exactly the setwise stabilizer. It is finite by part 1 and the finite stabilizer of \(\bar P\). Intersect \(P\) with the finitely many halfspaces which make a generic rational covector smallest in its orbit under this finite stabilizer. The finite group moves every point of \(P\) into the intersection. At interior points all comparisons with a nonidentity member are strict; comparing with the inverse excludes the whole closed translate. Elements outside the stabilizer already miss the original interior. The new full-dimensional rational polyhedral cone is a strong fundamental domain on \(C\).
