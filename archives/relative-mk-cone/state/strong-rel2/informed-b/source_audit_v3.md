# Source and revision audit for RMKC-STRONG-REL2, candidate 3

This records the dependence of the proposed proof on primary texts and the changes made after independent review of two full-proof revisions. The proof and the companion operator theorem are research candidates. Reading an original theorem and its proof verifies the stated application. It does not supply a new proof of all the foundational theorems on which the original authors rely.

## Ordinary Kodaira dimension in the real-boundary step

The second review identified an incorrect implication in the explanation of the real version of the relative good-model theorem in V2, SHA-256 b3bb65993b458e2d75cafd577b7552df267cb67935f5c1a1091fd96d9e875fd1. Z. Li, *On finiteness of log canonical models*, Cor. 1.2, assumes ordinary relative Kodaira dimension at least zero. Definition 3.1 defines it using \(f_*\mathcal O_X(\lfloor mD\rfloor)\). Remark 3.2 explicitly exhibits failure of invariance under real linear equivalence. A real principal equivalence to an effective divisor need not give such a section. The second full-proof review report has SHA-256 196add16c4de7a34cff5f6c3567e6fe2bbfbb47c29287e0ad5dcd6e6f263a382. The review supplied the product of two projective lines fibred over the second, with four disjoint constant sections having coefficients \(a,1-a,1/2,1/2\) for an irrational \(0<a<1\). The generic log adjoint is real-linearly trivial, but the degree of its \(m\)th round-down is \(-1\) for even \(m\) and \(-2\) for odd \(m\), since \(\lfloor ma\rfloor+\lfloor m(1-a)\rfloor=m-1\). Its ordinary relative Kodaira dimension is \(-\infty\). This diagnoses the invalid intermediate implication even for zero perturbation; the example has a strong relative numerical domain.

In V3 Section 1 first fixes a same-support *rational terminal* boundary \(\Delta_0\) with \(K_X+\Delta_0\sim_{\mathbb Q,S}0\), furnished by the rational numerical slice and the rational Hacon–Xu theorem already proved applicable. The original cone \(C\), space \(V\), and entire support-preserving group \(\Gamma\) are identical for the two boundaries. All later geography, good-model and group constructions use this working boundary. For each actual effective real perturbation \(\epsilon D\), clearing the one fixed denominator of the working adjoint gives the identity of rounded Weil divisors in Section 1. A local trivialization of the base Cartier divisor gives nonzero sections of the rounded sheaves at every positive multiple of that denominator, including \(D=0\) and vertical \(D\). This proves ordinary relative nonnegativity in the precise definition required by Cor. 1.2. This correction concerns the relative good-model argument. The distinct generic base-covering change is recorded below under “Complex surface theorem and marked descent.” The main statement still includes all the original real boundaries. [Z. Li, Cor. 1.2 and §3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.01370).

## Relative geometry and its exact hypotheses

**Li–Zhao.** The complete arXiv v8 PDF was available in the repository. I read the definitions and relevant proofs in §§2–5, including the corrected Lem. 2.3, the explanation after Thm. 2.4, the finite-geography argument Thms. 2.5 and 2.7, Prop. 3.3, Def. 3.4, Lem. 3.5, the generic restriction arguments of §4 and Lems. 5.1–5.2, and the special hypotheses in §6. Their base is normal quasi-projective over C. The relative numerical equivalence concerns divisors against contracted curves. Relative effectivity allows relative real linear equivalence to an effective Weil divisor. The pair's connected fibres, Q-factoriality and effective klt boundary are used in the proof. The codimension-one isomorphism in corrected Lem. 2.3 comes by extracting the missing crepant divisors and ruling out positive fixed components by the divisorial sigma decomposition. Its morphism to the selected good model pulls back the nef log adjoint and preserves semiampleness. Thm. 2.7 transfers *marked weak log canonical models* across effective classes of a cell, with the same small klt perturbation. The published proof need only be applied to pointed input cones as explained above. No assumption concerning the rank of the vertical space, the rationality of a chosen base Weil divisor, or the total dimension is involved. Their strong result in §6 assumes additional conditions, which are not invoked here. [Original PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.13701v8).

**Hacon–Xu and the real good-model criterion.** The full arXiv texts were opened, including the actual statements and proofs of *On finiteness of B-representations and semi-log canonical abundance*, Cor. 1.6 and its proof in §4, and *Existence of log canonical closures*, Thm. 2.12 and its proof on p. 12. The first result applies to a projective morphism of a semi-log canonical rational pair with relatively numerically trivial adjoint. Its output is relative Q-linear triviality. The second result takes a dlt rational pair, good models of the very general closed fibres, and finite generation of the relative canonical ring. It uses the relative Proj and eliminates the remaining horizontal and insufficient-fibre-type vertical divisors in the relative MMP. For real perturbations the proof here cites Li–Zhao Thm. 2.4 as their explicit klt real-coefficient extension, not the narrower rational Hacon–Xu statement on its own. Li–Zhao replace Proj by the relative canonical model for an effective klt real pair. Li's original *On finiteness of log canonical models*, Cor. 1.2, including its proof on p. 22 in the linked arXiv version, provides that model by variation among rational boundaries and the previously proved finiteness. In the actual V3 application the same-support rational working adjoint is relatively Q-linearly trivial. The round-down identity in Section 1 gives ordinary relative Kodaira dimension at least zero for every effective real perturbation. Separately its restriction to a very general closed fibre is pseudo-effective and has a good model by the low-dimensional results. These verifications supply respectively Li's relative canonical-model hypothesis and the fibre hypothesis in Li–Zhao's real extension. [Numerical triviality](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1107.4149v2). [Good models](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1105.1169v3). [Real log canonical model](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.01370).

**Surface abundance and smooth restriction.** Fujino's original *Minimal model theory for log surfaces* was opened in full. Thm. 1.1, the rational abundance statements in §§6–7 and the proof of Thm. 8.1 in §8 were checked. A projective complex lc or Q-factorial log surface with pseudoeffective adjoint has a good minimal model, and a nef log adjoint is semiample. Section 8 obtains the real-coefficient conclusion from a rational polytope of nef boundaries on fixed support. For an actual rational effective nef generic divisor L use the rational klt pair (F,(Δ₀)_F+εL) after algebraic closure and a sufficiently small rational ε. Its log adjoint is rationally linearly equivalent to εL. Flat base change of sections and faithful flatness then descend the base-point-free multiple to the original function field. The absolute curve case follows from positive degree and the fact that an effective degree-zero divisor is zero. In the geometric generic smoothness argument, Clemens–Kollár–Mori, *Higher dimensional complex geometry*, Cor. 6.13 was checked in the authors' monograph. They deduce smoothness in codimension two for terminal varieties from general sections and surface singularities. Generic smoothness in characteristic zero and the dimension inequality then dispose of the remaining smooth locus. Li–Zhao Prop. 4.1 proves geometric connectedness and klt by flat base change and a log resolution. [Surface abundance](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1001.3902v3). [Terminal singularities](https://www.numdam.org/issue/AST_1988__166__1_0.pdf). [Generic fibres](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.13701v8).

**Li II.** The complete original arXiv v1 was read in the repository, with direct checks of Lemmas 2.1, 2.5, 2.6, the group scheme constructions in §4, the proof of Thm. 1.3 and the proof of Thm. 1.8. In Step 1 of Thm. 1.3, an effective movable generic divisor has a horizontal closure with no vertical components. Generic sections avoiding horizontal fixed primes are cleared of vertical corrections by effective vertical representatives of negative vertical components. This argument depends on no lifting of automorphisms and is reproduced in Section 2. Separately, Thm. 1.8 has a weak result on the rational hull of the closed movable cone. Its proof calls Thm. 1.3; the proof of the latter starts by asserting equality of the full support-preserving generic and relative groups. The first nonauthor review gives a terminal-pair counterexample to that equality, so there is no dependence on that proof chain in the present revision. The arXiv record has a single v1. The journal index lists DOI 10.1090/jag/864 (published electronically 24 August 2026). The journal proof itself was not retrieved; no result is attributed to a conjectured revision in that edition. [Inspected original](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.04673v1). [Journal record](https://doi.org/10.1090/jag/864).

## Generic groups, cones and polarizations

**Complex surface theorem and marked descent.** Totaro’s *The cone conjecture for Calabi–Yau pairs in dimension two*, §2, defines the automorphism group by preservation of the boundary divisor. Its Theorem 4.1 gives a rational polyhedral domain in the geometric effective nef cone for complex projective klt surfaces. In V3, the algebraic closure of \(k=\mathbb C(S)\) is abstractly identified with \(\mathbb C\), and the smooth geometric surface with its rational working boundary meets the theorem's hypotheses. Section 2 then proves the descent to \(k\), in full. The finite Galois-stable subset of the Albanese torsor gives a subgroup of actual geometric automorphisms with the same numerical image and finite numerical kernel. Its finite generation follows from Looijenga, Corollary 4.15, after applying Proposition–Definition 4.1. Theorem 3.8 and the calculation in the proof of Proposition 4.6 give a finite set of actual semidirect-product elements that move the closed rational cone to meet itself in the ample interior. Only finitely many labelled conjugacy tuples occur; equality of those tuples gives genuine Galois-fixed automorphisms. The trace of an effective rational divisor on a finite field extension supplies effective representatives of invariant generators over \(k\). The group comparison with the relative action is the independent finite-index lifting in the same section. The marked descent was contributed by \(\texttt{/root/final_geometry_audit}\); the [freestanding argument](marked_generic_descent_v2.md) has SHA-256 895f2b4a29b70a3c87cd1b190fd0a883f2fc1f4e5c275c77bc6fa22b6cf41a81. [Totaro](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~totaro/papers/public_html/cone.pdf). [Looijenga](https://webspace.science.uu.nl/~looij101/conepaper.pdf). [Jinsong Xu](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1611.01804).

**Nonclosed-field comparisons.** Moraga–Stark, arXiv:2409.13068v1, states the generic surface cone theorem in Theorem 3.8. Its Iitaka-dimension-one proof uses invariants of a geometric exceptional curve in a calculation with an integral ground-field class. A ground-field exceptional curve can split into \(e\) disjoint conjugate geometric curves, in which case both its self-intersection and canonical degree are \(-e\). A separate audit exhibits this issue on an anticanonical pencil with a degree-two closed base point. It challenges that intermediate argument without contradicting the stated generic or relative cone conclusions. Fulin Xu, arXiv:2405.20899v3, Theorem 1.9, gives another route to a generic covering over a characteristic-zero field via the anticanonical fibration. Neither route is needed in V3. The new descent uses Totaro over the algebraic closure and the actual automorphism and Néron–Severi group over \(k\). In particular it uses neither a higher-dimensional Picard-space fundamental domain nor the relative cone conclusion sought here. [Moraga–Stark](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.13068). [Fulin Xu](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.20899).

**Generic log pluricanonical scalars.** Fujino–Gongyo's complete final text was opened. Defs. 2.11, 2.14 describe the B-pluricanonical action; Thm. 1.1 gives finite image for a projective lc pair with semiample log adjoint and Cartier multiple. The more specific Cor. 3.10 and its proof give finite image for a projective klt pair with Cartier multiple. On a log resolution it follows from Thm. 3.9, whose proof uses diagonalizability and bounded exponent. The generic pair used here has a *trivial* rth log pluricanonical bundle and an effective rational boundary. Every member of the finite-index component-fixing subgroup is a regular automorphism of this pair, so the result applies base-changed to an algebraic closure. Every scalar in the resulting finite subgroup of k* is already a complex constant, even though an abstract field isomorphism of the algebraic closure with C need not fix the embedded constants. The deduction of discrepancy invariance and of preservation of the vertical support is supplied in the candidate itself. [Authors’ final text](https://www.math.kyoto-u.ac.jp/~fujino/fg-comp-final.pdf).

**Jinsong Xu.** The complete arXiv v2 was opened. In Thm. 4.5 and its proof, the neutral group preserving the reduced support of a projective complex klt pair with Q-linearly trivial log adjoint is an abelian variety of dimension h¹(O). Lem. 3.3 excludes additive and multiplicative linear groups, Prop. 4.3 computes the dimension through logarithmic vector fields, and Thm. 3.1 gives the homogeneous quotient with finite kernel. The proof of Thm. 4.5 identifies the resulting fibration with the Albanese by connected fibres and an intervening isogeny. Section 1 chooses Δ₀ rational on the same support. Neutral groups depend only on that support, so no approximation to the automorphism group is presumed. The natural map from the group to the translations of the Albanese torsor is defined over k and becomes an isogeny after base change, including when F lacks a k-point. The isogeny into Pic⁰ associated to a *generic ample class* is also given by Li II Lem. 4.4 plus dimension equality. Sections 4 and 5 give direct calculations using respectively the Albanese orbit and the usual polarization of an abelian torsor, so no general semiample-divisor surjectivity theorem is required. [Xu original](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1611.01804v2). [Li II original](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.04673v1).

**Marked models and pointed convex theory.** Kawamata's original Lem. 1.5 and its proof reconstruct the model from the relative Proj of an ample marked divisor. The application's relatively ample cones are those of small markings. Looijenga's original Prop.–Def. 4.1 and Application 4.14 were opened in the author's published text. A lattice-preserving group covering an open nondegenerate cone by translates of a rational polyhedral cone covers the rational convex hull of the closure. The construction uses a rational interior covector of the dual and the cone of its minimizing orbit inequalities. Li–Zhao Lem. 3.5 chooses a covector with trivial stabilizer using proper discontinuity on the open dual. In the candidate the only base of this application is the pointed generic nef cone; the possibly degenerate total cone is handled by the independent operator theorem and finite-kernel refinement. [Kawamata](https://arxiv.org/pdf/alg-geom/9701006). [Looijenga](https://webspace.science.uu.nl/~looij101/conepaper.pdf).

**Abelian varieties.** Milne's complete *Abelian varieties* was opened, including §§12 and 17, Prop. 17.2 and the proof of Thm. 17.3 by traces and intersection products. Poincaré complete reducibility gives semisimplicity; the polarization identifies divisor classes with Rosati-symmetric endomorphisms rationally; the trace pairing is positive definite. The rational homology spectrum argument, the invariance of the actual quotient of k-points under the endomorphism algebra, and the effective boundary fibre on a torsor are separate proofs in Section 5. Those conclusions are not being attributed to the general reference. [Author's text](https://www.jmilne.org/math/xnotes/AVs.pdf).
