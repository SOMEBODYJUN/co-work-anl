A Linear Nonexistence Bound for Approximate Pure Nash Equilibria
                 in Weighted Congestion Games


                                              Research draft


                                                  Abstract
         We study the approximation factor required for pure Nash equilibria in finite weighted atomic
     congestion games with nonnegative polynomial costs. For every integer d ≥ 220 , we construct a
     game with positive rational weights and positive rational monomial costs of degree exactly d in
     which every pure state admits a strict d/10 improvement. Each player has only two nonempty,
     disjoint strategies. Together with the known d-approximate existence guarantee, this determines
     the order of the universal existence threshold as Θ(d) and rules out every universal sublinear
     guarantee. The construction first provides an improving exit from every product of ordered class
     fronts. Short adjacent windows constrain the relative phases of any putatively stable front; a
     full return window then provides an exit in either parity. Anchored triples within each weight
     class repair all remaining states. The front margin and global cost bound allow these additional
     resources to retain the original degree.


1    Introduction
Approximate pure Nash equilibria relax stability by allowing a bounded multiplicative incentive to
deviate. In weighted congestion games with polynomial costs, the natural existence question is how
this factor depends on the maximum degree, with no bound on the number of players or the range
of their weights. Caragiannis and Fanelli [1] establish a d-approximate potential and hence the
existence of a d-approximate pure Nash√ equilibrium. Christodoulou et al. [2] construct nonexistence
families with a factor growing as Ω( d/ ln d) and explicitly identify the gap to the linear existence
bound as a question for further investigation.
    We prove a linear nonexistence bound. The result concerns the universal guarantee over all
finite instances, so the size of a witnessing game may grow with the degree. A nonexistence result
must cover every state of that game. An improving cycle supplies only a sequence of unstable states
and leaves open the possibility of an equilibrium elsewhere. The construction below is designed
around this all-state requirement.

Main result. A player’s cost is the sum of the costs of the resources in its strategy, evaluated at
their total user weights. A state s is an α-approximate pure Nash equilibrium if

                Ci (s) ≤ αCi (ti , s−i )   for every player i and every legal strategy ti .              (1)

All deviations in this paper keep the other players fixed and include the deviator’s weight in the
new loads.

Proof mechanism. We group equal-weight players into ordered classes. A class front is a binary
string with at most one change in its linear order. The proof has three substantive steps. First, a
cyclic window construction gives a cost cap and usable improvement margins while allowing the
number of weight classes to grow. Second, every product of class fronts has a front-preserving
improvement: an adjacent phase difference outside two short intervals gives a local heavy-player exit,
while the remaining phase configurations are defeated by a full return window. Third, same-class
anchored triples give an improving repair outside the front set. Their leakage is exponentially small
in the degree, whereas the relevant cost-to-margin budget grows only as the exponential of a squared
logarithm. This permits the completion to use degree d itself.
    The short-window/full-return mechanism is the reason that asynchronous fronts cannot remain
stable. The triple completion alone does not establish this property for an arbitrary input game.
We separate these obligations so that the main lower bound cannot be inferred from an improving
cycle alone.

Scope and organization. The argument is constructive and finite but makes no polynomial-size
or computational-efficiency claim. Section 2 fixes the model. Section 3 proves the front-completion
lemma. Sections 4 and 5 build the input and prove its all-front property. Section 6 completes the
construction at the original degree and derives the universal consequences. Only the cited existence
bound is needed to pass from our lower bound to (2).

Corollary 6.3. The universal existence threshold satisfies a∗ (d) = Θ(d).

Proof. Theorem 1.1 gives a∗ (d) ≥ d/10. The d-approximate potential theorem of Caragiannis and

     For completeness, the monomial presentation of that result extends to the full nonnegative
polynomial convention here. First fix any player having a strategy consisting entirely of identically
zero-cost resources, including an empty strategy, to that strategy. Its cost is zero and it affects no
positive-cost resource. Remove these players; if none remain, the fixed state is an exact equilibrium.
Omit zero-cost resources from all remaining strategies. Every remaining strategy then has positive
cost. Split each positive monomial term into a separate resource with the original incidence. Realize
a constant term separately for each player by a private linear resource: when player i selects an
original resource with constant term a0 , it also selects the corresponding private resource of cost
(a0 /wi )x. Its occupied load is always wi , so its cost is exactly a0 . Identical resulting actions may be
merged. Costs and unilateral comparisons of the remaining players are preserved, and the fixed
zero-cost players are stable. These operations use degree at most d for d ≥ 1. Thus the imported
guarantee applies to every game in our definition of a∗ (d).


7    Scope and further questions
The construction permits a growing number of weight classes, an unbounded range of weights, and
strategies containing many resources. Its consequence concerns the worst-case existence threshold
over the full model. Bounds for restricted weight spectra or restricted strategy structures remain
separate questions. The exact leading constant in a∗ (d) and the smallest useful constructions are
also left open. Neither an efficient equilibrium algorithm nor an improved complexity classification
is required for the lower bound proved here.
