# Exact domain and representation conventions

This clarification makes the domain and representation clauses in definitions.md precise; it does not restrict the original mission. It supersedes any ambiguous reading of “distinct numerical translates” there.

In the exact Li2023 convention, a weak domain Π satisfies

\[
\Gamma\Pi=C,\qquad
\forall\gamma\in\Gamma:\quad
\gamma\Pi=\Pi\ \text{or}\ \gamma\Pi\cap\operatorname{Int}(\Pi)=\varnothing.
\]

An ordinary domain additionally satisfies

\[
\operatorname{Stab}_{\Gamma}(\Pi)=\{1\}.
\]

Consequently γΠ∩Int(Π)=∅ for every γ≠1 in the faithful numerical group. The usual interior/interior condition is also recorded for comparison, but “distinct translates” must not mean merely “distinct translated sets”, since that would allow a nontrivial stabilizer. The parent mission seeks the requested ordinary domains; a theorem with only weak domains remains a separately labelled partial result.

There is a second genuine formulation obligation when X is not Q-factorial. Strict transform under a pseudo-isomorphism need not preserve Cartier or R-Cartier divisors. Thus a natural representation of all PsAut on the Cartier relative space N^1(X/S) is not being asserted without proof in that generality. A solution of the full stated mission must establish the appropriate invariant numerical space and group action, or explain precisely the necessary corrected formulation and prove the strongest valid extension. Passing to a Q-factorialization and proving a theorem there does not by itself settle the original target on X.

These are open formulation/representation bridges. The source convention (Q-factorial X and connected fibers) and the user's broader setting remain explicitly distinguished.
