## 2. Exact strongest results established in this increment

No new unconditional all-dimensional geometric cone theorem has been proved. We have complete conventional proofs of the following reusable abstract statements, with internal cross-review of the stated arguments and exact hypotheses. No originality is claimed.

**Finite-stabilizer refinement.** For a faithful rational linear group action on a full-dimensional invariant cone C, a full-dimensional rational polyhedral weak domain D whose setwise stabilizer F is finite can be cut by finitely many comparisons with a rational linear functional into a strong domain with trivial setwise stabilizer. The proof compares ℓ(x) with ℓ(fx) for all f∈F. Finiteness and full dimension are used, not inferred from the word “weak”.

**Finite-kernel lifting.** Let p:V→V/T be an equivariant rational linear quotient of faithful linear actions, with finite kernel K of Γ→barΓ. Suppose Q is a full-dimensional rational polyhedral strong domain downstairs. If D=C∩p^−1(Q) is full-dimensional rational polyhedral and p(D)=Q, then the action upstairs has a rational polyhedral strong domain, obtained by finite K-cutting of D. There is no pointedness assumption on C. The geometric assertions that K is finite and D has the required polyhedrality remain hypotheses.

The full proofs and a direct counterexample to removing the kernel control are in [cone_obstructions_and_lifting.md](cone_obstructions_and_lifting.md). These implications are auxiliary theorems, not geometric conclusions about all Calabi–Yau fibrations.

**Finite-index covering.** A rational polyhedral orbit covering for a group G passes to any finite-index subgroup H: use the cone generated by the finitely many coset translates of the original cone. This proves covering, not separation. The bridge report also gives an integral hyperbolic example proving that coverage of every rational point need not cover irrational boundary rays, and a finite-kernel criterion from finite marked-nef-cone orbit data. See [bridge_and_refinement.md](bridge_and_refinement.md).

## 3. Counterexamples and rejected shortcuts

For C={(x,y):y≥0}, T={(x,0)}, and g(x,y)=(x+y,y), the quotient is a ray with trivial action. Yet no convex fundamental cone upstairs has strong separation: coverage forces the whole fixed line T into it, and then any positive-height point forces the cone to be all C. C is only a weak domain, with infinite stabilizer. This abstract example has not been realized as a Calabi–Yau fibration and does not refute the conjecture.

On a non-CM abelian surface E×E, the full closed nef cone has irrational rank-one extremal rays. No rational polyhedral subcone can meet one of these rays away from zero, so no union of rational-linear translates of rational polyhedral subcones covers the entire closed nef cone. These missing rays are not effective. This disproves the overly strong replacement of the effective cone by the full closure, while respecting the known abelian-variety theorem.

The proposed Poincaré-class test on E×E→E is rejected: the mixed class has self-intersection −2 absolutely but degree zero on every contracted fiber, hence is zero in the actual relative numerical space. It cannot be used as an example of a nonzero relative irregularity direction.

## 5. Exact unproved content on the chosen full-scope routes

1. **Actual geometric orbit covering.** Construct enough genuine automorphisms/pseudo-automorphisms for arbitrary Calabi–Yau pairs to cover the requested cone by translates of a finite rational polyhedral cone. A lattice symmetry or monodromy operator is not automatically induced by a birational map. Even arbitrary smooth absolute CY threefolds remain beyond the benchmarks checked here.
2. **All-dimensional model and chamber control.** Supply the good-minimal-model/geography/abundance hypotheses used by the chosen reduction, and establish nef domains and finiteness, not only a movable covering. Good models alone do not settle item 1: the three-dimensional good-model theory is already known while the general absolute threefold cone problem remains open.
3. **Generic-field descent and extension.** Work over C(S) with the actual group. Show which geometric-fiber maps descend, which extend to pseudo-automorphisms of X/S, and which stabilize a nef chamber and hence are regular. Numerical, geometric and actual relative groups must not be conflated.
4. **Relative irregularity and effective boundaries.** Construct the required Picard/Albanese translations for arbitrary-dimensional fibers and q, prove the kernel/linear-subspace assertions used upstairs, and establish the actual effective boundary and polyhedrality of D. Quotient coverage or rational-point coverage alone is insufficient by the explicit examples.
5. **Formulation bridges.** Resolve the non-Q-factorial and non-connected extensions and the precise requested group/cone/finiteness conventions. These are not allowed to disappear under a terminalization or an unproved replacement of the cone.

These are precise unproved steps of the proposed routes, not a claim that we have proved a logically minimal, unique or independent list of obstructions. Calling a universal orbit-cover theorem the “missing theorem” is nearly a restatement of the cone conjecture; it is not a new solution strategy by itself.

## 6. Concrete continuation

A further primary-source update is [constructibility_addendum.md](constructibility_addendum.md), using Serebrennikov, arXiv:2604.27303v2 (2026-10-05), Definition 2.11 and Theorem 5.5. This latest version explicitly fills a gap in v1. For a fixed absolute projective klt rational pair (X,B) with K_X+B≡0 and r(K_X+B) integral Cartier, every effective crepant klt model (Y,B_Y) has coefficients in {0,1/r,…,(r−1)/r}. If each such Y admits a very ample Cartier A_Y with A_Y^d≤N for a uniform N, then a general complete-intersection curve and adjunction give

\[
(B_Y)_{\rm red}\cdot A_Y^{d-1}
\le r B_Y\cdot A_Y^{d-1}
=-rK_Y\cdot A_Y^{d-1}
\le r((d-1)N+2).
\]

Thus the exact bounded-polarization hypothesis of the cited Theorem 5.5 holds, yielding finitely many log-isomorphism classes in that effective crepant class. The grid and adjunction deduction have been checked by a nonauthor. The external preprint theorem is used as a cited input, not independently reproved here. The uniform very-ample degree bound N is entirely unproved. This is an absolute Q-boundary conditional finiteness theorem, not A, B, relative C, or the full R-boundary mission. No novelty is claimed.

An ordinary flop test shows why carrying one polarization across all models fails: its strict transform has negative degree on the flopped curve even though divisor volume is unchanged. An initial numerical-orbit-to-section-ring shortcut was rejected and removed; the corrected argument above uses Theorem 5.5 directly. Fixed-volume numerical orbit finiteness alone must not be reported as a cone fundamental domain.

The next execution task is the construction problem in Route A of literature_and_strategy.md: given two marked minimal models, identify geometric data whose equivalence produces an actual isomorphism and hence a pseudo-automorphism, and determine whether finitely many rational chamber pieces cover the non-big effective boundary modulo those maps. Test the proposed criterion against abelian reduction theory, IHS Torelli/MBM methods, and a smooth CY threefold where those special structures are absent. A test must either prove the required invariant/finiteness statement or expose a precise failure; it must not assume orbit covering as its own input.

Route C continues in parallel by proving or falsifying the exact finite marked-nef-cone orbit and effective-boundary hypotheses in the bridge report for arbitrary-dimensional fibers. If these are not proved, the finite-kernel lifting theorem remains conditional.
