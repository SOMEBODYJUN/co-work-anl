## General background monotonicity

Write the fixed background as nonnegative client loads \(b_x\). For \(r\ge1\), define

\[
v_r(b)=\max_{p\in\Delta(\mathcal S)}\min_{c\in\mathcal S^{r-1}}
\sum_a p_a\sum_{x\in a}\frac1{b_x+1+h_x(c)}.
\]

Here the opponents' tuple is chosen without observing the realized focal action; the common menu is unchanged. For any legal action \(A\), any \(r\ge2\), and every rival tuple \(c\) of length \(r-2\),

\[
f_{b+\mathbf1_A,c}(a)=f_{b,(A,c)}(a).
\]

The columns defining \(v_{r-1}(b+\mathbf1_A)\) are therefore a subset of the columns defining \(v_r(b)\). For each fixed \(p\), the minimum over that subset is at least the full minimum; maximizing preserves the inequality:

\[
\boxed{v_{r-1}(b+\mathbf1_A)\ge v_r(b).}
\]

This proof holds for arbitrary nonnegative real backgrounds, not merely loads generated by a played prefix. The verifier additionally checked every matrix-embedding identity for every possible prefix of length zero or one, next action, focal action, and remaining rival in the supplied instance. These finite checks corroborate the implementation; the subset proof is universal.

## Telescoping and scope

Avoid using the same subscript both for number of remaining players and for position in the realized path. Let \(b_i\) be the background before player \(i\), \(r_i=n-i+1\), and

\[
V_i=v_{r_i}(b_i),\qquad s=V_1=v_n(0).
\]

At the last node, SPE optimality gives \(V_n=u_n\). With the final zero-coefficient term interpreted as zero,

\[
\boxed{W-ns
=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left[u_i-V_i+(n-i)(V_{i+1}-V_i)\right]+(u_n-V_n).}
\]

To check, each intermediate \(V_j\), \(j\ge2\), has coefficient \((n-j+1)-(n-j+1)=0\); the coefficient of \(V_1\) is \(-n\). No equilibrium assumption is needed for the algebra; equilibrium is used for \(V_n=u_n\).

In the 113-client example the full terms are

\[
\left(-571165576/1705656547,\;467/53,\;0\right),
\]

so a negative first increment is outweighed by a positive second increment. The 3,227-client terms are \((-111934422/9663953,250,0)\).

For the weak bridge, let

\[
g_i=u_i+(r_i-\tfrac32)V_{i+1}-(r_i-\tfrac12)V_i\quad(i<n).
\]

Then

\[
W-(n-\tfrac12)s=\sum_{i<n}g_i+u_n-\tfrac12V_n.
\]

The final term is \(u_n/2\), not zero. This explains why the negative weak first increment also does not refute the global weak bridge.

The two witnesses disprove the proposed universal local sufficient inequalities

\[
u_i+(r_i-1)V_{i+1}\ge r_iV_i,
\qquad
u_i+(r_i-\tfrac32)V_{i+1}\ge(r_i-\tfrac12)V_i.
\]

They do not disprove the aggregate bridges \(W\ge ns\), \(W\ge(n-1/2)s\), or half coverage. Both aggregate bridges actually hold strictly in both witnesses. Monotonicity alone does not make every increment nonnegative because \(u_i-V_i\) may be negative.

The known bound \(s\ge\operatorname{OPT}_n/(2n-1)\) has the correct common-menu, oblivious-rival quantifiers. The weaker root aggregate bridge would suffice for main half coverage since

\[
W\ge(n-\tfrac12)s\ge
\frac{n-1/2}{2n-1}\operatorname{OPT}_n=\frac12\operatorname{OPT}_n.
\]

## Explicit mathematical-error list

1. No mathematical error found in either witness, its exact saddle values, the simple 113-client bounds, or their stated local-only obstruction scope.
2. The requested identity written as \(\sum u_i-nv_n\) is ambiguous if the same request also defines path-indexed \(v_i=v_{r_i}(b_i)\) and \(v_n=u_n\). Under the path-indexed definition the left side must be \(W-nv_1\). It is correct as \(W-nv_n(0)\) when \(v_n\) denotes root security by remaining-player count. With \(v_n\) literally the last value, it is false.
3. `half_coverage_margin = 2673` in the larger author's audit is the doubled margin \(2W-\mathrm{OPT}\). The undoubled margin is \(W-\mathrm{OPT}/2=2673/2\); labels should make the factor two explicit.
