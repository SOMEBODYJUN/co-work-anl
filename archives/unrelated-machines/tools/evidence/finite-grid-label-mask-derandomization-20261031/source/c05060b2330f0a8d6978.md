If m=0 return empty labels. If a=0 select the first positive-probability label of each item. In both branches the potential equals its expectation. Otherwise put h_ij=Qw_ij, a nonnegative integer with each row total at most Q. Remove the label-independent sum_i ln V_i from the potential. For each i,k=0,...,Q precompute a rational approximation fhat_i(k) to -ln(nu_i+k/Q), of error at most alpha=epsilon/(2am).

For any fixed prefix, items still undecided remain mutually independent with their original label probabilities. The residual retained integer weight for each agent has generating polynomial

    product_(j undecided) [(1-pi_(j,t_i))+pi_(j,t_i) z^(h_ij)].

Its degree is at most Q. A Q+1-entry convolution-by-a-two-term-factor DP computes its rational probability distribution. With pi=P_j/D_j, maintain integer numerators by N'_k=(D_j-P_j)N_k+P_jN_(k-h), common denominator product_j D_j. Coefficients sum to that denominator; hence their bit lengths are bounded by the sum of the actual denominator bit lengths. Zero probabilities/weights and probability one are legal. No division by small prefix/label probabilities is needed.

For every positive-probability candidate label t of the next item, add its actual integer contribution to each agent base, compute the above remaining DP, and sum the rational fhat table expectations. The approximation error for that candidate conditional potential is at most a*alpha. Choose a minimum rational estimate. The true chosen child expectation is at most the minimum true child plus 2a*alpha, and that minimum is at most the current parent expectation by the law of total expectation. After all m choices the actual potential is therefore at most its original expectation plus 2am*alpha=epsilon. Every chosen label had positive original probability. The V_i constant is then restored semantically, without computing irrational expectations exactly.

For completeness, approximate ln x for any positive rational x by x=2^h y, 1<=y<2, |h| bounded by its numerator/denominator bit lengths. Set z=(y-1)/(y+1), so 0<=z<1/3, and use 2*sum_(k=0)^(N-1) z^(2k+1)/(2k+1). The tail is at most 9^(-N). Approximate ln2 by z=1/3 too. Choose N with (|h|+1)9^(-N)<=alpha. This uses polynomially many rational operations and polynomial bit lengths in the actual input and log(1+1/alpha); it does not expand by 1/nu, 1/epsilon or 1/pi. All log arguments nu_i+k/Q are positive. There are a(Q+1) such approximations, and even recomputing every candidate DP from scratch takes polynomial(a,m,L,Q,inputbits,log(1+1/epsilon)) total cost. The dependence on Q is by value; Q=4m in the stated intended application gives true total-bit polynomial time, with no claim for enormous binary Q.

Finally a physical item in both T_i and T_h would have the same single chosen label t_i=t_h and belong to both S_i and S_h. The extra fixed-label disjointness premise excludes this for distinct agents. Thus all T_i are globally disjoint, including zero-weight items. This consequence uses that extra premise, whereas the potential computation alone does not. Empty sets and all degenerate branches are covered above. No statement about any downstream NSW constants, source reduction, or global UM ratio is made by this proof.
