## 3. Elementary consequences of global M2

Work in the Boolean/one-hot moment quotient through degree four. If a polynomial r of degree at most two has (L(r^2)=0\), PSD and Cauchy–Schwarz imply

\[
L(rq)=0\qquad(\deg q\le2). \tag{R}
\]

At any unit-only machine, legal CONFIG(T), (T<2\), contains at most one job. Thus for distinct endpoint variables x,y on that machine,

\[
L(xy)=0,
\quad L((xy)^2)=L(xy)=0.
\]

Consequently xy is a degree-two radical as in (R), including against globally indexed q. If S is the sum of any subset of those endpoint indicators, then

\[
S^2-S=\sum_x(x^2-x)+2\sum_{x<y}xy
\]

vanishes when multiplied by every polynomial of degree at most two. So S and (1-S\) are affine Boolean indicators **to the full degree needed here**. This is not a presumed globally realizable event.

I also use this general elementary lemma. If affine h,k have their Boolean differences (h^2-h\), (k^2-k\) vanishing against all degree-two polynomials, then

\[
L(hk)=L((hk)^2)\ge0. \tag{B}
\]

Indeed replace (h^2\) by h against (k^2\), and (k^2\) by k against h; then use the global M2 square. Also (L(h)=L(h^2)\ge0\). This pays for products of the **derived affine** indicators below without asserting positivity of arbitrary quartic literal monomials.

## 4. Forward map: all original exports determine the hole

\[
a_i=\sum_{r>i}O_r. \tag{F}
\]

Assign the leaf's left-selector indicator to (a_i\) and its right-selector indicator to (1-a_i\). Set its left incidence's port indicator to (a_i\), private indicator to (1-a_i\); its right incidence's port indicator to (1-a_i\), private indicator to (a_i\). Every substituted primitive is affine Boolean through degree four by §3. Every new job one-hot is an affine identity. Pullback of a new degree-four polynomial has old degree at most four. \[
a(1-x)=0,\qquad b(1-y)=0

as degree-two radical relations; a+b=1 throughout degree four. For (q=(1-x)(1-y)\),

\[
L(q)=L(aq)+L(bq)=0.
\]

\[
