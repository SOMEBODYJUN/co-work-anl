## The research arc and why it matters

1. **Starting gap: weighted atomic clients, two facilities, shared catalog.** Clients must choose a reachable facility, independently mixing if appropriate, and minimize the realized load including their own weight. Facilities first choose locations, and a complete exact client Nash-equilibrium continuation is specified for every labeled location pair. IJCAI 2024 supplied a golden-ratio lower bound and left its tightness open. The repository's `SC-PHI-E/A` proves a matching universal upper bound and polynomial-bit construction for explicit rational inputs using a finite continuation menu, full-catalog response cycles, heavy-pair/anchor arguments, and a strong local chord. The inherited lower family was rechecked and its source-figure weight corrected; it is **prior work**, not our discovery. Value: closes a concrete sharp-threshold gap with an algorithmic certificate, while handling multiple possible client equilibria rather than a predetermined outcome. Read [`current/shared/README.md`](current/shared/README.md), especially `proof.md`, `local_chord_full.md`, `algorithm.md`, and `sharp_phi_lower.md`.
2. **Different location catalogs.** `HC-2-UP/LOW` gives a pure-continuation four-seed construction at factor 2 for arbitrary two nonempty catalogs and a separate near-2 integer lower family. It supplies the unrestricted comparison class and a second sharp constant, but remains marked **internal candidate** pending final paper-level sign-off. Do not say that the numerical 2 statement itself first appeared here: earlier literature asserted a general `k` upper bound, with a displayed co-location argument that requires a common feasible location; Vos 2023 explicitly limits that proof to unrestricted catalogs. Read [`current/heterogeneous/README.md`](current/heterogeneous/README.md), `four_seed_algorithm.md`, `full_catalog_cycle.md`, `sharp_two_lower.md`.
3. **Sparse intersection and its boundary.** `HC-RHO` initially showed sharp \(\rho=2\cos(\pi/7)\approx1.80194\) if every legal cross-catalog pair shares at most one client **and** at least one catalog has at most two locations. The later `SPARSE-RHO-ALL` removed the length limit: both catalogs can be arbitrarily large, with the same sharp factor and a fixed complete pure client-NE selection. The upper proof uses a high-reach descent and the first high-to-low boundary: the one-overlap condition forces the allegedly distinct shared clients to be the same, leading to the cubic contradiction at \(\rho\). The strict unique-NE `2×2` lower family already belongs to this larger class. This is the newest flagship result and may be independently publishable if positioned with a strong structural story and priority survives. Read [`current/heterogeneous/sparse_unbounded_rho.md`](current/heterogeneous/sparse_unbounded_rho.md) and `restricted_rho.md`.
4. **Why the proof mechanism matters.** `SPARSE-LONG-CYCLE` shows arbitrary-length exact response cycles under one-overlap. The stronger `SPARSE-BAD-LONG-ALL` shows genuinely non-\(r\)-stable instances with such unique full-response cycles for every \(1\le r<\rho\). Thus the sharp upper proof cannot be justified by a universal constant-size *exact response-closed* core; the first-boundary method escapes that obstruction. This is a supporting structural theorem, **not** a kernelization/complexity lower bound or a counterexample at \(\rho\). Read `sparse_bad_long_cycles.md` and `sparse_high_reach_barrier.md`.
5. **Exact computation layer.** Support-spectrum enumeration, bounded-overlap instance optimization, MITM, pseudo-polynomial DP, parameter work, and local weak NP-hardness clarify what one can compute for a fixed input. `EXACT-KAPPA` uses \(O(|U_1||U_2|[n+\kappa3^\kappa])\) rational operations with polynomial bit length, so \(\kappa\le1\) gives a polynomial exact **instance-optimal** \(\alpha^*\) algorithm. This is a potentially useful short algorithmic companion to paper B, but its ledger status is still internal candidate. A short certificate of an attained ratio alone does not prove instance optimality. Local hardness is not global hardness for \(\alpha^*(I)\). Read [`current/local_and_exact/README.md`](current/local_and_exact/README.md).

Within arbitrary heterogeneous catalogs, maximum cross-pair overlap \(\kappa=0\) gives exact factor 1, \(\kappa\le1\) gives sharp \(\rho\), and allowing \(\kappa\ge2\) gives sharp 2 (subject to `HC-2` sign-off). This **is** a nested overlap classification. Shared catalog and single-overlap are **incomparable restrictions**, so the shared-catalog \(\varphi\) belongs alongside that table rather than inside it. All these results concern **two** facilities; no general \(k\)-facility guarantee follows. The upper statements choose a complete continuation; they do not hold for every equilibrium selection. Keep positive weights, mandatory service, realized linear load cost, and exact customer NE explicit. Existence over positive reals and polynomial bit complexity over encoded rationals are different claims.
