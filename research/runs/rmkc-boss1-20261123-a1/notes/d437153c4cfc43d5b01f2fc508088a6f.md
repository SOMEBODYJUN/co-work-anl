# Genus-independent vertical kernel, and a proposed reduction of the irregular generic fibre

Run rmkc-boss1-20261123-a1, informed-a. Version 1 for independent review. The common target is the strong closed rational polyhedral domain on the actual effective movable cone for the numerical image. No change to the geometric assumptions. The finite-stabilizer refinement already in the archive is an input. No assertion of priority.

## A. The vertical kernel is finite

Let \(V=N^1(X/S)_{\mathbb R}\), \(T\subset V\) be the real span of the classes of vertical prime divisors, and \(p:V\to V/T\). Let \(\Gamma\) denote the numerical image specified in the problem. Assume good minimal models of effective klt pairs exist in the relative dimension. This holds in dimensions at most two. The following proof uses only a pointed effective two-generator cone in Li–Zhao, Theorem 2.7.

1. \(T\) is rational. Also \(T\subset\operatorname{Eff}(X/S)\), including both signs. For a vertical prime \(Z\), put a proper closed subset containing \(f(Z)\) in the support of an effective Cartier divisor \(A\) of the quasiprojective base. This is possible by restricting a sufficiently high degree hypersurface in a projective embedding. Then \(k f^*A-Z\geq0\) for large \(k\) and has relative numerical class \(-[Z]\). Every pseudoautomorphism over \(S\) permutes the finitely many divisorial components of \(f^{-1}(f(Z))\). The image \(f(Z)\) is closed by properness, and every prime divisor contained in this proper inverse image is an irreducible component. Choose finitely many classes of vertical primes spanning \(T\). Their respective finite orbits show that \(\Gamma|T\) is finite.

2. Let \(g\in\Gamma\) act trivially on \(T\) and \(V/T\). If \(g\ne1\), choose an effective Cartier divisor \(A\) with rational ample class \(a\) and \(l=(g-1)a\ne0\). Rational ample classes span \(V\), and their effective representatives can be obtained by choosing a sufficiently positive absolute multiple. The class \(l\in T\) is rational. Since \(g l=l\), we have \(g^na=a+nl\). We may suppose the relative dimension is positive, since in relative dimension zero the generic fibre is a point and a birational self-map over \(S\) induces the identity on the function field. In positive relative dimension \(a\notin T\), by restriction to the generic fibre. Thus \(P=\operatorname{Cone}(a,l)\) is a pointed rational polyhedral cone contained in the actual effective cone. A rational class with an effective real representative has an effective rational representative: solve the rational numerical equations for the coefficients of a fixed finite prime support in its positive face.

3. Represent \(g\) by a pseudoautomorphism \(\phi\) respecting \(\Delta\). Let \(B_n=(\phi^n)_*A\) for \(n\geq0\). Infinitely many \([B_n]\) lie in one member of the finite cover of \(P\) of Li–Zhao, Theorem 2.7 (v8, pp. 11–12). Fix an index \(n_0\) and indices \(n_j\to\infty\) in this member. The marking \(\phi^{-n}\) to \(X\) is a weak log canonical model for \((X/S,\Delta+\epsilon B_n)\) for sufficiently small \(\epsilon>0\). To verify it, resolve the marking by \(u,v\) (source and destination). The two exceptional sets agree in codimension one. The difference between the pullbacks of \(K_X+\Delta\) is exceptional and numerically trivial over \(u\), hence zero by the negativity lemma. The divisor \(u^*B_n-v^*A\) is exceptional over \(u\) and anti-nef over \(u\): every image on \(X\) of an \(u\)-contracted curve lies over one point of \(S\). The same negativity lemma gives \(u^*B_n-v^*A\geq0\). The target log divisor is nef over \(S\). These properties give the required discrepancies and the marked weak model. A small common \(\epsilon\) exists separately for each pair of indices; the cell theorem does not require a uniform threshold for this sequence.

4. Apply Theorem 2.7 first using the marking \(\phi^{-n_0}\) for \(B_{n_0}\) and then using \(\phi^{-n_j}\) for \(B_{n_j}\). Nefness on their respective copies of \(X\) gives
\[
 a+(n_j-n_0)l,\quad a-(n_j-n_0)l\ \in\operatorname{Nef}(X/S).
\]
Indeed \(K_X+\Delta\equiv_S0\). Divide both by \(n_j-n_0\) and take limits. The relative nef cone is closed and pointed by the contracted-curve definition of relative numerical equivalence. Thus \(l=0\), a contradiction. Hence the simultaneous kernel on \(T,V/T\) is trivial. The kernel on \(V/T\) embeds in the finite image on \(T\).

The argument also applies to a single element for which just \(g a-a\in T\): take a power acting trivially on \(T\). The two-sided nef argument shows this power fixes \(a\). A pseudoautomorphism sending a relatively ample numerical class to itself is regular, by the usual ample-to-ample criterion for a small birational map (or relative Proj of its strict-transform section ring). Its numerical image has finite order, since the stabilizer of an interior point in the integral automorphism group of a closed pointed full-dimensional nef cone is finite. For instance the order interval \(\{x:a\pm x\in\operatorname{Nef}\}\) is a compact neighborhood of zero; such a stabilizer preserves it and is a discrete subgroup of its compact linear stabilizer. Consequently every group element stabilizing \(p(a)\) has finite order in the numerical image. Its whole stabilizer in \(\Gamma\) is finite by intersecting with a torsion-free finite-index principal congruence subgroup (level three suffices).

## B. Effective lineality and lifting the quotient domain

Write \(W=\overline{\operatorname{Mov}}\cap(-\overline{\operatorname{Mov}})\) and \(E=C_e\cap(-C_e)\). Then \(E=W\cap T\). For if an effective class lies in \(W\), its restriction to the geometric generic fibre is pseudoeffective with pseudoeffective negative. It is zero there. An effective representative containing a horizontal prime has strictly positive degree against an ample \((r-1)\)-fold intersection on that fibre, where \(r>0\) is the relative dimension. Hence the representative is vertical. The reverse inclusion follows from A.1. In particular \(E\) is rational by Li–Zhao, Proposition 4.4 (rationality of \(W\)). The kernel on \(V/E\) is contained in the kernel on \(V/T\). The larger kernel on \(V/W\) can be infinite, consistently with the elliptic examples.

If a rational polyhedral domain \(D\subset p(C_e)\) covering \(p(C_e)\) has weak separation and finite stabilizer for the induced numerical image, put \(R=p^{-1}D\). It is rational polyhedral. Every point is the sum of an actual effective lift of its image and an element of \(T\), so \(R\subset\operatorname{Eff}\). Then \(P=R\cap C_e\) is rational polyhedral. Here are the details with no rational-hull substitution. Split \(R\) into finitely many pointed rational polyhedral cones if it contains lines. Apply Theorem 2.7 on each. A cell meeting \(C_e\) consists entirely of classes in \(\overline{\operatorname{Mov}}\): choose a point of this intersection, represented by an effective divisor \(B\). Good minimal models and Li–Zhao, Lemma 2.3, provide a marked small weak log canonical model of \(\Delta+\epsilon B\) (also the displayed union in the proof of Lemma 5.1(2)). The same small model works for any effective \(D'\) in the same cell by Theorem 2.7, with \(\epsilon\) shrunk for the two chosen representatives. Shrinking preserves the choice of model, since \(K+\Delta\) is crepant under this small map and relatively numerically trivial. Each transformed class is nef, so its strict transform lies in the closed movable cone. Cell closures remain in the closed movable cone and in the effective cone \(R\). Thus \(P\) is the convex union of finitely many closed rational polyhedral cones, and is their rational polyhedral conical hull. We have \(p(P)=D\). Coverage lifts, and \(p(\operatorname{Int}P)\subset\operatorname{Int}D\) by openness of a linear quotient. The stabilizer is the inverse image of the finite stabilizer of \(D\). By A it is finite. Apply the archived finite-stabilizer refinement. This reduces the original problem to construction on the exact quotient \(p(C_e)\); a chamber on the rational hull of the closed movable cone alone does not solve it.

## C. Two finite-inequality proofs for a variable positive semidefinite form

Input: a rational parameter cone \(D_0=\sum_i\mathbb R_{\geq0}d_i\), a lattice \(\Lambda\) in \(U\), positive semidefinite rational forms \(q_i\) on \(U\) with \(\sum q_i\) positive definite, and \(q_{\sum a_i d_i}=\sum a_i q_i\) well-defined. Let \(S\) be a finite union of \(\Lambda\)-cosets containing the zero coset, in a finite superlattice. For \(m\in S\) impose \(2\langle m,w\rangle\le q_D(m)\). The target inequality is linear in \((D,w)\).

* **Dickson descent.** Clear denominators on the superlattice. In each residue class of \(S\) modulo \(2\Lambda\), the vectors \((q_i(m))_i\) have finitely many coordinatewise minima in \(\mathbb N^k\). Each such vector is attained by finitely many points since \(\sum q_i\) is positive definite. Retain all these points. For another \(m\), there is \(n\equiv m\bmod 2\Lambda\) with every \(q_i(n)\le q_i(m)\) and one strict. Set \(a=(m+n)/2\in m+\Lambda\subset S\), \(b=(m-n)/2\in\Lambda\subset S\). Then \(a+b=m\) and \(q_i(a)+q_i(b)=(q_i(m)+q_i(n))/2\le q_i(m)\). Both have strictly lower total \(\sum q_i\). Descent proves all inequalities. No diagonalization common to the \(q_i\) is required.
* **Sign cones.** Decompose each rational positive semidefinite form into a rational positive linear combination of squares of rational linear forms by symmetric elimination. The common sign chambers of all resulting linear forms are finitely many rational polyhedral cones. In any one of them, the \(q_i\) are superadditive. For \(S=\Lambda\), a finite Hilbert generating set in each cone therefore suffices. For \(S\subset\Lambda'\), a finite superlattice, take Hilbert generators \(v_j\) for the cone's monoid in \(\Lambda'\). For \(m\in S\), write \(m=\sum n_jv_j\), reduce every \(n_j\) modulo the exponent of \(\Lambda'/\Lambda\). The remainder \(r\) lies in \(m+\Lambda\subset S\), is in the same cone and belongs to a fixed finite set. The peeled multiples are translations in \(\Lambda\) in that same cone. Keeping these remainders and the finitely many multiples gives every inequality by superadditivity. If the \(q_i\) have a common rational kernel one can first quotient it and record the equalities imposed by its integral basis and negatives. In the intended full-dimensional base chamber some ample form is positive definite on the translation-parameter space.

On any rational face, \(\ker q_{\sum a_i d_i}=\bigcap_{a_i>0}\ker q_i\) is rational. If \(T_D:U\to U^*\) is the corresponding semidefinite operator and \(w\in\operatorname{im}T_D\), the nearest-centre problem is attained after quotienting \(U\) by this kernel: the image of the lattice is discrete, finite unions of cosets remain discrete, and the induced norm is definite. Conversely the inequalities for integral kernel elements with both signs force \(w\in\operatorname{im}T_D\). If a crystallographic group acts by \(u\mapsto R_g u+b_g\) with all \(R_g\) preserving each \(q_i\), the Voronoi weak chamber of a centre over the base chamber has finite stabilizer and exact separation, by strictness of each nonzero rational linear constraint in its ambient interior. At interior base points \(T_D\) is invertible, and distinct centre functions have nonzero constraint. Coverage follows from attainment on every face and equivariance. These are conditional linear algebra statements; the geometric identification of the inputs is separate.

## D. Proposed geometric connection for irregular fibres

Let \(F=X_\eta\), \(B=\operatorname{Aut}^0(F,\Delta_\eta)\), \(P=\operatorname{Pic}^0(F)\) and \(J\) be the kernel of \(P(K)\otimes\mathbb Q\to V_{\mathbb Q}/T_{\mathbb Q}\) under extension of generic divisors; descent of Picard-scheme points can be handled after multiplying by a finite extension degree. After fixing an ample class \(H\), let \(\theta_H:B\to P\) be translation of \(H\) minus \(H\), with pullback/pushforward sign used consistently. This is an isogeny by Li, Theorem 4.1 and Lemma 4.4. A candidate parameter space is \(U=(B(K)\otimes\mathbb Q)/\theta_H^{-1}J\), identified with \(P(K)_{\mathbb Q}/J\). Section A's single-polarization stabilizer shows that, for an actual translating subgroup lifting to numerical pseudoautomorphisms with finite index, the kernel is independent of \(H\) and lies in the kernel of every \(\theta_D\bmod J\). This permits endomorphisms \(T_D=\theta_H^{-1}\theta_D\) on \(U\). Representatives of polarizations are Rosati-symmetric and nef ones semipositive on the abelian variety. One must establish these facts in the descended representation for the actual log pair and numerical quotient, including the finite-index lifting assertion. A positive involution on the rational algebra generated by these symmetric endomorphisms would give a rational invariant scalar product on every finite-dimensional quotient module by embedding it in a sum of copies of its semisimple regular module equipped with a positive rational trace form.

For the centre one candidate is a rational linear section of restriction to \(N^1(F)\). Choose a point over a finite extension and use its orbit map \(i_x:B\to F\). The map \(i_x^*\) on \(\operatorname{Pic}^0\) is an isogeny by the Albanese homogeneous fibration. For a divisor \(L\), take the antisymmetric part \((i_x^*L-[-1]^*i_x^*L)/2\) and apply the inverse isogeny over \(\mathbb Q\), then take the normalized Galois trace. This is a linear retraction onto the Picard-zero part. To check the needed compatibility, prove that if \(L\) is semiample this antisymmetric part is in the image of \(\phi_{i_x^*L}\) on rationalized points. A positive multiple of \(i_x^*L\) is basepoint free, so its restriction to the connected kernel of its polarization is semiample and numerically trivial, hence torsion. The vanishing of the antisymmetric part there should identify it with a point of the dual of the abelian quotient. Under \(i_x^*\) this image equals that of \(\theta_L\). This gives fibre coordinates \(w\in\operatorname{im}T_D\) with no non-linear shift if one proves both inclusions on the effective movable cone, including irrational convex combinations and the field-of-definition issue.

For the kernel \(G_0\) on generic \(N^1\), once translations form a full lattice of finite index, a rational finite-holonomy normalization does not need an integral splitting. The affine space of rational splittings modulo changes \(D\mapsto T_D n\), \(n\in U_{\mathbb Q}\), has a finite action of \(G_0/\Lambda\); take the average of finitely many translated sections. With a lift \(s'\) of this average, \(g s'(D)-s'(D)=T_D n_g\) uniformly in \(D\). At \(D=H\), one reads off the rational displacement. If \(A_g\) is the linear action on Picard-zero coordinates and \(B_g\) conjugates translations, \(A_g T_D=T_D B_g\). The normalization \(T_H=1\) gives \(A_g=B_g\), hence commutation; averaging the already compatible form over finite holonomy makes the action orthogonal. The centre orbit parameters are a finite union of cosets of the translation lattice. Section C then builds a weak domain with finite stabilizer over a known rational generic cone domain, after the geometric identifications have been paid.

## Handoff and sources

Preferred route: A+B reduce to the projected effective cone in generic linear equivalence; build a single chamber there via C+D. A distinct obstruction route is to search for a failure of translation lifting or positivity in D that is realized geometrically. A pure shear into *vertical* classes is excluded by A under the hypotheses. A pure shear into missing, noneffective closed-lineality directions gives no counterexample, as the two-dimensional diagnostic already in the archive illustrates.
