# independent

## Current mathematical obligation

Investigate geometric degeneracy, including the difference between the effective cone and its rational hull, over a non-Q-factorial base. All vector spaces in this note are relative to the original base. No change of base or of the group in the proposed conclusion is implicit.

## Derivation and evidence

### 1. Finite kernel of the action modulo vertical classes

Let `U` be the span of all vertical prime divisor classes in `V=N^1(X/S)_R`. It is a rational vector subspace. Every class in `U` is effective. Indeed, for a vertical prime `E`, choose a base effective Cartier divisor containing its image, and pull back a sufficiently high multiple. Its pullback minus `E` is effective, hence gives an effective representative of `-[E]` over `S`. The group acts through a finite group on `U`: a finite basis can be chosen among these classes, and each chosen prime `E` has finite orbit, since its transforms over `S` must be among the finitely many divisorial components of the fixed proper closed subset `f^{-1}(f(E))`.

**Lemma.** Assume existence of good minimal models for effective klt pairs in the relative dimension, in particular in relative dimension at most two. The kernel `K` of `Gamma -> GL(V/U)` is finite.

**Proof.** Pass in `K` to the finite-index subgroup `K0` fixing `U` pointwise and preserving coefficients of the given boundary. This last passage is legitimate since the group in the primary source preserves the finite support, and the image of the actual coefficient-preserving subgroup in `K` has finite index. Fix a rational relatively ample class `H`. The cone `P=R_+ H+U` is rational polyhedral and contained in `Eff(X/S)`. Every `gH` for `g in K0` belongs to `P` and is an interior point of the marked nef cone `g Nef(X/S)`. Li–Zhao, arXiv:2206.13701v8, Theorem 2.7 gives a finite subdivision of `P` by relatively open rational polyhedral cones with the same weak log canonical models for each effective perturbation in the cell. Consequently only finitely many of the marked nef cones `g Nef(X/S)` can occur. In detail, if `gH,hH` lie in one cell, the SQM carrying `gH` to an ample class is a weak log canonical model for a small effective perturbation representing `gH`; the theorem makes the transform of `hH` on the same model nef. Since `hH` is interior to the second nef cone, the two marked nef cones coincide. This last implication also follows by the section-ring characterization of the marked ample model, or by disjointness of distinct ample chambers, together with closed convexity and full dimension. Positive rescaling does not change a weak model, so the perturbations can meet the klt requirement. A small modification given by a pseudoautomorphism preserving the coefficients is crepant for the original pair by relative linear triviality and the negativity lemma. Thus the stabilizer `K1` of `Nef(X/S)` in `K0` has finite index. For `g in K1`, `(g-I)^2=0` as the image of `g-I` is in `U` and `g` fixes `U`. For every relatively ample `A`, the points `A+n(g-I)A=g^n A` lie in `Nef(X/S)` for all positive and negative integers `n`. After dividing by `|n|` and taking limits, `±(g-I)A` lie in the closed nef cone. This cone is pointed by the definition of relative numerical equivalence. Hence `gA=A` for every ample `A`, so `g=I` in the specified linear image. QED.

This rules out pure shears along actually effective vertical directions. The closed movable cone can still have other lineality, including directions whose nonzero vectors have no effective representatives. Li–Zhao Remark 5.4, citing Kawamata Example 3.8(2), records the distinction explicitly.

### 2. Quotient lifting lemma

Write `p:V->V/U`. Suppose the faithful induced group on the quotient has a rational polyhedral strong domain `D` on `p(C)`. Every rational generator of `D` has a rational effective lift: its effective expression gives a real nonnegative solution to a rational linear system involving finitely many integral effective divisor classes, which also has a rational nonnegative solution. It follows that `p^{-1}(D)` is rational polyhedral and entirely contained in `Eff`, by adding the whole subspace `U`. Intersecting it with `Movbar` gives a rational polyhedral cone: apply the Shokurov decomposition of Li–Zhao, Theorem 2.7, and the weak-model argument of Lemmas 5.1–5.2, or Li, arXiv:2309.04673v1, Lemma 2.5 with the trivial group, together with the equality of effective closed-movable and movable in these relative dimensions. To obtain that equality directly, an effective class in the closed movable cone has a small weak log canonical model for its small effective perturbation (Li–Zhao Lemma 2.3) and is semiample there by their Theorem 2.4. Conversely an actual movable divisor class lies in the effective closed movable cone. These statements go through for real effective classes using the rational finite subdivision and the semiampleness of real good minimal models. The intersection is exactly `p^{-1}(D)∩C`, so coverage is immediate. Since `p` is an open surjective linear map and the domains are full-dimensional, the weak separation property is inherited. Its stabilizer is the finite group `K` in §1. Refining with a general rational functional on a finite `K`-orbit yields a strong rational domain. Explicitly take the inequalities `l(x)>=l(kx)` for all `k in K`, where `l` misses all nonidentity fixed subspaces in `V*`; choose a chamber with interior by maximizing at a generic interior point of the original lifted cone. The strict separation and trivial stabilizer follow from uniqueness of the maximum there.

### 3. Sanity checks and the first cases

* For relative dimension zero the morphism is birational, so there is no nontrivial birational automorphism over `S`. For a small contraction every divisor is relatively movable, because the morphism is an isomorphism in codimension one, and both signs become effective after adding the pullback of a sufficiently ample base divisor. Thus `C=V`, a rational polyhedral cone. For a general crepant birational morphism it is of Fano type over `S`: for a fixed relatively ample Cartier `A`, `-A` is relatively big, so choose an effective `B~-A/S` (after a positive multiple) and small `epsilon` with `(X,Delta+epsilon B)` klt. Its anticanonical log divisor is relatively ample. The relative Mori dream space conclusion gives a rational polyhedral movable cone. Alternatively here `U=V`, so §2 applies to the zero-dimensional quotient and relative good models in dimension zero.
* In generic irregularity zero, `V/U = N^1(X_eta)_R` without assuming a Q-factorial base. Indeed any generic numerically trivial Cartier divisor is torsion when `Pic^0(X_bareta)=0`, and after a multiple it is generically principal (descent of the trivial line bundle by faithful flatness and Hilbert 90, or constancy of the dimension of cohomology). Its difference from a principal divisor on `X` is vertical. Conversely `U` restricts to zero. Actual relative effective movable classes restrict to effective nef classes in generic dimension at most two. Effective generic semiample Cartier divisors have movable horizontal lifts: close the terms of a general effective member, and use finitely many linearly equivalent generic members with empty divisorial base locus to remove divisorial base components over `S`. This is the argument in Li II, proof of Theorem 1.3, step 1. Subject to the required generic pair group and cone theorem, the quotient lifting supplies the strong domain, even for a non-Q-factorial base. For K3 fibres the finite-kernel argument also follows directly from Li–Zhao Theorem 1.4 and their lifted weak domain: its kernel fixes the restricted ample class, hence injects at the birational level into the finite numerically polarized group of the geometric K3 surface.
* For generic curves of genus one, `p(C)` is the degree-positive open half-space with its vertex adjoined. Effective divisors of generic degree zero are vertical. Conversely every rational positive-degree class has a horizontal movable representative modulo `U`, by generic ampleness and the horizontal-closure argument. The real assertion follows by expressing any interior degree-positive point as the convex combination of rational positive-degree points. Translations by the Jacobian act on the degree-one affine slice of `V/U`, through a full translation lattice whenever the generic translations extend to pseudoautomorphisms up to finite index. For an integral class of degree `m`, translation by `t` changes it in generic `Pic^0` by `±mt`. Integral classes of degree zero generate the vector differences, giving full rank after a common multiple. The remaining origin-preserving automorphism group of an elliptic curve is finite. Hence a generic rational point and an invariant rational Euclidean metric on the slice give a compact rational Dirichlet polytope there. Taking the cone on this cell, then §2, gives a strong domain. This is already unconditional for boundary-free terminal spaces because every generic birational automorphism is a pseudoautomorphism over `S`.

### 4. Primary evidence and conventions

* Z. Li and H. Zhao, *On the relative Morrison–Kawamata cone conjecture*, arXiv:2206.13701v8, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.13701. Section 2, pp. 3–6 defines fibration, effective cone, pseudoautomorphisms of the support and terminal conventions. Theorem 2.7 (pp. 9–10) supplies finite chamber subdivisions. Definition 3.4 is exactly the requested relative-interior separation and trivial stabilizer. Their Theorem 1.4 asserts a weak effective domain for K3 and a strong result under nondegeneracy. Their Remark 5.4 gives the necessary boundary distinction.
* Z. Li, *On the relative Morrison–Kawamata cone conjecture (II)*, arXiv:2309.04673v1, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.04673. Definition of terminal pairs in §2.2 allows effective boundary and tests exceptional discrepancies. Theorem 1.3 concerns rational movable points, and Theorem 1.8 and Corollary 1.9 concern the rational hull. Neither is an immediate proof of the target. Section 4, Theorem 4.1 summarizes Xu's description of the abelian identity component and the Albanese product modulo a finite group. Lemma 4.4 and Theorem 4.9 describe polarization homomorphisms. Remark 5.2 and Example 5.3 explicitly distinguish rational and irrational parameter translation span when only general linear maps are used.
* Y. Kawamata, *On the cone of divisors of Calabi–Yau fiber spaces*, https://arxiv.org/pdf/alg-geom/9701006, introduction, Definition 1.1, Lemma 1.5, and Example 3.8. The main theorem is expressly the finiteness part in total dimension three, not a general strong tiling theorem.
