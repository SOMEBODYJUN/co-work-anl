# Candidate proof of RMKC-STRONG-REL2, revision 2

**Review status.** This is a proposed complete mathematical argument, assembled for independent proof review. Its mathematical dependencies are identified in Section 7. The accompanying [source and revision audit](source_audit_v2.md) records the original-text checks and the response to the first independent review. In particular the primary references do not state the full result asserted here.

**Proposed theorem.** Let f:(X,Δ)→S be a complex terminal Calabi–Yau fibre space in the sense of Li–Zhao, with normal projective Q-factorial X, K_X+Δ numerically trivial over S and 0≤dim(X/S)≤2. In V=N¹(X/S)_R let C be the intersection of the closed relative movable cone with the relative effective cone and let Γ be the image of the relative support-preserving pseudo-automorphisms in GL(V). There is a rational polyhedral cone Π⊂C such that ΓΠ=C, every nonidentity translate γΠ is disjoint from the relative interior of Π, and the setwise stabilizer of Π in Γ is trivial. This quantifies over all total dimensions, all allowed bases and all lineality of the closed movable cone.

## 1. Objects, small dimensions and relative geography

Write V for the real Cartier numerical group for all complete curves contracted by f, with its usual rational structure. Write M for the cone generated by relatively movable Cartier divisors, E for the cone of relatively effective divisors, and C for the intersection of the closure of M with E. Write Γ for the faithful linear image of the pseudo-automorphisms over S preserving the support of Δ. Precomposition with the inversion map permits computations with pullback instead of pushforward; all sets of linear operators or translates below are the same. The base S is normal quasi-projective. With X projective and f surjective it is proper and hence projective. No divisor on S is assumed Q-Cartier unless specifically chosen to be a Cartier divisor.

For a terminal fibre space it is the pair (X,Δ) which is terminal in the terminology of Li–Zhao §2.2; see also Li II §2.2, which specifies Δ effective and log discrepancy >1 at all exceptional divisorial valuations. In dimension at least two the coefficients of Δ are <1: at the general smooth locus of any chosen component blow up a codimension-two smooth centre contained in it. This gives log discrepancy 2 minus that coefficient. All remaining log discrepancies are positive, so the pair is klt. Absolute dimension zero gives V=0. In absolute dimension one, a morphism to a point has the single nonnegative degree ray with trivial linear image, and a nonconstant contraction of normal curves is an isomorphism. These cases also cover any low-dimensional convention about terminal boundary coefficients. From now on we use klt.

Fix the finitely many prime divisors supporting Δ. Relative numerical triviality of K_X plus a boundary supported on them is a rational affine system in its coefficients. Indeed all these divisors are Q-Cartier, and the image of their span in the finite-dimensional numerical space is rational. Terminality is open under small perturbations on this fixed support (the discrepancy criterion on a fixed log resolution, Kollár–Mori Cor. 2.35). Choose rational points of this affine system in a small neighborhood whose convex hull contains Δ, all positive on its support and terminal. For each resulting boundary Δ' the rational relative numerical-triviality theorem of Hacon–Xu gives K_X+Δ' relatively Q-linearly trivial (Cor. 1.4 in Li–Zhao's published numbering, Cor. 1.6 of arXiv:1107.4149v2). Taking convex combinations gives relative R-linear triviality of K_X+Δ. Fix one such Δ' for the proof below.

The relative good-model and geography results of Li–Zhao apply because good minimal models exist for effective klt pairs of dimension at most two. For closed surface fibres one may use Fujino, *Minimal model theory for log surfaces*, Thm. 1.1, also for real boundaries. In the real-coefficient good-model statement we use Li–Zhao Thm. 2.4 with its explanation following the statement. Hacon–Xu Thm. 2.12, from which it is derived, directly assumes a rational boundary and finite generation. The cited real version uses the existence of a relative log canonical model for an effective klt pair. In this proof the perturbed adjoint is relatively R-linearly equivalent to an effective divisor. The following consequences are needed.

* M=C. For an effective representative D of a class in the closed movable cone, take the good model of (X,Δ+εD) over S, ε>0 small. Its existence follows from generic low-dimensional abundance and Li–Zhao Thm. 2.4; this includes vertical D with generically zero restriction. Lem. 2.3 allows a weak log canonical model X' which is a small Q-factorial modification. Write Δ_{X'} and D_{X'} for the strict transforms. The corresponding log adjoint is relatively R-semiample on X', since the pullback by the morphism supplied by Lem. 2.3 of the adjoint on the original good model is semiample. Strict transformation of the original relative principal relation gives K_{X'}+Δ_{X'} relatively R-linearly trivial. Thus D_{X'} is relatively R-semiample. A relatively R-semiample divisor is a positive real combination, up to relative real linear equivalence, of semiample rational classes; their transforms are relatively movable. Thus [D] belongs to M. The reverse inclusion follows at once from the definitions. This is equality with the effective cone intersection only; no equality with the rational hull is used.
* For each rational polyhedral cone P contained in E, the intersection P∩C is rational polyhedral. Here is the direct geography argument, which also proves the required instance of Li II Lem. 2.5(2). Li–Zhao Thm. 2.7 decomposes a pointed P into finitely many relatively open rational polyhedral cells on which effective small klt perturbations share their marked weak log canonical models. In any such cell meeting C take one [D]∈C and the small marked model for D from the preceding paragraph. For a small marking the unperturbed adjoints have the same pullback on a common resolution by their common relative R-principal relation with compatible canonical divisors. The discrepancy differences for the perturbation of D therefore scale linearly with ε>0, so the marked model stays weak log canonical when ε decreases. For every effective B in the same cell, take a common perturbation small enough for D and B. The same marking makes B nef, since its transformed log Calabi–Yau summand is relatively R-linearly trivial. A relatively nef class on the marked model is a limit of relatively ample classes, and their transforms on X are movable. Hence [B] is in the closed movable cone and, as [B]∈P⊂E, it belongs to C. The closure of this entire cell still lies in the closed movable cone and in P⊂E. Thus P∩C is precisely the finite union of the rational polyhedral closures of the cells meeting it. Convexity makes this union their Minkowski sum. For a P containing lines, intersect P with the finitely many coordinate orthants of any rational basis of V. This writes P as a finite union of pointed rational polyhedral cones. Apply the two results to each piece, where the convex hull of a suitably normalized finite set of nonzero ray generators avoids the origin, as required in the proof of Li–Zhao Thm. 2.7. A zero piece needs no decomposition. The finite union of all the resulting P_i∩C equals the convex P∩C, hence equals their Minkowski sum and is rational polyhedral. Finiteness of the marked models used below follows by taking the union of the finite lists of cells on the pointed pieces.

Let T⊂V be the real span of the classes of all vertical prime divisors, and p:V→L=V/T. This is a rational subspace. For a vertical prime D, an effective Cartier divisor on S containing its image has pullback containing D. Thus a multiple of that pullback minus D is effective. In particular T⊂E, with both signs. The action on T has finite image: choose finitely many vertical primes whose classes span it. The transforms of any one of these primes are among the finitely many divisorial components of the fixed closed subset f⁻¹(f(D)), since f is proper and all maps act over the identity of S.

**Finite vertical kernel.** The subgroup K=ker(Γ→GL(L)) is finite. Pass to a finite-index subgroup Kᵥ fixing T pointwise that is induced by coefficient-preserving pseudo-automorphisms. Such a passage is permitted since the original support is finite. If A is an effective relatively ample Cartier class, every gA for g∈Kᵥ belongs to the rational polyhedral cone Q=R_{≥0}A+T⊂E. The marked nef cones gNef(X/S) occurring here are finite in number. Indeed, use the finite geography on Q above. A marked small modification taking gA to an ample divisor is a weak log canonical model of the pair perturbed in the effective direction gA. To see this, on a common resolution the transform of the original log divisor is crepant, since the strict transform preserves the boundary and its relative R-principal relation. The difference between pullbacks of the perturbing divisor and of its relatively ample transform is exceptional for both projections and is nonnegative by the negativity lemma, applied over the original model. If gA and hA belong to the same cell, choose a common positive perturbation small enough for both. The same marked model then takes hA to a nef class. Thus hA, an interior point of hNef(X/S), belongs to gNef(X/S). Both cones are full dimensional, and distinct marked ample chambers have disjoint interiors (reconstruction by relative Proj from an ample divisor, as in Kawamata Lem. 1.5). Taking interior points of the latter cone near hA shows that the two cones coincide. A relative numerical ample class has an effective representative because the base is quasi-projective; we chose A in this way from the start.

Consequently the subgroup K₁ of Kᵥ stabilizing Nef(X/S) has finite index. For g∈K₁, (g−I)²=0. Every relatively ample class A' satisfies gⁿA'=A'+n(g−I)A' in the closed relative nef cone for all integers n. Its closure thus contains both signs of (g−I)A'. The nef cone is pointed by the very definition of numerical equivalence, so (g−I)A'=0 for every A'. Ample classes span V. This proves K₁={I} and the lemma.

**Lifting a quotient chamber.** Suppose the faithful image G of Γ on L has a strong rational polyhedral fundamental domain P on p(C). Every rational generator of P has a rational effective lift. Indeed, choose any effective real lift in C, express it in finitely many integral effective divisor classes, and solve for nonnegative rational coefficients giving the specified rational image under p. A nonempty rational polyhedron has a rational point. It follows that p⁻¹(P) is generated by such effective lifts together with T, hence is rational polyhedral and lies in E. Therefore D=C∩p⁻¹(P) is rational polyhedral. Its Γ-translates cover C. It is full dimensional since Γ is countable and C contains the relatively ample open cone. Surjectivity gives p(D)=P and openness of p gives p(Int D)⊂Int P. By the strong separation for P, a translate of D meeting Int D must project to the identity in G. The whole setwise stabilizer is exactly K, finite by the preceding lemma. Refine any full-dimensional weak cone D with finite stabilizer K by choosing a rational form l on V whose stabilizer in K is trivial, and imposing l(x)≥l(kx) for every k∈K. Its K-translates cover D since the maximum over K is attained; countability/full dimension implies the refinement has interior. On the interior every inequality indexed by k≠1 is strict because its functional is nonzero. Comparing that inequality with the one for the inverse proves kD' avoids Int D' when k≠1. Elements outside K already avoid Int D. This proves Definition 3.4 including the trivial setwise stabilizer.

If the relative dimension is zero, f is birational (a generically finite normal contraction in characteristic zero is generically degree one), G and Γ are trivial, and T=V since every divisor is vertical. The preceding intersection result with P=V⊂E gives C rational polyhedral. This also deals with non-small crepant morphisms. We assume relative dimension 1 or 2 from now on.

## 2. The generic quotient and its numerical base chamber

Let k=C(S), F=X_η, R=N¹(F)_Q, and write rational spaces below before extending scalars. Since Δ is effective, removing it can only increase exceptional discrepancies, so X itself is terminal. Clemens–Kollár–Mori, *Higher dimensional complex geometry*, Cor. 6.13, gives codimension at least three for its singular locus; no component of that locus dominates S when the relative dimension is at most two. Generic smoothness of the smooth open part gives F geometrically smooth. Normality and geometric connectedness of the generic fibre also follow from Li–Zhao Prop. 4.1. Restriction yields a geometrically klt Calabi–Yau pair on F. Generic pseudo-automorphisms of this smooth curve or surface are regular automorphisms, since a birational map of smooth curves or smooth surfaces that contracts and extracts no prime divisor is an isomorphism. Restricting a relative pseudo-automorphism and its inverse preserves the codimension-one isomorphism at the generic fibre. Conversely we do not initially need every generic automorphism to extend.

Restriction of divisors gives a surjection Pic(X)_Q→Pic(F)_Q by horizontal closure and Q-factoriality. Its kernel consists of classes with a vertical representative, because a rational function on F is one on X. Let K₀ denote the subspace of Pic(F)_Q coming from f-numerically trivial rational divisors. Such classes are numerically trivial on F by Li–Zhao Prop. 4.3 and lie in Pic⁰(F)(k)_Q: geometric numerical and algebraic equivalence of line bundles agree modulo torsion. Points of the Picard scheme versus actual line bundles defined over k differ by a torsion Brauer obstruction; allowing rational coefficients removes it. Thus
$$
 L_{\mathbb Q}=\operatorname{Pic}(F)_{\mathbb Q}/K_0,\qquad
 U_{\mathbb Q}=\operatorname{Pic}^0(F)(k)_{\mathbb Q}/K_0,\qquad
 0\longrightarrow U_{\mathbb Q}\longrightarrow L_{\mathbb Q}
 \stackrel{\pi}{\longrightarrow}R\longrightarrow0.
$$
In particular U is finite dimensional independently of the size of rational points of the abelian variety Pic⁰(F). Here and below a real Pic class denotes an element after tensoring over Q with R.

**The actual projected cone.** Put D_gen=Nef(F)∩Eff(F), where Nef(F) denotes the closed nef cone. The cone p(C) consists precisely of the images in L of effective real Pic classes on F with nef numerical class. To prove the forward direction, restriction of a relatively movable linear system has no fixed divisor on F; it is nef on a surface because for each given curve one may avoid it in a representative. A relatively effective Cartier divisor means one relatively R-linearly equivalent to an effective divisor, so its numerical representative restricts to a generic effective class, and nefness persists under real limits. For the converse, on a surface express an effective representative as a nonnegative real combination of finitely many prime curves. Nefness on this support is given by the finitely many rational intersection inequalities against the support curves (or their finitely many geometric components) and nonnegativity of the coefficients. Every other geometric curve has nonnegative intersection with each component. Thus it is a nonnegative sum of rational effective nef divisors on the same support by rational polyhedrality, imposing Galois-invariant coefficient relations if using geometric components. On curves this is immediate. Each rational summand is semiample by low-dimensional klt abundance applied to a small effective perturbation of a rational log Calabi–Yau boundary with the same support. The rational boundary Δ' of Section 1 restricts to such a boundary, so rational ε>0 gives K_F+Δ'_F+εD rationally linearly equivalent to εD. Surface abundance applies after geometric scalar extension and, on curves, the corresponding statement follows from degree or the effective degree-zero case. Semiampleness of a multiple descends to k by base change of global sections.

For a semiample generic Cartier class choose an effective multiple H and close its horizontal support to an effective Q-Cartier divisor D on X, with the original multiplicities. D has no vertical component. To avoid a putative horizontal fixed prime, choose H' in the same generic linear system as H avoiding that prime, with horizontal closure D'. Then D−D'−div(α) is vertical for a rational function α. An effective Cartier divisor on S whose pullback dominates the negative part makes D relatively linearly equivalent to D' plus an effective vertical divisor. After a Cartier multiple this yields a section in the relative system avoiding that prime. The original D avoids every vertical prime. Hence D is movable over S. This is Li II proof of Thm. 1.3, Step 1 and works for arbitrary base and total dimension. Multiples and real sums yield the assertion. In particular πp(C)=D_gen.

**Finite-index generic lifting.** Let J be the group of k-automorphisms of F preserving the support of Δ_F. Take the subgroup J' fixing all of its finitely many geometric prime components. This subgroup has finite index in J; it also fixes the coefficients of Δ'_F. Clearing denominators in the relative Q-linear triviality from Section 1 gives an integer r>0, a Cartier divisor D_S on S and a rational r-pluricanonical form Φ on X satisfying
$$
 \operatorname{div}_X(\Phi)+r\Delta'=f^*D_S .
$$
If relative triviality is defined locally on S, choose a common multiple over a finite affine cover. The nowhere vanishing local generators then glue to the pullback of a line bundle on S because f_*O_X=O_S. No Weil divisor of S is used as a Cartier divisor without this construction.

After dividing by the rth power of a fixed rational top form of k/C, Φ determines the generator of the one-dimensional space H⁰(F,ω_F^r(rΔ'_F)). Every g∈J' is a regular automorphism of the klt pair (F,Δ'_F), hence is log crepant and acts on this space by a scalar λ(g)∈k*. Apply Fujino–Gongyo, *Log pluricanonical representations and the abundance conjecture*, Thm. 1.1 (also their klt Cor. 3.10) to the geometric pair: it is projective klt with r(K_F+Δ'_F) Cartier and trivial. One may identify the algebraic closure of k=C(S) abstractly with C, since both have characteristic zero and the same transcendence degree over Q, and the space of sections commutes with this scalar extension. Its geometric log pluricanonical representation has finite image, so λ(g) is a root of unity. All roots of unity in k lie in its original constant field C. Since g fixes k, passage back to the absolute form gives g*Φ=λ(g)Φ. Every divisorial valuation v of C(X) over X is trivial on λ(g). Its discrepancy is
$$
 a(v;X,\Delta')=1+\big(\operatorname{ord}_v\Phi-
                    \operatorname{ord}_v f^*D_S\big)/r ,
$$
computed by pulling back to a smooth model carrying v. The second order in this formula is invariant because the induced birational map is over S. Therefore g preserves all discrepancies. For the effective terminal pair (X,Δ'), the valuations with a≤1 are precisely the prime divisors on X. It follows that g and its inverse contract no divisor and extend to a pseudo-automorphism of X/S. On these prime divisors discrepancy recovers the coefficient of Δ' as 1−a, so the extension preserves the support of Δ, including the vertical components. This proves that the actual generic subgroup contains J'. In particular, every k-point in the neutral support-preserving automorphism group extends, since a geometrically connected group fixes each geometric boundary component. Only a finite-index assertion for J is used.

**A strong base chamber for the actual group.** Moraga–Stark Thm. 3.8 over a characteristic-zero field supplies a rational polyhedral cone B₀⊂D_gen whose translates cover D_gen under the coefficient-preserving generic group in dimension two. In dimension one D_gen is the invariant degree ray. The larger group J also preserves D_gen, and hence J·B₀=D_gen. Choose finitely many coset representatives with J equal to the union of J' g_i, using the action on divisors (or the opposite group for the pullback convention). The cone generated by the g_iB₀ is rational polyhedral and contained in the convex D_gen. Its translates under J' already cover D_gen, and therefore so do its translates under the actual numerical image Γ_R. This group preserves the generic Néron–Severi lattice because generic pseudo-automorphisms are regular. The closed nef cone is pointed and D_gen contains the ample interior. Li–Zhao Prop. 3.3 gives D_gen=Nef(F)⁺, the convex hull of the rational closed nef points. Their Lem. 3.5, applied to the faithful group Γ_R, gives a strong rational polyhedral domain B⊂D_gen in the sense of Definition 3.4. This argument uses only the generic cone theorem, finite-index lifting and the nondegenerate convex theorem. It supplies the actual group on generic numerical classes directly.

If q=h¹(F,O_F)=0, U=0. Thus p(C)=D_gen has the required strong chamber; Section 1 completes this case. In the remaining cases assume U≠0, as U=0 is settled by the same argument independently of q. By Li II Thm. 4.1 and its descent in §4, the neutral support-preserving automorphism group A of F is an abelian variety of dimension q. Its action on the Albanese torsor induces a k-homomorphism A→Alb(F), since the geometric identity component of the automorphisms of that torsor consists of translations. Over the geometric closure this is the isogeny in Xu Thm. 4.5(2), hence it is an isogeny over k. The group preserving the reduced support has the same neutral component when the coefficients on that support are changed. Its orbits have dimension q (the geometric presentation is a finite quotient of A times the Albanese fibre). It follows q≤dim F≤2. If q=2, F is an abelian surface torsor: the smooth connected Albanese fibre is zero dimensional, or equivalently after algebraic closure the proper connected orbit has full dimension and a finite stabilizer; after quotienting by this stabilizer its underlying variety is an abelian surface. The full neutral automorphism group of an abelian surface is its translation group, which acts simply transitively. The effective horizontal boundary must be zero in this case, because its class equals −K_F=0 geometrically and an effective nonzero divisor on a projective surface has positive intersection with an ample class. Descent makes F a torsor for the abelian translation group A/k.

## 3. Actual translations and the group acting above a base point

Fix an integral relatively ample H_X on X with generic numerical class H. Section 2 gives the extension of every b∈A(k), including preservation of vertical boundary. For this neutral subgroup its action on the one-dimensional generic log pluricanonical space can also be seen directly to be trivial, since an abelian variety has no nontrivial algebraic character to the multiplicative group.

Write G for the faithful image on L of the actual Γ and G₀ for its kernel on R. An operator g below is the pullback φ* of a geometric map φ; the multiplication in G is composition of the operators, so φ*ψ*=(ψ∘φ)*. Equivalently this is the original pushforward representation after indexing φ by its inverse. Thus the operator denoted t_b^* is the pullback of translation by b and is the original pushforward by translation by −b. The preimage of G₀ in actual generic automorphisms preserves the numerical class of H. The subgroup scheme of support-preserving automorphisms preserving an ample numerical class is of finite type (bounded graphs with fixed Hilbert polynomial, cf. Li–Zhao Lem. 4.8), with identity component A. Thus its k-points modulo their intersection with A(k) form a finite group. This remains true for the actual subgroup and its image. The image of A(k) is therefore a normal finite-index subgroup of G₀.

For a line bundle M on F and b∈A(k), set λ_M(b)=t_b^*M−M in Pic⁰(F). The square theorem for abelian varieties (and restriction to orbits when q=1) shows that it depends only on the numerical class d of M, is additive in d, and is a homomorphism in b. For the ample H it is an isogeny A→Pic⁰(F), either by the usual polarization in the torsor case or by the orbit restriction computed below. Identify U with its description through λ_H. The actual translation parameters
$$
 \Lambda=\{[\lambda_H(b)]\in U\mid b\in A(k)\}
$$
form a full lattice in U. Their Q-span is all U because λ_H is an isogeny, surjective after tensoring k-points with Q: lift a rational point in a finite separable extension and sum its conjugates. Alternatively a quasi-inverse isogeny gives the same conclusion. They lie in a discrete lattice since a translation shifts H_X by an integral class of the Γ-preserved numerical lattice, and one projects along the rational subspace T. It suffices that the action preserve some full lattice (Li–Zhao §2.1); if needed multiply H_X by a common denominator to put it in that lattice. For a direct lattice proof, the image of integral Weil divisors in V is Γ-invariant because strict transformation permutes prime divisors. To see it is a lattice, take a projective smooth resolution ρ:Y→X. Every Weil class is pushed forward from a Cartier divisor on Y. For numerically trivial P on Y, the ρ-exceptional rational divisor ρ*ρ_*P−P is ρ-numerically trivial, hence zero by the negativity lemma applied to both signs; thus ρ_*P is numerically trivial, by projection. The image factors through finitely generated NS(Y). It is therefore a finitely generated group of rational numerical classes spanning V, hence a full lattice; p sends it to a lattice because T is rational. The two descriptions of λ_d below prove that a translation with parameter z acts by x↦x+T_{πx}z with T_H=1. Hence the image translation group in G is exactly this full lattice, no larger kernel being counted as an element of the faithful group. A finite extension of this lattice need not split; no splitting is required.

## 4. Case q=1, including elliptic curves and irregular nonabelian surface fibres

Here A is an elliptic curve. For a geometric point x of F let o_x:A→F be its orbit map. Pullback takes Pic⁰(F) to A^∨ by an isogeny j independent of x: the induced map on Albanese varieties is the fixed homomorphism A→Alb(F), because the action on the Albanese torsor is translation by its image. Its dual is j. The integer a(d)=deg o_x^*M is independent of x, since the orbit maps form a family over connected F and degree is constant in a family of line bundles on A. It is a rational linear functional on R, nonnegative on nef classes and strictly positive at H because the orbit map is nonconstant and H ample. If P₁=O_A(0), the identity
$$
 j\big(t_b^*M-M\big)=t_b^*o_x^*M-o_x^*M
                    =a(d)\lambda_{P_1}(b)
$$
follows from o_x∘t_b=t_b∘o_x and the identification of elliptic NS with the degree group. As an equality of homomorphisms on the connected abelian group A it determines λ_M (the finite kernel of j introduces no ambiguity); on Q-vector spaces one can directly invert j. In particular λ_H is an isogeny and on U one has
$$
 T_d=\frac{a(d)}{a(H)}\,I,\qquad [\lambda_d(b)]=T_d[\lambda_H(b)].
$$
Use any rational positive definite scalar product on U.

Here is a rational linear section s:R→L with the correct fibre on all effective faces, including those where the scalar vanishes. Choose x in F over a finite Galois extension. Average the pullbacks o_{σx}^* over its Galois orbit, obtaining a map r:Pic(F)_Q→Pic(A)_Q defined over k (take norms, then divide by the degree). Its restriction on the rational Pic⁰ groups is j. Set
$$
 w_0(M)=j^{-1}\big(r(M)-a([M])P_1\big).
$$
This projection to Pic⁰(F)(k)_Q is the identity on Pic⁰(F)(k)_Q and determines a rational section s before and after quotienting by K₀. Suppose M is effective nef with a([M])=0. For rational M some multiple is globally generated by Section 2. Its restriction to every o_x is then globally generated and of degree zero, hence trivial. It follows that w₀(M)=0. For real M the decomposition in Section 2 expresses it as a nonnegative combination of rational effective nef classes, each of which has zero orbit degree if their sum does. This proves the same assertion for real classes and in particular proves that each such d has the unique effective-nef lift s(d).

For rational effective nef d with a(d)>0, take a rational effective nef lift L_d. Its actual translates are still effective nef, and in the quotient they differ from L_d by T_dΛ. The convex hull of a full lattice in a real vector space is the entire space, as a real fundamental parallelepiped and all of its translates are the convex hulls of their vertices. Thus the entire fibre s(d)+U occurs. For real d one can either use its positive rational-generator decomposition or the general Minkowski argument in Section 6.

## 5. Case q=2, an abelian surface torsor

Write A^∨=Pic⁰(F); use the ample polarization h=λ_H:A→A^∨. For each generic numerical class d put α_d=h⁻¹λ_d∈End_k⁰(A) (extend linearly in real d). A rational line bundle on the torsor defines this symmetric homomorphism in the same way as on A; translation of a chosen geometric origin has no effect on λ_d. The symmetry is taken with respect to the Rosati involution for h. For a geometric affine automorphism φ with linear part u, pullback on Pic⁰ is u^∨; via h this is u†=h⁻¹u^∨h on the parameter module. If this operator is in G₀, then u^∨hu=h and u^∨λ_du=λ_d for each d, so u†=u⁻¹ and u† commutes with α_d. This is consistent with conjugation of translation pullbacks, which changes b to u⁻¹b. We always use the ordinary left action of End_k⁰(A) on A(k)_Q; dualization itself reverses the order and is not identified with that left action. To let α_d act on the visible quotient, identify U with the quotient of A(k)_Q by h⁻¹K₀ and prove endomorphism invariance as follows.

The class of the torsor F has finite period m, since it has a point over a finite separable extension and the restriction-corestriction composite is the degree. For a∈End_k(A), the endomorphism u=1+mn a fixes this torsor class for every integer n. Take a nonzero n making u an isogeny: the determinant polynomial on a faithful rational Tate representation is nonzero at zero. Since u_*[F]=[F], contracting the torsor along u gives a u-equivariant k-morphism F→F. It acts as u^∨ on Pic⁰, independently of the translation used to identify torsors. Extend the generic morphism as an S-rational map of X. Resolve it as p':Z→X birational and q':Z→X a morphism. For any rational divisor D≡_S0, q'^*D≡_S0. The divisor p'^*p'_*q'^*D−q'^*D is p'-exceptional and p'-numerically trivial, hence vanishes by the negativity lemma applied to both signs. Consequently p'_*q'^*D≡_S0 (lift any contracted curve by a generically finite curve on the resolution). Its restriction to F represents the generic pullback because the initial generic map is a morphism. Vertical divisors are sent to vertical divisors in this computation. This proves u^∨ preserves K₀, and subtraction and division by mn show a^∨ preserves it. Duality (or isogeny via h) gives stability of h⁻¹K₀ under the entire rational endomorphism algebra E_A=End_k⁰(A). In particular α_d induces an operator T_d on U, T_H=I, rational linear in d. By construction [λ_d(b)]=T_d[λ_H(b)] in the identification by h.

For completeness, there is a rational positive definite scalar product on U making all T_d for nef d self-adjoint and positive semidefinite. The algebra E_A is semisimple by Poincaré complete reducibility over k. The finite-dimensional rational E_A-module U embeds in the regular left module E_A^n: a finite generating set gives a surjection and semisimplicity gives a splitting. Choose on E_A the form (v,w)=Tr(vw^†), where Tr is the ordinary rational trace on a faithful homology representation of A after an embedding into C. Equivalently one can take the rational endomorphism trace of the abelian variety, as in Milne, Abelian Varieties, Thm. 17.3. This form is positive definite and the adjoint of left multiplication by a is left multiplication by a†, by cyclicity of trace. Restrict the sum form on E_A^n to U. For rational nef d, on the tangent representation of the complex abelian variety the Hermitian form for H is positive definite and the form for d semipositive (nef classes are limits of ample ones). The operator α_d compares the two forms and has only nonnegative real eigenvalues. The exponential uniformization presents the underlying real tangent space of A(C) as H₁(A(C),Q)⊗R, equivariantly for every endomorphism. Indeed its period lattice is H₁(A(C),Z), a full lattice in the real tangent space. Hence the eigenvalues of α_d on complexified rational homology consist of its complex tangent eigenvalues and their conjugates, all nonnegative real numbers. Homology is faithful as a rational representation of E_A, so the rational minimal polynomial on homology annihilates α_d in E_A itself and hence on every rational E_A-module, including U. Self-adjointness on U now gives positive semidefiniteness. For ample d, the endomorphism is invertible and positive definite. One may embed the finitely generated field supporting the abelian variety, polarizations, and chosen endomorphism basis into C for this argument, or fix a field isomorphism from the algebraic closure of C(S) to C; no embedding fixing the original copy of C is required. The real statement on positive combinations of effective nef classes follows by linearity.

We next supply an initial rational section with the exact effective fibres. In a finite Galois splitting field choose a point p of F. Let i_p be reflection x↦2p−x. On geometric Picard groups the rational class s_p(d)=(M+i_p^*M)/2 is independent of the lift M of d, because i_p^* acts as −1 on Pic⁰ and as +1 on NS. Average over the conjugates of p and use the norm to obtain a rational section s(d) in Pic(F)_Q, and then in L. This construction suffices; it does not require an integral origin or a splitting of the automorphism group. If d has an effective rational lift M, the averaged lift s(d) is rationally effective: take effective representatives, reflect them after splitting, sum all conjugates and descend the resulting divisor.

For an effective rational line bundle M on F all its translates are nef. Work over the algebraic closure with any chosen origin and put N=ker(λ_d)^0. The annihilator of N inside A^∨ equals im λ_d. Indeed restriction i_N^∨∘λ_d=(λ_d∘i_N)^∨=0 by symmetry, and both connected subvarieties have dimension dim A−dim N by the dual exact sequence. In particular translating M does not change its restriction to N up to isomorphism after identifying the coset with N. Such restrictions have numerical class zero, since the polarization homomorphism of the restriction vanishes. On a general coset a nonzero section does not vanish identically; it restricts to a nonzero section of a numerically trivial line bundle on the abelian variety N, which must be trivial. It follows that M has trivial restriction on every coset under the translational identifications. This also holds for its reflected conjugates, hence for the rational section s(d) on these cosets. Therefore M−s(d) is a rational Pic⁰ point annihilating N, i.e. it lies in im λ_d on geometric points. Its class lies in λ_d(A(k)_Q): multiply to remove denominators, take a preimage over a finite extension and take the sum of its Galois conjugates. This proves that any actual rational effective lift differs from s(d) by an element of im T_d in U. This implication still applies to real effective nef combinations as in Section 6.

Conversely for rational nef effective d choose a rational effective lift as above. Since its relative translations form the coset T_dΛ, the convex hull of these effective lifts is s(d)+im T_d. Conv(T_dΛ)=im T_d follows by applying the linear map T_d to Conv(Λ)=U. No discreteness of the image is necessary for this argument.

## 6. Real fibres, the rational polyhedron, and the strong condition

In both cases the following hold for every real d in the generic domain B and indeed throughout D_gen:
$$
 (p(C))\cap\pi^{-1}(d)=s(d)+\operatorname{im}T_d .
$$
For the forward inclusion, Section 2 represents any actual lift as a finite nonnegative combination of rational effective nef bundles M_i whose numerical images d_i may lie anywhere in D_gen. Their differences from s(d_i) are in im T_{d_i} by Sections 4 and 5 (automatic when the scalar is positive in Section 4). For positive coefficients t_i the radical of Σ t_i T_{d_i} is the intersection of their radicals, since the forms are positive semidefinite. Taking orthogonal complements gives im T_d=Σ im T_{d_i}. For the reverse inclusion over a rational generator of B the translated effective lifts and convexity suffice by the preceding calculations; for real d in B take its nonnegative ray-generator expansion and add these full affine fibres. To see the same statement on D_gen one can instead use any effective rational-nef decomposition. At d=0 the permitted fibre is precisely the zero vector.

We apply the abstract operator-domain theorem proved in full in the companion file [operator_domain_v1.md](../informed-a/operator_domain_v1.md). Its hypotheses are verified here:

1. The exact rational sequence in Section 2 has finite dimension, G acts faithfully on L through the Γ-image, and it preserves p(C). It is countable and p(C) contains the image of the relatively ample open cone. The strong nondegenerate chamber on R is B.
2. There is a rational linear section from Section 4 or 5. The exact real fibre equality holds, including zeros and all boundary faces. The normalized operators are rational linear with T_H=I; they are self-adjoint positive semidefinite on B in a rational positive definite scalar product. The sum of the ray generators of the full-dimensional B lies in its interior, hence in the ample cone of F. Its operator is positive definite. Here the ample cone is the interior of the closed nef cone.
3. The actual image of A(k) in G₀ is the normal finite-index subgroup identified with the full lattice Λ by Section 3 and the identities [λ_d(b)]=T_d[λ_H(b)]. The possible finite linear holonomy and affine displacements are handled by the theorem. In particular one does not replace G₀ by an arbitrarily selected translation lattice or assume that an extension by the finite point group splits.

To describe the construction and exact separation, average the rational section modulo the translations d↦T_dz over the finite quotient G₀/Λ and use an invariant rational positive scalar product. This yields a section s' with the same real fibres. The action of g∈G₀ on a lift s'(d)+w is s'(d)+A_gw+T_dn_g, where A_g is orthogonal, commutes with T_d and n_g∈U_Q; the offsets form a finite union S of Λ-cosets containing Λ. The closed cone over B is cut out by
$$
 2\langle m,w\rangle\ \le\ \langle m,T_dm\rangle \quad (m\in S).
$$
There are only finitely many needed inequalities, by the congruence-class and Dickson-lemma argument in the companion theorem (valid for rational semidefinite ray forms). They force w perpendicular to ker T_d on every face because ker T_d is the rational intersection of the ray radicals in that face. Thus the inequalities add no ineffective boundary directions. Nearest lattice-coset centres in U/ker T_d give coverage, including d on faces. A nonzero offset yields strict separation against the whole translated cone by evaluating the inequality for that offset and the inverse element. A nonidentity base action is separated by B. Only the finite zero-offset subgroup can stabilize the cone. The finite-group refinement in Section 1 now gives a strong rational polyhedral fundamental domain P on p(C). Section 1 then produces a rational polyhedral domain Π⊂C on V with ΓΠ=C, γΠ∩Int Π empty for every nonidentity γ, and trivial setwise stabilizer. Its interior is the full-dimensional relative interior used in Definition 3.4.
