Its net-price slopes before and after t=2 are respectively (a+q)/2 and (a-q)/2, so its maximum is B(v)=a+q=W/2. This counterfamily therefore satisfies the particular common-price target being compared.

For the positive decomposition theorem, the needed uniqueness is the entire m-player payoff vector at every component child history. The currently acting player becomes a past player in that child, so uniqueness for future players alone does not fix his continuation payoff. Under the full hypothesis, each joint action has additive coordinate functions F_j(a_j), and a joint maximizer must maximize each coordinate. Independent child subtrees then realize the projected equilibrium outcome; the converse independent-product construction uses no uniqueness hypothesis. The color component in the counterexample violates precisely the past-player part of uniqueness.
