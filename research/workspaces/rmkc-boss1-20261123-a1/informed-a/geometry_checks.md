# Checks on the generic-to-relative argument

These checks supplement journal.md and the frozen operator_domain_v1.md. The geometric statements in this note use the numerical divisor space defined by curves contracted by the fibration.

## Integral structure, low dimensions, and the whole group

**A lattice for pseudoautomorphisms.** For a projective \(\mathbb Q\)-factorial \(X\), the relative numerical image of all integral Weil divisors is a full lattice. Indeed, let \(u:Y\to X\) be a smooth projective resolution. If \(D\) is an integral divisor on \(Y\) numerically trivial on all curves, then \(u^*u_*D-D\) is \(u\)-exceptional and numerically trivial over \(u\). The negativity lemma gives equality with zero as a rational divisor. Every curve of \(X\) is dominated by a curve in \(Y\), so \(u_*D\) has zero relative numerical class. Consequently pushforward gives a rational-valued map from the finitely generated Néron–Severi lattice of \(Y\), surjecting onto the numerical images of all integral Weil divisors. It has full rational span and lies in the specified rational structure. Clearing finitely many denominators makes such a group a full lattice. The numerical pseudoautomorphism group permutes integral Weil divisors and preserves the lattice. This avoids a divisorial Cartier-index assumption.

**Low dimensions.** For \(\dim X=1\), the terminal-pair convention checking only exceptional valuations can be vacuous on a smooth curve. The actual problem is direct: a nonconstant connected-fibre map to a curve is an isomorphism (normal curves, finite degree one), while over a point \(N^1(X)_\mathbb R=\mathbb R\) by degree and the numerical group is trivial. On a curve over a point the effective movable cone is the nonnegative ray. For \(\dim X\ge2\), strict terminality on exceptional valuations with \(\Delta\ge0\) forces each coefficient \(b<1\) by the blow-up of a smooth codimension-two centre in the smooth locus of the corresponding prime and disjoint from other boundary components at its generic point. The exceptional log discrepancy is \(2-b\). Thus the pair is klt. In relative dimension zero the morphism is birational and \(T=V,\Gamma=1\), but \(V\) itself need not vanish. The rationality of \(C_e\) follows by applying the lifting argument of Section B of the journal with quotient cone \(\{0\}\). A zero-dimensional \(X\) is immediate.

**Full kernel of the generic numerical action.** Fix a generic ample class \(H\) over \(K=\mathbb C(S)\). Every element acting trivially on \(N^1(F)_\mathbb Q\) preserves the geometric numerical class of \(H\), since numerical restriction to \(\bar K\) is injective on \(N^1(F)\). In relative dimension at most two the generic pseudoautomorphism is an automorphism. The subgroup scheme of automorphisms preserving the numerical polarization is of finite type; imposing preservation of the boundary is a closed condition (or finitely many components of the support-permutation condition). Its geometric component group is finite. The quotient of its \(K\)-points by the \(K\)-points of its neutral component injects into that finite component group. Therefore the subgroup of allowable generic translations has finite index in the **whole** kernel of the generic numerical action, after passage to the specified numerical image and intersection with liftable automorphisms. The allowable subgroup has finite index in the generic \(B(K)\) by Moraga–Stark, proof of Theorem 5.7 (arXiv:2409.13068, pp. 25–26). Their Theorem 3.8 treats klt surface pairs over characteristic-zero fields, and their Definition 2.5 allows real effective boundaries. For a finite-index subgroup of the numerical generic group the conical hull of finitely many coset-translates of a covering cone is a rational polyhedral covering cone in the true effective nef cone. Pointed reduction (Li–Zhao, Propositions 3.3 and Lemma 3.5) then gives the required strong domain for its exact cone: the covering cone lies in the rational hull, its orbit covers the interior, and Proposition 3.3 equates this orbit with the rational hull.

## The actual Picard quotient and positive operators

Let \(P_F=\operatorname{Pic}(F)\otimes\mathbb Q\) denote actual line bundles modulo linear equivalence, \(P^0_F=\operatorname{Pic}^0(F)(K)\otimes\mathbb Q\), and let \(T\) be the vertical span. Every Weil closure on \(X\) is \(\mathbb Q\)-Cartier, and any two linearly equivalent generic divisors extend to divisors that differ by a principal divisor and vertical primes. Hence the extension homomorphism \(P_F\to N^1(X/S)_\mathbb Q/T_\mathbb Q\) is surjective. Its kernel \(J\) is contained in \(P_F^0\), since numerical restriction to the generic fibre exists. The equality of actual numerically trivial line bundles, after tensoring with \(\mathbb Q\), with \(P_F^0\) uses two basic torsion facts. Néron–Severi torsion is killed by tensoring, and a rational Picard-scheme point acquires a line bundle after a finite extension, whose norm represents a multiple of the point. Accordingly \(L=P_F/J\) is the projected numerical space, while \(P_F^0/J\) measures actual Picard-zero directions still visible on \(X\).

Let \(B=\operatorname{Aut}^0(F,\Delta_F)\) and \(\theta_D:B\to\operatorname{Pic}^0 F\) be \(t\mapsto t^*D-D\). Parametrize the numerical action of a translation by the negative of its group point when using the pushforward convention. Let \(H\) be the generic restriction of the class of an effective, relatively ample Cartier divisor on \(X\). Define \(K_0=\theta_H^{-1}J\subset B(K)\otimes\mathbb Q\). The argument following Section A of the journal makes \(K_0\) invariant under \(A_D=\theta_H^{-1}\theta_D\) for every \(D\in N^1(F)_\mathbb Q\). In detail, a suitable multiple of \(t\in K_0\) is a point of the actual lifting translation subgroup. Its numerical action carries the chosen relatively ample class to itself modulo \(T\). The two-sided nef argument and pointed polarized stabilizer prove it has finite order on \(N^1(X/S)\). After taking a further multiple this numerical action is trivial on every extended divisor. The translated generic class minus the original class therefore lies in \(J\) for each actual generic line bundle. Dividing out the multiples gives \(\theta_D(t)\in J\). The \(\theta_H\) isogeny identifies \(U=(B(K)\otimes\mathbb Q)/K_0\) with \(P_F^0/J\), and all \(A_D\) descend to this finite-dimensional rational \(U\).

The image of the actual translating subgroup is a full lattice in \(U\). Indeed its image under \(\theta_H\) is the difference between the orbit of one integral ample class and that class in the quotient by the rational vertical subspace. It lies in the quotient lattice coming from the first paragraph. The isogeny on rationalized points and finite index of allowable translations give full rational span. Its action on \(L=P_F/J\) is exactly \(x\mapsto x+T_{\pi x}z\), where \(T_D\) is induced by \(A_D\); if \(z=0\) the action is trivial, and injectivity follows by evaluating at \(H\). No finite-generation assertion about all \(B(K)\) is necessary.

**A rational scalar product.** Pass to \(\bar K\) for the polarized endomorphism algebra. For a geometric point \(x\in F\), \(i_x:B\to F\) is the orbit map. Composing with the Albanese is, up to translation, an isogeny by Xu's homogeneous-fibration theorem. In particular \(i_x\) is finite onto its image and \(i_x^*H\) is ample. The group \(i_x^*:\operatorname{Pic}^0 F\to B^\vee\) is an isogeny. Equivariance gives
\[
 i_x^*\theta_D=\phi_{i_x^*D},\qquad
 A_D=\phi_{i_x^*H}^{-1}\phi_{i_x^*D}\in\operatorname{End}^0(B_{\bar K}).
\]
Thus \(A_D\) is Rosati-self-adjoint; it is positive semidefinite in each matrix factor if \(D\) is nef and positive definite if \(D\) is ample. This uses Prendergast-Smith, Theorems 3.4 and 4.3, arXiv:1008.4509 (also J. Math. Sci. Univ. Tokyo 19 (2012)).

Let \(\mathcal A\) be the unital rational subalgebra generated by all \(A_D\) with \(D\in N^1(F)_\mathbb Q\). It is stable under Rosati \(\dagger\) because its generators are self-adjoint and \(\dagger\) reverses products. Set \(\mathcal E=\operatorname{End}^0(B_{\bar K})\). The rational bilinear form
\[
  (x,y)=\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb Q}\bigl(L_{x^\dagger y}:\mathcal E\to\mathcal E\bigr)
\]
is symmetric positive definite (over \(\mathbb R\) it is a sum of positive multiples of real Hilbert–Schmidt products on the real, complex and quaternionic matrix factors). Restrict it to \(\mathcal A\); left multiplication by \(a^\dagger\) is the adjoint to left multiplication by \(a\). To obtain such an inner product on \(U\), take the \(\mathcal A\)-linear surjection \(\mathcal A^{\dim_{\mathbb Q}U}\to U\) given by a rational basis of \(U\). Its kernel is rational and \(\mathcal A\)-stable, hence so is its orthogonal complement. Transport the form along this complement. For \(D\) nef, the positive square root of \(A_D\) in the ambient real matrix product is a real polynomial in \(A_D\) by interpolating \(\sqrt{\lambda}\) on its finite spectrum. It is therefore in \(\mathcal A_\mathbb R\), is self-adjoint, and squares to \(A_D\). Consequently \(T_D\) on \(U\) is positive semidefinite. For \(D\) ample, its inverse is likewise a polynomial, so this action is positive definite. This proves the simultaneous positivity on the **whole** generic nef cone, including classes outside a chosen base domain.

## Exact generic movable classes and real fibres

First, any effective movable generic rational Cartier divisor has a movable closure in \(X\) by Li, arXiv:2309.04673, proof of Theorem 1.3, Step 1 (pp. 21–22). One can see this directly for a generic basepoint-free multiple. Take the effective horizontal closure of a member of the generic system. This divisor has no vertical component. For each desired generic member, take the horizontal closure of its divisor and the rational function identifying it with the first member. Their difference after correcting by that rational function has vertical support. After adding the pullback of a sufficiently positive effective base divisor containing its image this correction becomes effective. This gives an effective divisor linearly equivalent over \(S\) to the first closure, avoiding any prescribed horizontal prime avoided by the chosen generic member; the first closure already avoids all vertical primes as components. Clear finitely many Cartier indices. Thus the relative system has no divisorial fixed component. The projected lift depends only on the generic linear class.

Conversely, an actual relative effective class in \(\overline{\operatorname{Mov}}\) has an effective representative whose generic divisor is nef: restriction takes relatively movable classes to movable generic classes, and on a projective smooth curve or surface those numerical classes are nef. Write its generic divisor as an actual finite effective real combination \(D=\sum r_iD_i\) of primes on \(F\). The cone of coefficients
\[
\mathbb R_{\ge0}^k\ \cap\ \{(u_i):[\sum u_i D_i]\in\operatorname{Nef}(F)\}
\]
is rational polyhedral. Indeed the cone of the finitely many numerical prime classes intersects \(\operatorname{Nef}\) rationally polyhedrally by Li–Zhao, Theorem 2.8. Over a characteristic-zero field this follows on extending scalars to \(\bar K\), where the same theorem on rational effective support applies; the rational inverse image recovers the displayed cone. Equivalently embed the finitely many defining data into \(\mathbb C\) and use geometric invariance of numerical nefness. Thus the **actual coefficient vector** decomposes into a finite positive sum of rational effective nef divisors. Each such rational divisor is semiample: on a generic klt surface choose a rational boundary with \(K+\Delta'\sim_\mathbb Q0\) on the same support, perturb by a small rational multiple of the effective nef divisor and use surface log abundance, with norm/base-change descent of generation. A curve is simpler. Thus \(p(C_e)\) equals, inside \(P_F/J\), the conical hull of actual rational semiample line bundles on \(F\).

For a rational splitting \(s\) with the property that every actual semiample lift \(M\) over \(D\) lies in \(s(D)+\operatorname{im}T_D\), this gives the fibre equality of the operator theorem. For the upper bound, any such positive real sum of classes \(M_j\) has its residual coordinate in \(\sum_j\operatorname{im}T_{D_j}=\operatorname{im}T_{\sum t_jD_j}\) for all \(t_j>0\), by positive semidefiniteness. The \(D_j\) need not belong to the chosen base domain. For the lower bound, each rational generating ray \(D_i\) of that domain has an actual rational semiample lift \(M_i\): a rational numerical effective class has a rational effective representative by solving for coefficients on a finite support; then use abundance. The convex hull of the *actual* translates \(M_i+T_{D_i}\Lambda\) is exactly \(s(D_i)+\operatorname{im}T_{D_i}\); the convex hull of a full lattice is all of its real span and linear images commute with convex hull. Positive Minkowski sums give every fibre over \(D=\sum t_iD_i\), since \(\sum_{t_i>0}\operatorname{im}T_{D_i}=\operatorname{im}T_D\). This is exact at irrational parameter values and requires no closure or rational-density inference.

A splitting with this property is constructed using one point over a finite Galois extension \(E/K\). Given \(M\), let \(L=i_x^*M\), take its Picard-zero part \((L-[-1]^*L)/2\), apply the inverse of \(i_x^*:\operatorname{Pic}^0 F\to B^\vee\) on rationalized \(E\)-points, and average its Galois conjugates. This gives a rational linear retraction \(r:P_F\to P_F^0\) because it acts as the identity on \(P_F^0\). It descends modulo \(J\) and gives the section \(s(D)=[M-r(M)]\). For semiample \(M\), \(L\) is semiample. On \(N=(\ker\phi_L)^0\), \(L|_N\) is numerically trivial and semiample, hence torsion; the same holds for \([-1]^*L|_N\). The antisymmetric component restricts to zero in \(\operatorname{Pic}^0(N)\otimes\mathbb Q\). The kernel of \(B^\vee\to N^\vee\) is the image of \(\phi_L\), since this symmetric homomorphism annihilates \(N\) and the complementary dimensions agree. Thus the Picard-zero part of \(L\) is in the rationalized image of \(\phi_L\). To pass from geometric preimages to rationalized points, take a finite extension carrying a preimage and apply its trace. The identity \(i_x^*\theta_M=\phi_L\) then implies \(r(M)\) belongs to \(\operatorname{im}\theta_M\) over rationalized generic points; the trace of each conjugate remains in this \(K\)-defined image. Passing modulo \(J\) gives the required condition.
