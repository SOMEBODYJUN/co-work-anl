# Nonsplit glide action with shifted rational centres

Take \(c=(0,1/3)\), \(T=\operatorname{diag}(a,b)\) with \(a,b\ge0\), and the group generated by integer translations of \(\mathbb R^2\) and \(g(x,y)=(x+1/2,-y)\). Write \(w\) for the fibre coordinate measured from \(Tc\). The centred orbit is
\[
  S=\mathbb Z^2\ \cup\ \big((1/2,-2/3)+\mathbb Z^2\big).
\]
Any representative \(\tau_{(p,q)}g\) of the nonidentity holonomy class squares to translation by \((2p+1,0)\). In particular the extension by \(\mathbb Z^2\) is nonsplit. The orbit offsets are generally asymmetric.

The exact domain is given by \(a,b\ge0\) and the following eight inequalities:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |2w_1|&\le a,& |2w_2|&\le b,\\
 \pm w_1+\tfrac23w_2&\le \tfrac14a+\tfrac19b,&
 \pm w_1-\tfrac43w_2&\le \tfrac14a+\tfrac49b .
\end{aligned}
\]
To obtain all orbit inequalities \(2m\cdot w\le a m_1^2+b m_2^2\), decompose any integer lattice point into its signed coordinate units. For a glide point in a fixed coordinate orthant first subtract its nearest coordinatewise corner \(r=(\pm1/2,r_2)\), where \(r_2=1/3\) in the upper half-plane and \(r_2=-2/3\) in the lower half-plane. The two residual coordinates are still integer multiples of the signed coordinate units. In each coordinate, subtracting the comparison for \(r\) and then \(i\) unit comparisons leaves \(i(i-1)+2|r_j|i\) in the squared-coordinate bound. This is nonnegative for every integer \(i\ge0\). The same formula at \(r=0\) covers the lattice case. For every one of the eight comparisons the certificate gives positive rational weights and a rational witness where precisely that comparison is tight. Thus each inequality is necessary for this domain.

On \(a=0\), the first signed axis forces \(w_1=0\) and the remaining coordinate satisfies \(-b/3\le w_2\le b/6\). Independently the two nearest distinct projected centres around zero are \(-2/3,1/3\), with exactly these scaled midpoints. On \(b=0\), \(w_2=0\) and \(-a/4\le w_1\le a/4\), the midpoints for \(-1/2,1/2\). At \(a=b=0\) both coordinates vanish. These boundary tests require nearest centres in a projected lattice *and its shifted coset*.
