Blowing up at a general smooth codimension-two centre on each boundary component forces its coefficient to be strictly less than one when \(\dim X\ge2\). Absolute dimensions zero and one admit the stated zero or degree chamber independently. A normal quasi-projective base receiving this projective surjection from a projective total space is proper and projective.

Hacon–Xu, Corollary 1.6 in arXiv:1107.4149v2, applies to each rational boundary. A single rational \(\Delta_0\) with the original support serves every subsequent step. ## The actual generic field, group and cone

The singularities of terminal \(X\) occur in codimension at least three. Relative dimension at most two and generic smoothness make \(F\) geometrically smooth. Connectedness and geometric klt follow by generic restriction. A small birational map of smooth surfaces is an automorphism, as is the corresponding map for a smooth curve. Horizontal closure, \(\mathbb Q\)-factoriality and the equality of function fields identify \(L_{\mathbb Q}\) with \(\operatorname{Pic}(F)_{\mathbb Q}/K_0\). Li–Zhao, Proposition 4.3, shows \(K_0\) consists of geometrically numerically trivial classes. The torsion differences between numerical and algebraic equivalence, and the Brauer obstruction for Picard-scheme points, disappear after rationalization. This gives the stated sequence with the actual \(k\)-Picard group.

A movable system restricts to a generically movable system and therefore to a nef class on the smooth surface. Conversely, for an effective real divisor of nef numerical class the tests against its finitely many geometric support curves and nonnegativity of coefficients define a rational polyhedral cone on that support. All other curves have nonnegative intersection with each support curve. The invariant coefficient equations descend its rational decomposition to \(k\). Rational effective nef summands are semiample by a rational klt perturbation and surface abundance, followed by faithful flat descent of base-point-freeness. Closing a base-point-free system's horizontal representatives gives one avoiding every vertical prime and alternatives avoiding each potentially fixed horizontal prime. Negative vertical corrections can be cleared by a Cartier pullback from \(S\). These are effective movable lifts in the correct relative classes. This proves the claimed description of \(p(C)\) as a cone of actual real Picard classes.

For a \(k\)-automorphism fixing each geometric component of the generic boundary, Fujino–Gongyo, Theorem 1.1, applies to the projective geometric klt pair and its trivial Cartier multiple of the log canonical bundle. The scalar on its one-dimensional space of forms is a root of unity in \(k\), hence belongs to the original \(\mathbb C\). Wedging with a top form of \(k/\mathbb C\) gives the same scalar for the absolute pluriform. The order of the pulled-back base Cartier divisor is fixed on every transformed valuation over \(S\). The displayed discrepancy formula therefore holds equivariantly at horizontal and vertical valuations alike. Exactly the prime valuations on \(X\) have log discrepancy at most one for the effective terminal working boundary. Both directions of the field automorphism are small, and the log discrepancies recover the coefficients on those prime divisors, including the vertical support. The argument gives the required finite-index subgroup and every connected \(k\)-point. It avoids the false unrestricted generic-group equality diagnosed in V1.

For a smooth generic surface, Totaro, §2 and Theorem 4.1, applies over the abstract \(\bar k\simeq\mathbb C\) to the coefficient-preserving geometric group. Its rational cone is contained in the rational hull of the geometric nef cone. Looijenga's polyhedral-type criterion and Corollary 4.15 make the faithful numerical group finitely generated. Xu, Theorem 4.5, identifies the action of the neutral geometric automorphisms on the Albanese torsor with an isogeny to its translation group. A degree-\(n\) closed point marks that torsor by a Galois-invariant finite coset of its \(n\)-torsion. Every translated marking can be returned by a connected geometric automorphism, which acts trivially on the numerical group. Stabilization of a numerical ample class is of finite type by the Hilbert polynomial of graphs. Its connected part preserving the marking is finite by the isogeny. One finite Galois extension defines the generators of this marked group and the geometric Néron–Severi classes. Its finite Galois group \(H\) acts on both. ## Translation parameters and every real fibre

Its automorphism group has a finite component group modulo the neutral abelian variety \(A\), again by bounded Hilbert polynomial. Its actual \(k\)-points modulo the intersection with \(A(k)\) inject into this finite group. ## The two chambers and their stabilizers

Conjugacy of actual translations over the base gives \(A_gT_d=T_d B_g\); evaluation at \(H\) identifies the vertical linear part with its conjugation on the lattice. Averaging the rational section modulo translations and averaging the metric over the finite linear image produces the rational offsets \(n_g\) and finitely many lattice cosets \(S\), even for a nonsplit finite extension. The rational generator forms \(q_i(m)\) are semidefinite and their sum is definite. In each congruence class modulo \(2\Lambda\), coordinatewise-minimal integral tuples of values form a finite set and definite total norm bounds their possible vectors. For a nonminimal tuple take \(n\) in its class dominated by \(m\); the two vectors \((m+n)/2,(m-n)/2\) lie respectively in \(S\) and \(\Lambda\subset S\). Their form values add to at most the corresponding values for \(m\), with strictly smaller total for each new vector. This proves finite determination by induction and gives a rational polyhedral cone.

At a real boundary point the radical of \(T_d\) is an intersection of rational generator radicals. Both signs of the lattice in that radical impose orthogonality to it. Every chamber point therefore belongs to the allowed affine fibre, and the fibre of the chamber over zero consists of the origin alone. Projecting the offset cosets along this rational radical gives a finite union of discrete lattice cosets with attained nearest centres. Affine isometries from the actual kernel permute the centres and carry a nearest centre to zero, giving full real coverage. Comparing the inequality for a nonzero offset with the inverse-translate inequality gives opposite weak inequalities on the two cones, with strictness on the first interior. The zero-offset stabilizer injects into the finite quotient by translations. Nonidentity base elements are already separated by the strong base chamber and openness of projection.

Use a rational covector with trivial stabilizer in that finite group; its finitely many inequalities refine to a strong chamber on \(p(C)\). In both refinements, full-dimensionality follows from coverage of an open set by countably many translates of a polyhedron. Strict inequalities on this ambient interior and the inverse inequalities throughout the translated closed cone give the whole-translate conclusion, including trivial setwise stabilizer. In relative dimension zero the contraction is birational, all divisors are vertical, and the group over the identity is trivial. Here geography applies to \(V=T\subset\operatorname{Eff}\) and gives a polyhedral \(C\) directly.

## Geometric challenge and evidentiary scope

I tested the descent on a smooth projective example which rules out two shortcuts. Take nonisogenous complex elliptic curves \(E,B\) and points \(p\in E[n]\), \(q\in B[n]\) of the same order \(n>1\). Project the abelian threefold
\[
 (E^2\times B)/\langle(p,0,q)\rangle \longrightarrow B/\langle q\rangle .
\]
This is a smooth terminal Calabi–Yau fibre space with zero boundary. Its generic \(E^2\)-torsor has no rational point. A rational section would pull back to a rational map \(b\mapsto u(b)\) from \(B\) to \(E^2\) with \(u(b+q)-u(b)=(p,0)\). The map extends on the complete smooth curve and is a translate of a homomorphism. Nonisogeny forces it to be constant, a contradiction. Its torsor class has the form \((c,0)\) with \(c\ne0\), so the geometric coordinate interchange does not even give a field-defined automorphism with that linear part. A base chamber argument assuming a field origin or descent of the full geometric group fails this test. The finite marking and centralization in the actual semidirect product work without either assumption. On the effective-face side, on \(E^2\) the class of a pullback from the first factor can be translated in the first dual factor; twisting in the second dual factor is effective only for the trivial second twist, exactly as the radical calculation predicts after rationalization. The examples supplement the deductions.

I found no geometric counterexample or outstanding proof obligation for this exact revision. This is internal independent mathematical review.
