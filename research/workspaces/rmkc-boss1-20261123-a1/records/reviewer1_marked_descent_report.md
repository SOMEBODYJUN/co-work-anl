## Group, marking and one extension of fields

1. Let \(k=\mathbb C(S)\). Its algebraic closure is abstractly isomorphic to \(\mathbb C\), since the fields have identical characteristic and transcendence degree over \(\mathbb Q\). Under this identification Totaro Theorem 4.1 applies to the smooth, geometrically klt projective surface and the effective rational \(\mathbb Q\)-log-trivial boundary. His §2 uses the group preserving the divisor with its coefficients. His actual geometric covering \(Q\) has rational nef generators, so it lies in the rational hull \(O^+\) of the geometric nef cone and its translates cover the geometric ample interior \(O\). This pointed, full-dimensional open cone has the Néron–Severi lattice modulo torsion. Looijenga Proposition–Definition 4.1 and Corollary 4.15 give finite presentation, in particular finite generation, of the faithful geometric numerical group \(\Gamma\). No finiteness assertion is made here about the group of all geometric translations.
2. For a separable closed point of degree \(n\), the multiset \(z_1,\ldots,z_n\) of its Albanese images is Galois invariant. In any origin for the geometric torsor the condition is \(nz=\sum z_i\); a different origin gives the same points. In characteristic zero multiplication by \(n\) on the Albanese variety \(D\) is a surjective isogeny, so the solutions form a finite nonempty \(D[n]\)-coset \(Z\), including the case \(D=0\). An automorphism of the surface induces an affine automorphism of its Albanese torsor, whose linear part sends \(D[n]\) onto itself. Thus \(gZ\) is a \(D[n]\)-coset. Xu Theorem 4.5 makes the action of the geometric connected support-preserving group \(A\) on the Albanese torsor the full translation group up to isogeny, also for the coefficient-preserving group because the components of the boundary form a finite set. Therefore an element of \(A(\bar k)\) returns \(gZ\) to \(Z\) and has trivial action on the numerical lattice. This proves that \(M_Z\to\Gamma\) is surjective without a rational origin or rational element of that translation group.
3. The stabilizer of one integral numerical ample class in the geometric pair group has finite type. For an ample \(H\), the graph polynomial is \(\chi((H\otimes g^*H)^m)\), constant for \(g^*H\equiv H\). The automorphism graphs lie in an open part of the corresponding projective Hilbert scheme. Stabilization of the boundary components with their coefficients cuts out finitely many closed incidence conditions inside the automorphism scheme. The identity component of this stabilizer is \(A\), since the action of \(A\) on Néron–Severi is trivial. In one component, two elements preserving \(Z\) differ by a member of \(A\) taking \(Z\) to itself. The latter group is contained in the inverse image of the finite group \(D[n]\) under an isogeny, so is finite. This proves the numerical kernel of \(M_Z\) is finite. A lift of a finite generating set of \(\Gamma\), together with its finite kernel, generates \(M_Z\).
4. The absolute Galois group preserves both the geometric pair group and \(Z\). Each of the finitely many marked generators is defined as a morphism over a finite separable extension of \(k\). The same is so for line bundles representing a basis of the geometric numerical lattice. One finite normal compositum thus makes the Galois action on *both* \(M_Z\) and that lattice factor through the same finite \(H\). Geometric points of \(Z\) can also be split in this extension without changing any argument. In the actual semidirect product, for each \(h\in H\) the fibre over the identity numerical transformation has at most \(|\ker(M_Z\to\Gamma)|\) elements. Its full numerical kernel is finite. Galois commutation inside this actual semidirect product recovers fixed geometric morphisms, whose graphs descend to \(k\); it imposes no condition merely on the numerical action.

## Finite elements and the effective boundary

5. Looijenga Theorem 3.8 gives more than a finite family of possible intersections of two cones. For a pair of relative interiors of polyhedral cones, the *elements* yielding a nonempty intersection are finitely many cosets of the pointwise stabilizer of their containing face in \(O^+\). If two translates of \(Q\) intersect at an ample point, use the pair of faces of \(Q\) containing the two points in relative interior. Each face has an ample point there. Its relative interior lies in the full open cone \(O\): a point in that relative interior can be written as a positive multiple of the known interior point plus an element of the closed nef cone. Thus the smallest face of \(O^+\) in Theorem 3.8 is all of \(O^+\); its pointwise stabilizer in the faithful linear image is trivial. There are finitely many face pairs. The set \(\{a:Q\cap aQ\cap O\ne\varnothing\}\) is finite in the faithful image, and remains finite in \(M_Z\rtimes H\) by the finite numerical kernel. Looijenga's proof of Proposition 4.6 also gives this exact finite-element inference verbatim in its own setting. This would fail as a deduction about the full unmarked group with its unrestricted geometric translations; the marking removes precisely that difficulty.
6. For \(x\in O\cap W^H\) choose an actual \(g\in M_Z\) with \(g^{-1}x\in Q\). Then \(g^{-1}hg\) fixes \(g^{-1}x\) and belongs to the finite set for every \(h\). Finitely many labelled tuples suffice. Equality of the tuple for \(g\) and for a representative \(g_j\) gives \(hgg_j^{-1}=gg_j^{-1}h\) as actual group elements for every \(h\). Hence \(gg_j^{-1}\in M_Z^H\) descends as an actual automorphism and \(x\in(gg_j^{-1})(g_jQ\cap W^H)\). The marking does not need to supply an \(H\)-equivariant lift of any single numerical automorphism in advance.
7. Invariants in the rational geometric Néron–Severi group are exactly the rational numerical classes over \(k\). Pullback is injective by numerical descent. A finite Galois norm of a divisor representing an invariant class gives a multiple, and the norm preserves effectivity. For a rational ray of \(g_jQ\cap W^H\), its geometric effective numerical class admits an effective rational representative: write the class as a positive real combination of finitely many prime classes and solve the same finite rational system in nonnegative rationals. A larger finite Galois extension may be used for that particular effective representative without altering \(H\)'s already defined action on the lattice. Therefore the finite rational cone \(Q_k\) generated from these intersections is inside the actual \(D_k=\operatorname{Nef}_k\cap\operatorname{Eff}_k\).
8. The \(k\)-ample cone is \(O\cap W^H\), is full in the fixed space and has pointed closure; an invariant ample class exists. Use the faithful image of \(M_Z^H\) on this fixed space, preserving its full invariant lattice. Looijenga Proposition–Definition 4.1 turns the ample-interior coverage by \(Q_k\) into coverage of the entire rational hull by that same cone. This gives the inclusion of the rational hull in \(D_k\). Conversely, extend an arbitrary real class in \(D_k\) geometrically, write it as a nonnegative real combination of rational geometric nef classes in one Totaro chamber, and average each class over \(H\). Their averages are invariant rational nef classes, lie in the numerical space over \(k\) by the norm argument, and sum with the same real weights to the original invariant point. Hence \(D_k\) lies in the rational hull. This proves equality even on irrational boundary points. Enlarging \(M_Z^H\) to the full coefficient-preserving \(k\)-group preserves the covering since the cone is already effective and invariant as a target. The later finite-index extension argument in the full proof is a separate application to the group acting on the relative numerical cone.
