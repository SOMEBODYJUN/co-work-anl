## Direct check of base nonfactoriality and permitted lineality

As a further test of the published quantifiers, consider the three-dimensional projective quadric cone \(S=\{x_0x_3=x_1x_2\}\subset\mathbb P^4\). Incidence with a kernel line of the \(2\times2\) matrix of these four coordinates gives a projective small resolution \(f:X\to S\). Projection to the kernel lines exhibits \(X\) as a smooth \(\mathbb P^2\)-bundle over \(\mathbb P^1\). The only nontrivial fibre of \(f\) is an exceptional \(\mathbb P^1\) over the vertex. Since \(S\) is Gorenstein and \(f\) is small, \(K_X=f^*K_S\). A plane in \(S\) indexed by a fixed kernel line has strict transform of degree one on the exceptional curve. Any Cartier multiple downstairs would pull back with degree zero there. The base therefore fails \(\mathbb Q\)-factoriality.

Here the relative numerical space is a line, measured by degree on the exceptional curve. Every line bundle is relatively generated away from this codimension-two curve because \(f\) is an isomorphism there. Consequently both signs are movable, and they are effective by the vertical sign argument. Thus \(C=V=\mathbb R\) in this admissible example. The group over the identity of \(S\) acts trivially, and the full line is its strong rational polyhedral chamber. This test complements the nontrivial generic abelian-surface torsor in my full-proof report. Neither computation supplies a premise of the general proof.
