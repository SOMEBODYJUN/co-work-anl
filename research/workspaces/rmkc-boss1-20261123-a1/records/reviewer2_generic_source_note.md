## Galois orbits in the generic surface input

Moraga–Stark, arXiv:2409.13068v1, correctly notes in the opening of §3, pp. 12–13, that a negative curve generating a contractible extremal ray over the ground field may split as a Galois orbit of disjoint geometric exceptional curves. In the proof of Theorem 3.8, the argument of Claim 2 in the Iitaka-dimension-one case (pp. 15–16) employs both the integral Picard group over the stated base field and the self-intersection and anticanonical degree of a single geometric exceptional curve. For a reduced integral ground-field curve E splitting into e such curves, the applicable equalities are E² = K·E = −e. Thus using a single geometric curve in these equalities and using the integral ground-field class in the Mordell–Weil congruence computation require additional justification. A sign in the printed subsequent limiting calculation also reads −K·E = −1, where the anticanonical degree of one geometric exceptional curve is +1.

A test in the very Iitaka-dimension-one case is as follows. Put k = C(t), and fix a smooth complex cubic J in the projective plane and seven different constant points on J. The line bundle O_J(3)(−p₁−⋯−p₇) has degree two. Its complete linear system defines a finite separable map J→P¹ of degree two. Its generic effective divisor is one degree-two closed point over k, split into two distinct conjugate points over the algebraic closure of k. The cubic restriction sequence lifts the corresponding generic section of O_J(3) to a degree-three plane polynomial over k. The pencil spanned by this cubic and J has nine distinct simple base points geometrically, namely the seven constant points and the two conjugates. Blowing them up gives a smooth projective k-surface Y, whose anticanonical divisor is a fibre of the cubic pencil. Its Iitaka dimension is one. The generic plane cubic in the pencil is smooth since one of its members is J. Two distinct smooth members exist over the infinite field k. Their strict transforms with coefficients 1/2 form a rational terminal boundary and K_Y+Δ is Q-linearly trivial. On a smooth surface, the component coefficient is <1 and the two components are disjoint, so every blow-up in codimension two has log discrepancy greater than one.

The Picard rank over k is 1+7+1=9. Blow down only the exceptional curve over the degree-two closed point. The morphism has relative Picard rank one and small enough pullback of any ample class on its target exposes this K-negative extremal ray (indeed the pullback itself is nef and has degree zero exactly on the contracted curves). The integral exceptional divisor E is geometrically the two disjoint components. Its self-intersection and canonical degree are −2. A geometrically irreducible exceptional divisor for this contraction cannot be defined over k because Galois interchanges the two disjoint components. The only integral curve over k on that ray is supported in the exceptional set, hence equals E. Thus the failure cannot be excluded by the Iitaka dimension or the Picard rank assumptions in the printed subargument.

The degree e is bounded for a fixed surface by the rank of its geometric Néron–Severi group, since disjoint curves of square −1 are linearly independent. Several numerical estimates in the printed proof can be repaired with this bound and a partition by e. This observation alone does not supply the whole descent argument. An independent source for the actual generic numerical covering is Fulin Xu, arXiv:2405.20899v3, Theorem 1.9 and Remark 1.10. Its proof in §6.4 reduces fields by Lemmas 2.28–2.29, applies the absolute divisor MMP and invokes its own Theorems 1.3, 1.5 and 1.7. The form of the theorem has no finite-generation restriction on its ground field. The hypotheses hold for the generic rational pair because −K_F is Q-linearly equivalent to its effective rational boundary. Its input concerns pairs preserving their coefficients. The currently pending check is the exact two-dimensional specialization of the source's proof and any descent detail it needs. No approval of a publication freeze is asserted here.
