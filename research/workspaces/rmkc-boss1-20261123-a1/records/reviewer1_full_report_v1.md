## Confirmed source-level defect at §2 line 43 and source ledger line 126

Li II 2309.04673v1, p. 22, first sentence of the proof of Theorem 1.3, asserts equality of the total and generic support-preserving pseudo-automorphism groups by Lemma 2.6; the proof of Theorem 1.8 then invokes Theorem 1.3. Li's definitions at pp. 5–6 use preservation of support, so coefficients cannot be silently fixed in testing this equality. Section 3 of V1 proves total extension for the connected generic group only. Here is an example meeting the target hypotheses:

* Let Y=P¹_z×P¹_t and f₀ be projection to P¹_t. Add the four constant sections z=0,∞,1,−1 with weights 3/5,3/5,9/20,7/20, respectively, and the fibre F₀ at t=0 with weight 1/2. The section weights sum to 2. Thus K_Y plus this boundary is Q-linearly trivial relative to the projection.
* Blow up the two points (0,0),(∞,0) of Y in the (z,t) coordinates. Put on the smooth resulting X the crepant boundary Δ, with the same strict-transform weights and coefficient 3/5+1/2−1=1/10 on each exceptional curve. Writing μ for the blowup, K_X=μ* K_Y+E₀+E∞ and μ*Δ_Y=the strict boundary+(11/10)(E₀+E∞). Hence K_X+Δ=μ*(K_Y+Δ_Y), proving relative Q-linear triviality. The SNC boundary has all coefficients less than 1, and at every intersection the sum is less than 1. At the two blown-up points the new sums are 7/10 and 3/5; at the other original sections they are 19/20 and 17/20. On a smooth surface this implies terminality: blowing up any point gives an exceptional boundary coefficient strictly below zero; further point blowups preserve the same inequality for all exceptional divisors. The total space is projective and Q-factorial and the fibres are connected.
* The generic automorphism g(z)=(z−1)/(z+1) permutes {0,∞,1,−1}, taking 0,∞ to −1,1. The unique induced rational map is μ⁻¹gμ. At the images gμ(E₀),gμ(E∞) the inverse μ⁻¹ is regular, so this composition is constant on each exceptional curve, with values the unblown points above −1 and 1 on F₀. In particular g does not extend to a relative pseudo-automorphism. It need not preserve the four weights on the generic fibre, as the convention expressly requires only support preservation.

I checked the four homogeneous point images and these weights using exact Python fractions. The computation gave the point permutation (0,−1,∞,1) as a four-cycle and intersection sums 7/10, 3/5, 19/20 and 17/20; the blowup and valuation justifications are the arguments above.

This example is confined to the unqualified group equality. In particular its generic numerical degree group has trivial action; it neither contradicts a finite-index assertion nor shows a bad chamber for any actual geometry. The literal statement of Li's Theorem 1.8 is a statement about the right target group. Citing it by its theorem statement would normally be legitimate. Here its precise proof contains a demonstrably false assertion, and the manuscript expressly claims to have avoided that assertion while relying on this proof. Approving the exact theorem requires the already available direct alternative to be given for this load-bearing application.

**Direct replacement for the application.** Use the same rational terminal boundary Δ', integer r and actual form Φ as §3, including when q=0. In the group of generic support-preserving regular automorphisms, fixing every geometric component of the boundary has finite index. It preserves Δ'_F with its coefficients. Such regular pair automorphisms act by B-pluricanonical automorphisms. Fujino–Gongyo, Theorem 1.1, says that their action on the one-dimensional space of generic plurilog forms has finite image after algebraic closure. The result applies under an abstract identification of the algebraic closure of C(S) with C. Accordingly each scaling factor in k is a root of unity, hence lies in the original constant field C. Wedging the relative form with the top base form gives exactly the same scalar on Φ. Thus all discrepancies in §3's displayed formula are invariant. Terminality identifies the valuations of discrepancy at most 1 with the prime divisors on X. The map is consequently small in both directions and preserves the support of Δ, including its vertical primes. This proves finite index of the actual extending group. Equivalently one can apply the generic cone theorem directly to the rational pair (F,Δ'_F) and use its coefficient-preserving subgroup. The generic cone theorem supplies a rational covering, finitely many coset translates and convex hull give a rational covering for the actual image, and pointed Looijenga gives the required strong chamber there. This uses no relative rational-hull result or equality of the full groups. I verified the stated input in Fujino–Gongyo's original Theorem 1.1, arXiv:1104.0361, p. 2 (projective lc pair, Cartier multiple, semiample log canonical class). The frozen journal already records this alternative under its second-pass item 4: this is a delivery/source-chain correction, not a new condition on the target.
