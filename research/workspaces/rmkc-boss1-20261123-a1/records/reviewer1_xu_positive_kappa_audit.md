# Positive anti-canonical dimension in the replacement generic-surface source

**Finding.** For a projective geometrically klt log Calabi–Yau surface with rational boundary over the characteristic-zero field of the generic fibre, the branches \(\kappa(-K)=1,2\) in Fulin Xu's Theorem 1.9 have an independent surface-specialized proof. The proof below uses the genus-one calculation and vertical-divisor argument of that paper, together with the original surface nef polytope and abundance theorem of Fujino. No assertion about a negative extremal ray over a nonclosed field being the class of a single geometric \((-1)\)-curve is needed. This note addresses the absolute generic-surface input alone. The original strong relative result requires the other applications audited separately.

The paper checked is Fulin Xu, *On the cone conjecture for pairs of large Kodaira dimension*, arXiv:2405.20899v3, 19 November 2026. The group and numerical cone appear in Conjecture 1.1 and Theorems 1.7 and 1.9. Definition 2.5 separately defines the cones of effective nef and effective movable **Picard classes** over fields of finite type over \(\mathbb Q\). Theorem 5.1 is the numerical assertion for varieties with \(K\) torsion and zero boundary. Theorem 1.5 is a different statement about rational polyhedral covering of Picard classes over finitely generated fields. Section 6.4 uses the latter to prove Theorem 1.9 after an anti-canonical fibration. The proof for the positive-dimensional branches here uses only its genus-one Lemma 5.4. The complete paper has no citation of Moraga–Stark. Li II appears in an introductory comparison and in a comment preceding Theorem 5.6; its generic/relative group equality is no input below. [Fulin Xu, original PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.20899)

## Reductions used here

Let \(Y\) be the surface over a finitely generated field \(l/\mathbb Q\) after the field reduction of Lemmas 2.28–2.29, with effective rational boundary \(D\), \(K_Y+D\sim_{\mathbb Q}0\), and \(Y\) Q-factorial and geometrically klt. In the intended application one first chooses the same-support rational terminal boundary on the arbitrary-dimensional total space. Its restriction supplies the rational generic boundary and the ordinary Kodaira condition for this **integral** divisor \(-K\) since \(-rK\) has the effective representative \(rD\). Rational approximation of arbitrary negative or non-effective generic divisors is unnecessary. The descending model can include the finite data defining the linear equivalence, the ample polarization and a numerical basis.

For \(\kappa(-K)=1\) run a \(D\)-MMP to its nef model, as in Xu §6.4. In dimension two a birational extremal step contracts divisors and strictly decreases the numerical Picard number. A pseudoeffective divisor having positive Iitaka dimension cannot end in a fibre-type anti-ample step. Surface termination does not need the unidentified “Proposition ??” printed in the full general-dimensional proof of §6.4. Each contracted prime occurs in the effective boundary since a curve outside its support has nonnegative intersection with it. This also applies to a whole Galois orbit of curves by geometric intersection. Xu Theorem 1.3 transfers the numerical cone assertion upon contracting these boundary components. The separate source audit is responsible for that theorem and the field reduction. At the nef model, \(K_Y+(1+\epsilon)D\sim_{\mathbb Q}\epsilon D\) for small positive rational \(\epsilon\), and surface abundance yields a base-point-free multiple of \(D\); base-point-freeness descends by scalar extension. The anticanonical semiample contraction \(h:Y\to B\) has \(B\) a normal projective curve and \(-K_Y\sim_{\mathbb Q}h^*A\) for an ample rational divisor \(A\). All hypotheses concerning the dimensions and adjoint in Xu Theorem 1.7 are satisfied. Its Assumption 2.10 supplies the klt MMP and nonvanishing needed for its general proof, and is supplied in dimension two by its Proposition 2.11 or classical surface MMP. An explicit surface proof of the needed consequence follows.

## The \(\kappa=1\) generic curve and actual group

Put \(K=l(B)\) and \(E=Y_\eta\). A normal klt surface has isolated singularities, and generic smoothness in characteristic zero gives a geometrically smooth curve. Its fibres are geometrically connected generically; the contraction has geometrically integral generic fibre. Since \(K_Y\) is relatively Q-linearly trivial, \(K_E\) is torsion, so \(E\) has genus one. An effective \(D\) with \(D\sim_{\mathbb Q}h^*A\) has degree zero on \(E\), so \(D\) has only vertical components. The generic curve may have no \(K\)-point.

The torsor \(E\) under its Jacobian \(J\) carries translations by all \(J(K)\). On \(\operatorname{Pic}(E)_{\mathbb Q}\) a translation by \(b\) changes a class \(M\) by \(\deg(M)b\), up to the uniform pullback sign. This follows after a geometric origin is chosen and is invariant under descent. The rational Picard group removes the finite-degree Brauer obstruction. The field \(K\) is finitely generated over \(\mathbb Q\), hence \(J(K)\) is finitely generated by Lang–Néron and Mordell–Weil, as proved in Xu Theorem 2.1 and Corollary 2.2. Pick integral points \(b_1,\ldots,b_t\) spanning \(J(K)_{\mathbb Q}\) independently, and a rational degree-one class \(z\) obtained from a positive-degree divisor divided by its degree. In \(\operatorname{Pic}(E)_{\mathbb R}\) the effective nef cone is the open halfspace of positive degree together with zero. Indeed, positive-degree rational classes have an effective multiple by genus-one Riemann–Roch, and the positive-degree real halfspace is their convex hull. An effective real divisor of degree zero is zero. The cone over \(z+\sum_i[0,1]b_i\) consists of effective classes. For any positive real degree, translations move its normalized degree-one point into this parallelepiped by taking coordinate integer parts. This proves Xu Lemma 5.4 on all real classes and Theorem 1.7 hypothesis (1), directly for torsors. The group \(\operatorname{Aut}(E)/J(K)\) is finite, via the origin-preserving automorphisms of the Jacobian. Thus \(\operatorname{Aut}(E)\) is finitely generated. For this group the “essentially finite index” conclusion of Lemma 6.10, defined in 6.1 using a quotient whose finitely generated subgroups are finite, already gives ordinary finite index. No properness of a linear action on a non-pointed Picard cone is required.

There is also a direct verification of the lifting required in Theorem 1.7 hypothesis (2). Take \(rK_Y=\operatorname{div}(\phi)+h^*A_0\) for a Cartier divisor \(A_0\) on the base and choose a rational \(r\)-canonical form \(\Phi\) representing \(h^*A_0\). A generic curve automorphism acts on its \(r\)-canonical form by a root of unity: translations fix the invariant differential and the origin-preserving automorphism group is finite. The determinant of absolute versus relative differentials shows the same scalar acts on \(\Phi\). Every valuation has order zero on any root of unity. The formula \(a(v;Y,0)=1+(\operatorname{ord}_v\Phi-v(h^*A_0))/r\) therefore makes all discrepancies invariant.

A klt surface has only finitely many exceptional divisorial valuations of log discrepancy at most one. To check this, take an SNC resolution with rational crepant coefficients \(b_i<1\). An exceptional curve newly created by a point blowup has nonnegative crepant coefficient only at an intersection of two positive components, where its coefficient is \(b_i+b_j-1\). This is strictly smaller than both positive input coefficients. The denominators are bounded by a common multiple. The tree of possible new nonnegative extractions is finitely branching with bounded depth. A blowup with negative coefficient and its descendants on that branch cannot produce a new nonnegative coefficient. Every exceptional divisorial valuation on a smooth surface appears in a sequence of point blowups, so this proves finiteness, also after geometric scalar extension. Existing SNC exceptional divisors form the initial finite set.

These exceptional valuations all map into a finite set of closed points of \(B\). Above these points there are finitely many actual prime curves on \(Y\). The generic automorphism group acts over \(B\) and permutes the finite collection of valuations of discrepancy at most one with centres in these fibres. The stabilizer of the subset consisting of exceptional valuations therefore has finite index. All actual divisorial valuations on \(Y\) are exactly those with discrepancy at most one outside this exceptional subset, so its stabilizer extends to a pseudo-automorphism of \(Y/B\). Taking the finite-index subgroup additionally fixing the finitely many geometric prime components above the base points supporting \(D\) preserves the marked boundary. This proves the requisite ordinary finite index. The argument may be performed over \(\bar l\) for the finite sets, then restricted to \(\operatorname{Aut}_K(E)\); Galois invariance and flat descent of the codimension-one isomorphism give the same conclusion on \(Y\). This proof also explains the specialization of Xu Lemmas 6.7–6.10. Lemma 6.10 uses the unmarked pair \((Y,0)\) over \(B\), so the coefficients of \(D\) need not be standard.

## The \(\kappa=1\) Picard covering on the surface

Let \(G\) be the actual extending subgroup just proved. Finite cosets and convex hull of translates turn the genus-one covering into a single rational polyhedral covering cone \(P\) for \(G\) on \(\operatorname{Pic}(E)_{\mathbb R}\). All cones in this paragraph are cones of effective nef classes represented in the indicated Picard space. In dimension two or one their image on numerical classes is the effective nef cone. The argument below supplies the specialization of Xu Theorem 1.7, proof Steps 2–5, without using the general-dimensional equivalences between different versions of geography.

The vector space \(\operatorname{Pic}(Y)_{\mathbb R}\) is finite dimensional by Xu Corollary 2.4. Restriction onto \(\operatorname{Pic}(E)_{\mathbb Q}\) is surjective by horizontal closure and Q-factoriality, and its kernel is spanned by vertical prime classes: a rational function determining a generic principal relation is a rational function on \(Y\). Choose finitely many vertical primes \(F_i\) spanning that kernel. For each rational generator of \(P\), choose a rational effective representative on \(E\) and take its effective Q-Cartier horizontal closure. Let \(D_j\) be the primes among these closures. Choose an effective rational representative \(A'\sim_{\mathbb Q}A\) containing every point \(h(F_i)\), using a high multiple of the ample divisor on the projective curve \(B\). For a small positive rational \(\delta\), the klt rational boundary \(D'=(1-\delta)D+\delta h^*A'\) is effective, gives \(K_Y+D'\sim_{\mathbb Q}0\), and contains all \(F_i\) with strictly positive coefficients.

On the finite support consisting of the components of \(D'\) and all \(D_j\), take a small closed rational coefficient box around \(D'\), inside the effective cube, with \(D'\) interior in each positive coordinate and every coordinate allowed a small increase. All its points give klt pairs by openness. Imposing nefness of \(K_Y+\Theta\) cuts out a rational polytope \(N\). In the proof of Fujino Theorem 8.1 the cube for finite support is intersected with the cone-theorem inequalities to give exactly this rational nef polytope. Apply it to geometric components and intersect with the rational equal-coefficient subspace for their Galois orbits. For finitely generated \(l\) the algebraic closure embeds in \(\mathbb C\); numerical equivalence, nefness and the rational nef polytope are preserved on algebraically closed field extension. Each rational vertex has semiample adjoint by surface abundance, and a globally generated multiple descends to \(l\) by flat base change of sections. Thus the cone \(W\) of the images \([K_Y+\Theta]\) from \(N\) in \(\operatorname{Pic}(Y)_{\mathbb R}\) is rational polyhedral, contains zero and consists entirely of actual effective nef Picard classes. [Fujino, proof of Theorem 8.1, p. 28](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1001.3902)

Writing \(p\) for restriction, one has \(p^{-1}(P)\cap\operatorname{Nef}^e_{\rm Pic}(Y)=p^{-1}(P)\cap W\). For the nontrivial inclusion take an actual class \(M\) on the left. Express its generic restriction using the chosen positive generators and lift them. The difference is vertical, so \(M=\sum_j a_j[D_j]+\sum_i c_i[F_i]\) in the real Picard space with \(a_j\ge0\) and arbitrary \(c_i\). For sufficiently small \(u>0\), put \(\Theta=D'+u(\sum_j a_jD_j+\sum_i c_iF_i)\). The negative coefficients in the vertical directions fit inside the positive coordinates of \(D'\), so \(\Theta\) lies in the box. Its adjoint is real-linearly equivalent to \(uM\) and is nef. Hence it lies in \(N\), which yields \(M\in W\). The reverse inclusion follows from the verified actual effectivity and nefness of every vertex. This proves that the preimage slice is rational polyhedral. Its translates cover the full effective nef Picard cone, since the generic restriction of an effective nef class has positive degree or is zero and \(G\) covers that generic cone. Push down to numerical space to obtain a rational polyhedral covering cone under the actual coefficient-preserving group of the marked surface.

This handles possible positive rank of \(\operatorname{Pic}^0(Y)(l)\), every real generic degree and nonsplit genus-one torsors. All finite generation in this paragraph concerns finitely generated fields over \(\mathbb Q\). The comparison to the original \(\mathbb C(S)\) uses numerical cones and field reduction only.

## The \(\kappa=2\) branch

There is a shorter proof even on the initial surface. The effective rational boundary \(D\sim_{\mathbb Q}-K_Y\) is big. By Kodaira's lemma choose \(D\sim_{\mathbb Q}H+N\) with \(H\) ample rational and \(N\) effective rational, all defined over the given field. For sufficiently small rational \(t>0\), \(\Theta=(1-t)D+tN\) is klt and \(-K_Y-\Theta\sim_{\mathbb Q}tH\) is ample. Thus \(Y\) is of Fano type. The surface cone theorem gives a rational polyhedral geometric nef cone, and the base-point-free theorem gives semiampleness of any rational nef divisor. Intersect this geometric cone with the rational numerical space of the field of definition. The nef cone is rational polyhedral there as well, and a multiple of each rational ray divisor is effective by flat base change of sections. Hence the entire nef cone is the effective nef cone. This covering is immediate. In the semiample anticanonical description the generic fibre in this branch is zero dimensional, but no induction on a fibre or field reduction is required for this big-boundary proof.

The surface-specific proofs here cover the two positive-Iitaka cases and avoid any appeal to the disputed extremal-curve assertion in Moraga–Stark. The independent field reduction, reverse contractions and \(\kappa=0\) branch are the subjects of the other review.
