## Derivation and attempted objections

1. Lines 7–18 and 22–34. All fibres, group kernels and linear maps use the same exact sequence. Translating and conjugating and then evaluating at the point with T_H = id identifies the two actions of the point group on U. Averaging a rational positive product over its finite image preserves symmetry and positivity of each generator. Modulo the subspace of sections D↦T_Dz the lattice acts trivially, so averaging a finite set of coset representatives fixes the class of the section. Each new section value is still in the same prescribed affine fibre. Evaluation at H uniquely recovers the rational displacement n_g, even if H is outside B. The cocycle identity then gives that S is a union of full left translation cosets of the same lattice. A potential objection that the section averaging assumes an actual group splitting is **not-real**.
2. Lines 36–49. Choose a common denominator on a finite superlattice. Dickson's lemma applies separately to the finitely many classes modulo 2Λ. Although several vectors may have the same tuple, positive definiteness of the sum makes the fibre of each tuple finite. For a nonminimal tuple choose a strictly smaller comparable tuple; the two half-sums a,b respectively lie in m+Λ and Λ. Their individual q-values are smaller because they are nonnegative and their sum is strictly less than q(m). This is a well-founded integral induction. A potential objection that S must be closed under arbitrary addition or negation is **not-real**. Finite generation of the simultaneous inequalities follows for every positive linear combination on B.
3. Lines 51–55. For real nonnegative coefficients the kernel of T_D is the common intersection of the kernels of its positive summands, by positivity. This intersection is rational. Testing ±(Λ∩ker T_D) forces perpendicularity to that full kernel, hence membership in im T_D. At D=0 all of U is tested. Quotienting by this rational kernel gives a genuine lattice and the finite union of cosets is discrete there. A closest centre exists for the induced positive definite form. The affine action permutes these centres and preserves distances, so translating from the minimizing centre returns to the zero cell. This covers the actual prescribed affine slice for each real D including irrational points on rational faces. The argument never infers real coverage from rational point coverage. A proposed possible failure from failure of discreteness on a singular quotient is **not-real**.
4. Lines 55–61. The linear openness of π, the nonempty ambient interior, countable covering by translates and the elementary fact that a countable union of proper linear subspaces contains no open ball imply the needed full dimensions. If the base action is nontrivial, strong separation on B excludes the *whole closed* competing translate meeting the interior. In the base kernel with nonzero n_g, the functional F_m is nonzero (variation in w suffices) and nonnegative on the cell. At any interior point it is strictly positive. On the entire translated cell the inverse-centre inequality gives the opposite sign. Zero-displacement elements preserve the cell; these and only these are its stabilizer and inject into the finite group G_0/Λ. This remains valid when a nonzero displacement collapses on a boundary slice. The frozen lines yield a weak domain with finite stabilizer; the faithful action on the full space lets one choose a rational covector with trivial stabilizer in that finite group. Intersect with its halfspaces as in lem:finite. The finite-orbit minimum gives coverage, strictness of every nonidentity comparison on ambient interior gives the full requested separation and trivial stabilizer. A potential confusion between disjoint interiors and closed-translate exclusion is **not-real**.
5. Dimension zero and faces with zero operators introduce no unlisted hypotheses. A vacuous metric on the zero space and a singleton quotient are allowed. The theorem's finite-dimensional convex setting is the one of the problem. I found no confirmed mathematical defect or unresolved implication inside this conditional claim.
