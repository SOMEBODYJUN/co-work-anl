# Author's boundary check on frozen candidate 2

I rechecked Sections 4–6 of the frozen proof b3bb65993b458e2d75cafd577b7552df267cb67935f5c1a1091fd96d9e875fd1 from the definitions of effective Picard classes and the actual relative numerical quotient. The following are independent checks of those two arguments by one of the candidate's authors. They do not represent an additional nonauthor review.

## Elliptic orbits of degree zero

Let A act on F with q(F)=1. After geometric scalar extension fix a line bundle M effective up to a positive power and with orbit degree zero. The orbit action defines a family of degree-zero bundles o_x* M on A. More formally, pull back M to A×F by the action and tensor with the inverse of the pullback of M from F to rigidify along {0}×F. The Picard functor gives a morphism ψ:F→Pic⁰(A). Take a multiple with a nonzero section u. On the dense open where u(x) is nonzero, its restriction to the orbit of x is nonzero at the origin. A nonzero section of a degree-zero bundle on a smooth proper curve has no zeros, so the corresponding multiple of ψ(x) is zero. As the Picard variety is separated, that multiple of ψ is zero on all of F. This deals even with a special orbit lying wholly inside the zero divisor of the chosen section. It gives the vanishing of every averaged orbit pullback in rational Picard groups without needing global generation of the multiple. The proof in the frozen manuscript also has global generation from the surface abundance theorem.

If M and M' are two rational effective lifts of the same such d, their difference P belongs to Pic⁰(F)(k) tensored with Q. The common orbit pullback j sends P to zero. The dual of the isogeny A→Alb(F) is j, so its kernel as a group scheme is finite. Consequently P=0 rationally before any quotient by K₀. A nontrivial torsion twist can remain at the level of individual integral line bundles, which is harmless for the vector spaces in the theorem. For real lifts, use the nonnegative rational effective-nef decomposition on the finite support and nonnegativity of orbit degrees. When the sum has degree zero each summand has degree zero. This puts every such lift in the rational section's kernel projection and proves uniqueness over the whole effective zero-degree face.

For example on E×P¹ with four disjoint sections E×{p_i} of coefficient one half, a pullback from P¹ has orbit degree zero. Twisting by an infinite-order degree-zero bundle from E kills its sections for all positive powers. The two elementary arguments above show that finite orbit stabilizers, nontrivial torsors, and the orbit of a chosen geometric point do not provide counterexamples to the rational conclusion.

## An abelian-surface torsor and its numerical quotient

Let ξ∈H¹(k,A) be the class of F. Its finite period m is enough; a chosen origin over k is unnecessary. For every integral a∈End_k(A), u=1+mna fixes ξ for all n by additivity, and some nonzero n makes u an isogeny since the determinant on the Tate space is a polynomial nonzero at n=0. The contracted product of F with A along u is an A-torsor representing ξ, with its canonical u-equivariant map from F. A k-isomorphism back to F gives a dominant self-map on F. Geometrically it differs from u by a translation. Its pullback on Pic⁰ is u^∨, independently of the chosen isomorphism.

On a resolution of its relative graph write p:Z→X for the birational source projection, q:Z→X for the target morphism. For f-numerically trivial D, q*D has degree zero on every curve contracted over S. As X is Q-factorial, p* p_* q*D exists as a rational divisor, and differs from q*D by a p-exceptional divisor numerically trivial over p. Negativity for both signs makes that difference zero. To test p_*q*D on any f-contracted curve C, take the closure of a finite-residue-field closed point of the generic fibre of p above C. The resulting integral curve maps with positive finite degree onto C, so projection gives zero intersection. On the generic fibre q_F=u_F∘p_F as morphisms by agreement on a dense open; hence restriction of p_*q*D is the ordinary generic pullback. This tests exactly K₀, not all geometrically numerically trivial divisors. Subtraction and division by mn gives a^∨K₀⊂K₀. Since b↦b† is a rational involution, taking a=b† and clearing denominators gives b(h⁻¹K₀)⊂h⁻¹K₀ for every b∈End⁰_k(A).

To test the most restrictive effective boundary, put N=ker(λ_d)⁰ for a rational nef effective class d. The kernel of restriction A^∨→N^∨ is connected by the dual exact sequence. Symmetry of λ_d and equality of dimensions identify this kernel with im λ_d even when ker λ_d has a disconnected finite part. Restrictions of M to N-cosets have the same class after identifying each coset with N because every translation difference lies in this annihilator. The common restriction is numerically trivial. One coset contains a point where some effective multiple has a nonzero section. On a projective abelian variety an effective numerically trivial divisor is zero, by intersection with an ample class, so that restriction is trivial rationally. Reflections about all conjugate origins carry N-cosets to N-cosets. Each reflected summand defining s(d) therefore has the same trivial rational restriction. The difference M−s(d) lies in im λ_d, and a geometric preimage can be made rational after taking the sum of its finite Galois orbit and dividing by the extension degree. This proves the upper boundary before and after passing to the actual quotient. The extreme cases N=0 and N=A yield respectively the whole Pic⁰ direction and only zero.

For a rational numerical d in the cone choose a rational effective-nef lift using finite support: in the rational polyhedral coefficient cone giving the original real lift, intersect with the rational affine system fixing its class to d and choose a rational point. Its translates differ by T_dΛ, whose convex hull is im T_d, since convex hull commutes with every linear map. Thus the lower bound does not require the image of the lattice under a singular operator to be discrete. Finally if d is a sum of positive real multiples of rational effective d_i, positivity gives ker T_d=∩ker T_{d_i}. Orthogonal complements establish the image-sum identity, so taking finite sums of the affine fibres gives exactly the real effective fibre.

For a product A=E₁×E₂, a divisor pulled back from E₁ has connected null subvariety E₂; twisting by an infinite-order Pic⁰(E₂) class prevents effectivity. An abstract relative numerical quotient by an arbitrarily chosen diagonal subspace could spoil this test. The graph argument above forces invariance under the rational projection operators whenever those are defined over k, and under the entire k-endomorphism algebra in general. Thus a noninvariant diagonal quotient cannot come from the actual K₀ in the claimed category.

## Scope of the source reads

The full texts listed in source_audit_v2.md were available to inspect. The checks made are the statements, proof passages, and definitions explicitly named there. In particular “the complete original arXiv v1 was read in the repository” under Li II records access to and reading from the full original, with the detailed mathematical reading limited to the stated sections and their dependencies. It does not assert that every line of the 32-page text or every cited foundational work was read. Likewise an entire PDF having been opened for Fujino, Fujino–Gongyo, Xu, Moraga–Stark or Milne does not signify cover-to-cover reading. The Hacon–Xu numerical-triviality input and the good-model input are two different papers. The numerical-triviality Cor. 1.6 of arXiv:1107.4149v2 and its proof, and the good-model Thm. 2.12 of arXiv:1105.1169v3 and its proof were directly read. Only metadata of the 2026 journal edition of Li II were available; its text plays no role in the proof.

Neither boundary test produced a geometric counterexample or a new mathematical obligation in the frozen candidate.
