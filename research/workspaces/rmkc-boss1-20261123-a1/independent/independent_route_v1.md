# Independent geometry route, v1, 23 November 2026

**Status.** This is a fixed candidate argument, not a certification or a claimed existing theorem. I was given the original assertion, acceptance conditions, two primary-source PDFs and the instructions for my own workspace. I did not use any other researcher's candidate or the repository's earlier mathematical work. I found no geometric counterexample. Sections below identify steps that should be challenged independently before any global claim.

## Relative geometry preceding all cases

Set `K=C(S)`, `Y=X_eta`, `V=N^1(X/S)_R`, and `U=span_R{[E]: E prime vertical over S}`. Denote the projection by `p:V->V'=V/U`. Every vertical prime has both signs in Eff: take a base effective Cartier divisor passing through its image and pull back a sufficiently high multiple. The span is rational. The action on it is finite, since a finite collection of prime divisors gives a basis and any one can only be sent to a divisorial component of the fixed closed subset over its original image. Properness is used for the image to be closed.

The kernel of the action on `V'` is finite. Restrict it first to the finite-index part fixing `U` pointwise and preserving the actual coefficients of the finite boundary support. For a rational relatively ample class `H`, every translated `gH` belongs to the rational polyhedral cone `P=R_+H+U ⊂ Eff`. Li–Zhao v8, Theorem 2.7, gives a finite subdivision by open rational cones on each of which the weak models of the klt effective perturbations are constant. Every translated marked nef chamber attached to `gH` is a weak model for a small effective representative of `gH`. Two such classes in a common cell therefore lie in the first marked nef chamber, and the second is interior to its own chamber. Distinct marked ample cones have disjoint interiors (section rings on SQMs, cf. Kawamata Lemma 1.5). A point in the interior of the second cone cannot lie in the closure of the full-dimensional first one unless the two agree. Thus only finitely many of these chambers occur. The subgroup stabilizing the original nef cone has finite index. For its elements `(g−I)^2=0`; the entire orbit `H+n(g−I)H`, for all signs of `n`, is nef. Closedness and pointedness give `(g−I)H=0`. This argument for every ample class proves identity on `V`. The same reasoning can be carried out with rational ample classes alone, which span `V`.

Suppose `D` is a rational polyhedral strong domain on `p(C)` for the induced faithful action. Each rational generator has a rational effective lift. Indeed, start with an effective real representation and solve the corresponding finite rational linear equalities with nonnegative coefficients. Then `p^{-1}(D)` is a rational polyhedral cone in Eff because all of `U` is effective. Its intersection with the closed movable cone is rational polyhedral by the finite chamber theorem of Li–Zhao, Theorem 2.7 and the argument of Lemma 5.2: in an effective rational cone all classes meeting the closed movable cone have a small weak model on which they are nef (Lemma 2.3), and effective good models in the relative dimension give semiampleness (Theorem 2.4). The cells meeting this locus are entirely contained in it after closure. Equivalently use Li II, Lemma 2.5 with the trivial subgroup and justify equality of actual movable and effective closed movable by those same statements. This proves the assertion even if the rational polyhedral input contains a line. For any concern about applying the subdivision to a cone with lineality, subdivide it into finitely many ordinary pointed cones using rational signs on a basis of its linear part.

The resulting cone `P'=p^{-1}(D)∩C` is a weak domain by surjectivity and openness of the linear projection. Full dimension follows from covering and countability of the lattice group. A full-dimensional `P'` has `p(Int P')⊂Int D`; hence the stabilizer is just the finite kernel already proved. For a finite group preserving `P'`, intersect it with `l(x)>=l(gx)` for all kernel elements, using a rational linear form avoiding their nonidentity fixed subspaces. These cells cover by maximizing on each finite orbit, have full dimension by covering, and ties of distinct functionals occur only on proper hyperplanes. This yields the Definition 3.4 separation, including the boundaries of translates, and trivial setwise stabilizer.

At the generic level, the horizontal closure of a movable effective rational divisor on `Y` is movable over `S`. Close finitely many effective generic linear-system representatives with no common horizontal divisorial component. Making their difference from the distinguished horizontal closure linearly equivalent over the base may require adding vertical effective components; those cannot be fixed components of the distinguished representative. This is Li II, Theorem 1.3 proof, step 1. Classes with the same generic linear class differ by `U`. Every generic line bundle extends up to a multiple by taking a Weil-divisor closure and using Q-factoriality of `X`. It follows that

`Pic(Y)_Q -> V'_Q`

is onto, with its kernel inside the generically numerically trivial classes. Base change to the geometric generic point is injective on generic numerical classes, Li II, Lemma 3.7. For a rational generic good effective class we may use an actual effective member after taking a positive multiple. All real-cone extensions below use finite sums of these horizontal lifts, not a replacement by a closed rational hull.

## Relative dimension zero and generic irregularity zero

In dimension zero the generic field equals `K`, since a connected-fibre dominant generically finite morphism between normal varieties in characteristic zero is birational. Its relative birational group is trivial, and `U=V`. Apply the finite effective chamber argument to `V` itself. A small birational morphism has `C=V` directly.

When `Pic^0(Y_bareta)=0`, the surjection onto `V'_Q` kills every generic numerically trivial bundle, since its geometric class is torsion and a line bundle trivial after field extension is trivial on a geometrically integral proper variety by flat base-change for sections. Thus `V'=N^1(Y)_R`. In generic dimension at most two movable implies nef, and the effective nef classes arising from rational generic semiample divisors lift as above. An effective relative class in the closed movable cone restricts to an effective nef class (apply genuine effective representatives and take the closure for nefness). A strong generic cone theorem for the image of the *extending pair group* then yields the target by the relative lifting lemma. In the K3 case an independent shortcut applies. Li–Zhao Thm 1.4 supplies the weak effective domain, via their quotient construction. The kernel of the action modulo maximal closed-movable lineality fixes the restriction of a relatively ample class since lineality restricts to zero on the projective generic fibre. The automorphism group of a polarized geometric K3 surface is finite, and a birational map over the base is determined on the generic fibre. This upgrades that particular weak domain by the finite refinement. Geometric fibre group versus extending group and generic cone theorem for all boundary pairs in this case are audit items, not concealed assumptions.

## Generic irregularity one

For a genus-one curve the generic numerical class is degree. On a generic irregular surface with `q=1`, use the smoothness of a terminal surface and Xu's homogeneous Albanese structure (straightforward base-field extension in characteristic zero, quoted as Li II Theorem 4.1). Over the geometric generic field,

`Ybar=(B×F)/J`,

with `B=Aut^0(Ybar,Deltabar)` an elliptic curve, `J` a finite group acting by translations on `B`, and `F` a smooth log-CY curve. Thus `F` is rational or elliptic. The product cover is finite étale because the action on `B` is free. In the rational case the Néron–Severi group over Q has just the two factor classes. In the elliptic case its mixed piece is `Hom(B,F^dual)_Q`; this has no invariants since `H^1(F)^J=0` by `q(Ybar)=1`, and some element acts on the origin-preserving elliptic part by a nontrivial unit. Translation acts trivially on numerical classes. Pullback and normalized pushforward identify numerical classes downstairs with the invariants. The nef cone is the rational quadrant in these two classes. Its rational rays have semiample representatives after descent: sum/norm conjugates of basepoint-free bundles from the two factors, obtaining the indicated rational rays. The `B`-orbit degree `d(L)` (using the orbit map, with any isogeny multiplicity included consistently) distinguishes the rays, so each is Galois stable. Averaging Galois conjugates and clearing finite multiples produces generic rational representatives. The same distinction makes the action of generic automorphisms on this rank-two numerical group finite and in fact trivial: it preserves the two primitive rays separately. The ray of degree zero has a distinguished effective lift in `V'`, up to scaling. Indeed pull an effective generic divisor on this ray back to `B×F`. Its restriction to a generic `B×{z}` has degree zero and is effective and consequently trivial. The restriction to a `B` orbit is an isogeny on the groups `Pic^0(Ybar)->Pic^0(B)` by Xu's Albanese description. Thus two effective bundles on this ray with the same rational NS class differ at most by torsion in `Pic^0`. Equivalently that semiample ray contracts the `B` orbits and its target quotient of `F` has genus zero.

For any generic divisor, the homomorphism `t↦t^*L−L` on `B` composed with the isogeny `i^*:Pic^0(Ybar)->Pic^0(B)` from the orbit map satisfies

`i^*(t^*L−L)=d(L) lambda_B(t)`,

where the sign depends on the convention for translating and has no effect on the group. Therefore all translations on the visible Picard difference space `M=ker[V'->N^1(Y)]` are the scalar `d(L)` times the same translation group, including after descent to `K`: a reference ample class supplies an isogeny `B->Pic^0(Y)`, so its action on `K` points is onto up to a bounded multiple. The class-space lattice proves discreteness of that image after fixing a positive integral degree; the images span `M`, giving a full translation lattice. This assertion uses availability of generic translations on the total space, discussed below. All remaining automorphisms act on `Pic^0` with finite linear image because this group is elliptic. With rational horizontal lifts chosen on the rays, a group element's possible shift at degree zero vanishes by uniqueness there. Hence each shift depends only on the scalar `d`. At `d=1` the parameter space `M` supports a fixed rational crystallographic affine group, independent of the coefficient of the other ray. On every class with `d>0`, the full visible `M` occurs in `p(C)`: on the second ray the translates of an effective horizontal basepoint-free lift span a full lattice and their convex hull gives its whole affine real space; adding the first ray gives the general case. At `d=0` only its unique effective lift occurs. A Voronoi cell about a general rational point for an invariant rational Euclidean norm is a compact rational fundamental polytope. Coning it along the positive-degree ray and adding the zero-degree ray gives the exact projected strong domain. For a genus-one generic curve remove the second ray of degree zero; the projected cone consists of all positive degrees and just the origin at degree zero. For genus zero with a klt log-CY boundary of degree two, at least three geometric marked points give finite geometric automorphism group and the previous irregularity-zero argument applies.

## Generic irregularity two, the abelian torsor and the visible module

Xu's theorem makes a smooth generic surface of irregularity two a torsor `T` under its Albanese abelian surface `A/K`. Its horizontal boundary is zero because `K_T` is trivial and an effective divisor numerically zero on a projective surface vanishes. Let `B=End_K(A)_Q`, fix a polarization `lambda` defined over `K`, and put `b^dagger=lambda^{-1}b^dual lambda`. The relevant generic Néron–Severi space consists of the Rosati symmetric endomorphisms of `B`. This follows over an algebraic closure from the abelian NS/polarization identification, then by Galois invariants, finite norms and Brauer torsion; a torsor has the same rational identification. Geometric genuine nef classes are positive semidefinite in the real Rosati algebra. Rational nef classes admit symmetric semiample multiples, descendable up to a finite norm. The case `M=0` has no translation variables.

Write `M_Q` for the visible image of the generically numerically trivial bundles in `V'_Q`, and identify their rational points with a quotient of `A(K)_Q` via `lambda`. Crucial point: its kernel is a `B`-submodule, even if the generic unit group acting on `T` alone would not generate `B`. For an isogeny-type generic morphism `v:T->T` take the graph resolution `p_1:Z->X` birational on the source, `q_1:Z->X` its morphism to the target, both over `S`. If a divisor `L` is numerically trivial over `S`, so is `q_1^*L`; and `p_1^*(p_{1*}q_1^*L)−q_1^*L` is exceptional and numerically trivial for `p_1`, hence vanishes by both signs of the negativity lemma. Here `X` is Q-factorial. The pushforward is numerically trivial over `S` by the projection formula on lifting curves. Vertical divisors are mapped to vertical divisors by `p_{1*}q_1^*`. Thus the operator induces the stated `v^*` on `V'`, regardless of any issues of rational-map composition on the full space. The torsor class `delta` has finite period `m` (restriction to a finite splitting field and corestriction). For `u∈End_K(A)`, `id+n m u` preserves `delta` and hence comes from a torsor morphism, taking a suitable `n` to ensure it is an isogeny. On `Pic^0` its pullback is `id+n m u^dual`, so subtracting the identity gives stability under `u^dual`, or `u^dagger` after identifying by `lambda`. This handles all of `B`.

The finite-dimensional rational module `M_Q` admits a rational strictly positive definite bilinear form `h` with `h(bx,y)=h(x,b^dagger y)`. Obtain a quotient of a finite direct sum of regular `B`-modules, on which the trace form `Tr(b^dagger a)` is positive definite by Rosati positivity, and take the rational orthogonal complement of the kernel. The kernel is a module and its orthogonal complement is a module. Positive semidefinite Rosati-symmetric elements act by positive semidefinite endomorphisms on this space (use the positive square root in the real endomorphism algebra, or the real spectral theorem). Positive definite, invertible classes act positively definitely.

Choose a geometric torsor origin `t0` with `sigma t0−t0∈A[m]` for all Galois elements, e.g. the fibre over zero of a torsor morphism to `A` with linear part `[m]`. Translation by a torsion point acts trivially on `Pic(Tbar)_Q`, by the theorem of the square. The symmetric/antisymmetric splitting under inversion about `t0` therefore descends rationally:

`V'_Q = NS(T)_Q ⊕ M_Q.`

For finite Galois descent with Q coefficients, every required class is defined over a finite extension and Galois norm provides a rational preimage. A generic automorphism is `z↦u z+a` relative to this origin, with `u∈End_K(A)^×`, and `m a∈A(K)` since its Galois differences are torsion. For pullback, writing a class as `(D,w)`, one has

`D'=u^dagger D u,     w'=u^dagger w + D' u^{-1}a.`

Taking inverses converts this to the pushforward convention with the same set of group elements. Among automorphisms inducing the identity on the full NS space, `u^dagger u=1`, since the polarization represents `D=I`. Such units form a finite group, either by compactness plus the integral endomorphism lattice, or finiteness of origin-preserving polarized automorphisms. Each commutes with every admissible `D`. On points of form `w=Dy`, their action is obtained by applying `D` to one fixed affine isometry group `H` of `(M,h)`: `y↦u^dagger y+u^{-1}a`. Its linear parts are finite. Its pure translations include the full-rank lattice induced by `A(K)` via `lambda`: the images span because `lambda` is an isogeny, and evaluation of integral group actions at a fixed rational polarization in the quotient lattice bounds denominators. All translations in `H` lie in a common lattice after clearing the denominator of this one polarization; thus `H` has a full lattice of translations of finite index and is crystallographic. The action on `V'` of the kernel of the NS action is faithfully represented by these affine transformations, as evaluation at `D=I` and the transformation formula show.

The image of the full torsor group in the linear unit group contains the congruence subgroup acting trivially on `A[m]` (it preserves the torsor cocycle). Thus it has finite index in the generic integral unit group. Provided the generic group extends or at least contains this congruence and the full translations, choose a rational strong chamber `R` for its faithful action on the generic effective nef cone. The reduction theorem for arithmetic groups of self-dual homogeneous cones, as in Prendergast-Smith arXiv:1008.4509 and Looijenga, gives this once applied to `End_K(A)` and its unit group. The paper states the abelian theorem for an algebraically closed field. In using this argument over `K`, verify the rational homogeneous cone and the lattice arithmetic for the actual endomorphism order, or use a separate generic abelian theorem over arbitrary fields. A finite-index subgroup inherits a finite rational covering (finitely many translates of the original covering); on the pointed generic cone that covering upgrades to a single strong rational chamber by Li–Zhao Lemma 3.5. It is insufficient merely to pass to a subgroup in the final relative conclusion.

## Exactly which abelian projected classes are effective

For `D∈R` the predicted exact condition is `w∈im(D:M_R->M_R)`. In the forward direction take a genuine effective divisor representative which is a finite positive linear combination of prime divisors. Ignore vertical components, then each generic prime gives an effective line bundle on the abelian torsor and hence a nef numerical class `D_i`. For an effective abelian line bundle, its antisymmetric/Picard-zero offset belongs to the image of its polarization homomorphism over the algebraic closure. One direct argument uses the quotient by the connected kernel of `phi_{L_i}`: a holomorphically effective degree-zero line on a generic translated fibre of this quotient must be trivial; using a symmetric representative up to a multiple and the connected kernel, its `Pic^0` character annihilates that kernel, hence lies in the image in the dual abelian variety. Taking finite Galois norms descends the preimage after an integer multiple. Alternatively use the semiample morphism of the symmetric class and Li II Theorem 4.9 and formula (5.0.8). Thus `w_i∈im D_i` in the visible module. For positive semidefinite matrices, `im(sum alpha_i D_i)=sum im D_i` when all `alpha_i>0`, by their common-kernel description. This yields the requisite inclusion for real effective classes without any irrational extrapolation from rational coverage.

Conversely write a point in the rational polyhedral cone `R` in terms of its rational rays, `D=sum alpha_i D_i`. On each rational nef ray choose a symmetric representative with an effective basepoint-free multiple (quotient abelian variety by its null connected subgroup, or the standard semiampleness theorem for symmetric nef line bundles, then finite Galois norm). Its horizontal closure is movable. Translates of this effective generic class yield `(D_i,D_i a)` with `a` running over a full lattice on `M`. Their real convex hull is the entire `(D_i,im D_i)`: a linear image of the convex hull of a full lattice is the full real image. Combining with positive `alpha_i` and the same sum-of-images identity covers every `(D,w)` in the predicted condition by actual finite convex combinations of movable classes. This works on every face of `R`, including the zero face, and does not add any excluded points in closed-movable lineality.

## Uniform polyhedral Voronoi chamber, including singular forms

Let `R=cone(D_1,...,D_k)` with rational generators as above, using the action of each on the visible module `M`. Pick a rational point `c∈M_Q` with trivial stabilizer in the crystallographic group `H`. For a rational choice, avoid the finitely many relevant fixed affine subspaces in a compact translational fundamental box; there are finitely many by discreteness of the lattice and finiteness of the linear parts. Write `Sigma=Hc`, a finite union of rational translates of a single full lattice `Lambda`. Define the cone by

`D∈R,     2h(w,s−c) ≤ h(Ds,s)−h(Dc,c)     (s∈Sigma).`

For positive definite `D`, substitute `w=Dy`. The inequalities say that `c` is one of the closest sites to `y` for the squared norm `h(D·,·)`. The affine action of `H` preserves this norm on differences and is transitive on the sites. The corresponding Voronoi cells tile with disjoint interiors.

**Uniform finite-face proof.** Fix a compact fundamental parallelepiped `B0` for `Lambda` in the `y` coordinates. The sites nearest to some point of `B0` for some `D∈relint R` collectively form a bounded set. Otherwise take an unbounded sequence of those sites. For each rational `D_i` its image of `Sigma` is discrete (the images of finitely many rational lattice translates lie in a bounded-denominator lattice). After passing to subsequences for all finitely many indices, for each `i` the image `D_i s_n` is a constant or tends to infinity in norm. At least one tends to infinity because an interior form is positive definite on `M`. Choose a fixed site `s_*` from the subsequence. For every constant index the individual squared distances from `s_n` and `s_*` agree for all `y`; their difference is in `ker D_i`. For every divergent index the distance from `s_n` tends to infinity uniformly on `B0`, by positive semidefiniteness in a finite-dimensional space. Thus the fixed site has strictly smaller distance for any form with a strictly positive combination of all the `D_i`, contradicting nearestness. Every relative-interior form has such an expression. This is a compactness argument, with no irrational-kernel approximation.

In particular the Voronoi cell of `c` for any such form has bounded `y` coordinates, with a bound independent of the form: translate `y` back into `B0`, when the correspondingly translated site of `c` is one of the uniform finite list. Every site sharing a point of this cell has a uniform bound too, by the same translation and the bound just obtained. For each fixed positive definite `D`, its ordinary Euclidean Voronoi cell is the intersection of its facet inequalities, all of which correspond to a site sharing a point of that cell (local finiteness follows from discreteness in the fixed positive definite norm and cocompact translations). Thus a single finite subset of `Sigma` defines every positive-definite slice with `D∈relint R`. Each finite inequality is rational and linear in `(D,w)`. The resulting finite closed polyhedral cone agrees with the full intersection on these slices and hence also on every boundary slice: take convex combinations of a candidate point of the finite cone with a fixed point over an interior form and pass to the limit in the closed infinite intersection.

For singular `D∈R`, its kernel is the intersection of the rational kernels of the generators used with positive coefficients. That makes it rational. Testing sites `c±l` with `l∈kerD∩Lambda` forces `w` orthogonal to that kernel; hence `w∈imD`. Conversely for `w=Dy` a closest site exists in the quotient by `kerD`, since a quotient of a lattice by a rational subspace has projected lattice discrete and the induced quadratic form is positive definite. This gives coverage on all effective faces by translates; irrational `D` in a face are included. The condition for a translate to intersect the relative interior of this cone first forces the generic action to fix all NS by the strong separation on `R`, then forces an affine stabilizer of `c` by strict interior Voronoi separation at a positive definite form. The latter is trivial. The same observation excludes boundary/interior intersections: a competing centre equality is a proper linear equality, and the relative-interior cone avoids it. This constructs a single polyhedral strong chamber in the projected effective cone conditional on the stated geometric identifications. The finite kernel and lifting proved above return to the original space.

## Extension across the non-generic locus

Li II uses an equality of generic and relative pseudoautomorphism groups in its Theorem 1.3 proof. Applying that statement blindly to nonzero vertical or unequal boundary coefficients is unsafe. For the abelian case a direct route is available. Choose a nonzero invariant top form on `T/K` and wedge with a rational top form from the base to get a rational top form `Omega` on `X`. Its canonical divisor is vertical. All of `Delta` is vertical in this case. Hence `K_X(Omega)+Delta = f^*D_S` as actual R-divisors for a real Cartier `D_S`. To see the equality, write the given relative R-linear equivalence with real principal divisors. Their total has zero horizontal divisor. The kernel of the map `K(X)^*->Div(Y)` is `K^*` for the geometrically integral proper generic fibre, and tensoring abelian groups with R is exact. Thus their total is the pullback of an R-principal base divisor. If a convention uses local relative linear equivalence the same reasoning on base charts suffices for discrepancy.

Every `K`-automorphism of `T` scales `Omega` by `a∈K^*`. The scalar is actually constant. Spread the generic automorphism to an automorphism over a connected, smooth, dense base open in the smooth proper family. It acts on the integral cohomological local system in top holomorphic degree `R^d f_* Z` with a locally constant characteristic polynomial; `a(s)` is an eigenvalue of this matrix on the holomorphic top-form line on that fibre. Thus `a` ranges over the finite root set of one polynomial and is constant. On any common graph resolution the pulled-back differential differs by only a nonzero scalar and both pullbacks of the displayed base divisor agree. Consequently the divisorial log-discrepancy function is invariant. The original terminal-pair condition makes its prime valuations on `X` precisely those with log discrepancy at most one, because the boundary is effective and every exceptional discrepancy is greater than one. The generic automorphism therefore gives a pseudoautomorphism over the whole base preserving the boundary coefficients. For the boundary-free terminal case this also follows directly from the familiar minimal-model criterion `Bir(X/S)=PsAut(X/S)`.

For irregularity one with horizontal boundary, only the extension of the connected translations is needed for the crystallographic slicing, since the action of the rest on the generic NS and Picard zero origin-preserving part is finite, as checked above. A candidate direct argument is to decompose the original effective real boundary, on its finite fixed support, into a finite convex combination of rational log-CY boundaries `Delta_j` with the same support and with `K_X+Delta_j~_Q0/S`. The relative numerical constraints are rational and the klt locus is open near the given terminal pair; standard numerical-trivial log abundance gives the relative Q-linear equivalences. On each generic pair choose a rational plurilog form with logarithmic divisor exactly the negative horizontal boundary times its index. The connected abelian translation group preserves each support component, and acts trivially on its one-dimensional space because an abelian variety has no nontrivial character to `G_m`. Wedging with a base top form as above (and taking the appropriate tensor power), changing canonical divisor by the resulting principal rational factor, gives a vertical adjoint divisor. The previous actual-pullback argument then gives invariance of the discrepancy function for every rational `Delta_j`; linearity gives it for the original real `Delta`. It is the terminal property of the original pair which now extends the translations. In irregularity zero the analogous generalization for the entire discrete generic group needs a finite-index invariance of generic log-pluricanonical characters (a finite B-representation theorem, if applicable), or an independent finite-index extension theorem; do not infer this solely from connectedness. Direct ordinary volume forms suffice when the generic boundary is zero and a multiple of the canonical class is trivial, using a canonical cover or a finiteness theorem if the top form itself is not generically a single-valued ordinary form.
