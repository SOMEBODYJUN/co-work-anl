## Skeptical checks and precise wording

- A fresh review found no surviving fatal objection in the sparse first-boundary proof, `HC-2` full-cycle argument, shared G1–G3 and local-chord interfaces, or new bad-cycle quantifiers. `HC-2` remains **internal candidate** in the ledger until author-level paper sign-off; absence of an identified defect is not a status promotion.
- **Continuation choice is essential.** Let one weight-1 client cover all four places, with `U1={A,B}`, `U2={C,D}`. Each pair has exactly one common client, and either pure choice is an exact NE. Choose payoffs `AC=(1,0), AD=(0,1), BC=(0,1), BD=(1,0)`. Every pair then has a zero-payoff facility that can deviate to earn 1. Thus this complete but inconsistent pure selection has no finite-factor stable location profile. The sparse theorem is for **any pre-fixed consistent reach/priority rule**, not for every arbitrary equilibrium selection.
- IJCAI 2024 Theorem 5 gives the prior \(\varphi\) lower bound and calls tightness open. Its full-version Theorem 6 SAT reduction uses **\(m+2\) facilities with heterogeneous restricted feasible sets**, so it does not give the two-facility shared-catalog hardness counterpart or hardness at \(\varphi\). Its Observation 4 states a general \(k\) upper bound, while its displayed co-location argument is directly valid for a common feasible place; [Vos 2023 Theorem 17](https://essay.utwente.nl/96484/1/Vos_MA_EEMCS.pdf) says differing catalogs invalidate that argument. Attribute the numerical claim, then explain precisely what the constructive heterogeneous proof adds. See [IJCAI 2024](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2024/0315.pdf) and its [full version](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.03114).
- Whether `HC-2-LOW` contradicts the **intended domain** of the earlier broad weighted-\(\varphi\) conjecture is a model-mapping and priority question, not a settled headline. The [Krogmann 2025 dissertation](https://doi.org/10.25932/publishup-69272) remains an identified but unread full-text source in this audit. Obtain it and make a theorem-by-theorem priority table before an originality claim.
- The long-cycle theorem excludes a uniformly bounded nonempty **exact response-closed core** below \(\rho\). It does not establish kernelization hardness or rule out a smaller selected-deviation certificate; there is no bad-cycle obstruction at \(r=\rho\).
