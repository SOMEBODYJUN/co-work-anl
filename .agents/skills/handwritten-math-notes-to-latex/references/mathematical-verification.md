# Mathematical Verification

## Verification target

Check mathematical equivalence, not wording. Focus on points most likely to fail in handwritten lecture notes:

- all hypotheses and quantifiers;
- dimensions, ranks, codimensions, and index shifts;
- domains, codomains, and variance of induced maps;
- signs in brackets, orientations, Stokes formulas, chain homotopies, and boundary operators;
- compactness, properness, closedness, and boundary assumptions;
- regular and critical points or values, transversality, and Sard-type hypotheses;
- coefficient rings, orientation data, and moduli-space dimensions;
- whether a local proof actually establishes a claimed global theorem.

## Correction classes

1. **Type I — typographical:** repair in the clean version and record when mathematically relevant.
2. **Type II — missing standard hypothesis:** restore only after verification and explain why it is needed.
3. **Type III — substantive discrepancy:** do not silently rewrite; preserve a clear editorial record and escalate unresolved choices.
4. **Type IV — alternative or concealed convention:** determine the convention and apply it consistently rather than calling it an error.

## External and online references

Use authoritative sources when the manuscript and internal context are insufficient or a claim needs checking. Browse rather than relying on memory when facts are specialized, a citation or theorem is named, or there is a meaningful chance the recalled formulation is wrong. Read [online-verification.md](online-verification.md) before internet research. Record the exact source actually consulted and the specific point it supports. Never list a proposed, remembered, or search-snippet-only reference as consulted. Paraphrase external material and preserve the source manuscript's strategy whenever possible.

## Verification entry schema

For each nontrivial issue record:

```text
Location / chapter:
Source PDF page:
Visible manuscript claim:
Issue:
Classification:
Integrated or suggested correction:
Reason:
Reference actually consulted:
URL / bibliographic locator and theorem or section:
Confidence:
Trace-back to earlier chapters:
```

After integrating a correction, search the entire project for repeated statements, symbols, labels, and dependent formulas. A local correction is incomplete if earlier or later chapters retain the incompatible convention.
