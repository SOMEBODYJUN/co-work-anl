# Online Verification of Mathematical Content

## When to browse

Use internet research when at least one of these applies:

- a symbol, word, coefficient, sign, index, or formula looks like a typo;
- a theorem statement may omit hypotheses or use a nonstandard convention;
- the manuscript cites a paper, book, lecture note, author, or named result;
- an argument depends on a specialized or unfamiliar fact;
- two parts of the manuscript contradict each other and local context does not resolve them;
- a correction would materially change the mathematics.

Do not browse merely to rewrite correct material in a more textbook-like style.

## Search strategy

1. Transcribe the uncertain fragment and list the plausible readings before searching.
2. Search the exact theorem name, distinctive formula, or phrase together with the mathematical subject and suspected hypothesis.
3. Prefer sources in this order when available:
   - the cited original paper or an author-hosted version;
   - an official journal, publisher, university, or author webpage;
   - a standard reference text or authoritative lecture notes;
   - peer-reviewed papers that explicitly state or apply the result.
4. Use blogs, forums, snippets, and informal notes only as leads. Do not base a substantive correction on them alone.
5. Open and read the relevant theorem, definition, proof, or surrounding section. A search-result snippet is not verification.
6. For a substantive change, seek a second independent authoritative source when practical, especially if conventions vary. One direct authoritative source may suffice for a clear typographical error or an exact cited theorem.

## Compare rather than replace

Write down:

- what the manuscript visibly says;
- what each source states, including hypotheses and convention;
- whether the difference is a typo, omitted hypothesis, substantive discrepancy, or alternative convention;
- whether the proposed repair preserves the manuscript's proof strategy and later notation.

If sources use different conventions, translate them into the manuscript's convention before comparing formulas. Do not silently import a stronger theorem or a different proof.

## Evidence record

For every online-supported correction, record the source title, author, stable URL or DOI/arXiv identifier, relevant theorem/section/page, access date when useful, and the precise claim verified. Place citations near the supported editorial claim and list only sources actually opened.

Avoid long quotations and copyrighted reproduction. Paraphrase the mathematical content. If reliable sources remain inconclusive or internet access is unavailable, keep the passage unresolved rather than guessing.
