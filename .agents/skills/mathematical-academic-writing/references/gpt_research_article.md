# GPT mathematical-paper rules

Read the original problem and the complete proof before writing. Fix the theorem's quantified class, the actual group, the exact cone and its effective boundary, and every exceptional case. Maintain a notation ledger with each symbol's ambient space and whether it denotes rational or real objects. Use distinct names for distinct kernels and actions, and choose one consistent pullback or pushforward convention.

Follow the governing paper's established English terminology. Write *terminal Calabi--Yau pair*, *pseudo-automorphism*, *relative movable cone*, *effective cone*, *generic fibre*, *geometric generic fibre*, *rational polyhedral cone*, and *fundamental domain* precisely. Never turn a weak fundamental domain into a strong one through wording, or replace the actual effective cone by its closure. Check that each imported theorem has the exact hypotheses used in the manuscript.

Write like a conventional mathematics article. State the full result directly, with `\begin{theorem}` and no ornamental optional title such as `\begin{theorem}[Main theorem]`. Follow it with `\begin{proof}` and the argument in dependency order. Use a small number of necessary lemmas; introduce each notation where it first matters. Keep project management, assistant commentary, review statuses, and research gate tables outside the paper. Use ordinary transitions such as “Let,” “By,” and “It follows that.” Do not add self-congratulatory claims or repetitive parenthetical paraphrases to theorem headings.

Write full proof steps. For each asserted implication, identify the previous lemma or cited result and check the relevant hypotheses, including real coefficients, degenerate faces, lineality, non-closed effective cones, zero-dimensional cases and descent to the actual field-defined group. If the source is only a synopsis, do not manufacture the omitted bridge: obtain the full derivation or mark the theorem unfinished. A previous internal approval is not itself a proof or an originality certificate.

Produce a self-contained LaTeX entry point with accurate bibliography and stable macros. Compile it, resolve citations and references, and inspect the rendered result. Compilation verifies the document, not the mathematics. Report remaining proof or originality obligations outside the paper.
