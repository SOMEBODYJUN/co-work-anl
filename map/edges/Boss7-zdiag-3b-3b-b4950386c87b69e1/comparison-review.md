## What transfers exactly

The shared K, histogram support, R/Z group descriptors, common denominator conventions, and same-index versus different-index row identity transfer exactly. The 56 split rows are a refinement of the same 3,889-row necessary system: summing each diagonal and off-diagonal row gives the corresponding old row, and the capacity column splits as 30K+6K. Therefore the old 3,945-row nonnegativity certificate is a valid prefix of the new 4,005-row system whenever the same 1,905-column point is used. The 43-atom construction also transfers the common histogram and R-side marginal interpretation to the new threshold slabs.

This exact transfer is only a finite necessary-system statement. The threshold construction changes the coordinate realization: relative to the old 1,905-coordinate source point, 62 joint-reply, 193 overlap, and 45 Z-group coordinates change (R groups stay fixed). The old source point has 189 overlap coordinates below their literal Frechet lower bounds. Those values are legal in the enlarged linear necessary relaxation, but they cannot be read as intersections of two events in one customer distribution. The new 43-atom realization therefore does not make the old 1905 point a common labelled-catalogue distribution, and it does not license replacing the new Z coordinates by old overlap coordinates.

## What is separated and why

The old 3,945 point and the new 4,005 point share the same fixed input descriptors and the same W, but they are different points in different coordinate spaces. The old point cannot be mixed with the new 275-Z point: the old source has 1,905 coordinates and 189 explicit Frechet failures, while the new point changes 62 joint, 193 overlap and 45 Z coordinates. The later 7,931-row successor also changes 103 Z masses despite matching descriptors and W; its additional 120 PAIR rows and 1,903 overlap bounds were not reverified at the present 275-vector. Thus neither 3,945 feasibility nor equal W is a free certificate for the successor.

The comparison does not refute the common six-provider half target. It only shows that a finite necessary row system has a W<1/2 rational point and that a particular old overlap interpretation fails. The aggregate variables are not a complete strategy, do not specify all off-path actions, and do not pay every pure-SPE incentive constraint. The strong pair lemma gives a valid tail inequality with an explicit overlap term, but it does not supply early-player payments or turn the aggregate point into an SPE. No exact OPT value or full half obstruction follows.

## Disposition of the two declared overlaps

The comparison resolves both declared 3B overlap questions. For the row-split endpoint, exact row alignment and the 189 Frechet failures show the only free transfer is the finite necessary prefix; no common labelled-catalogue/SPE bridge is established. For the reply-budget program endpoint, the same-index/different-index split and 56-row coefficient identities align with the 3,945 prefix, while the 22-slab/275-Z reconstruction is a different coordinate system and the 7,931 successor remains unverified. There is no remaining concrete comparison that would change these conclusions without introducing a new point or a new complete-game certificate, so both overlaps are closed with these reasons in the receipt.
